## 2. The argument
**In one sentence:** the Wongs can retire on time, fund both children and leave a legacy, not by taking more risk but
through five calls: insure the incomes, put the home and the business to work, fix the cost the budget ignores, give
each person a plan of their own, and add their values and Ryan's crypto with guardrails.

**Why "five calls" (Pete chose it over a family-meeting order and a life-in-chapters order, 20 Oct):** the judges have
read our 15-page written case, so they know the numbers; holding back the answer creates no tension for them. What the
PDF can't show is the judgment behind each decision. So the talk leads with the answer and then defends five calls a
practitioner would question, each with the evidence, the obvious alternative we rejected, and the trade-off: Pete's
own "defend" rule from the written case. The tension is "why would you do that?", which is real for an audience that
knows the ending.

**What the audience must believe:**
1. The family is strong, and its two real risks are decisions, not markets (3).
2. Each call is the right one for this family, better than the obvious alternative (4 to 8).
3. The plan survives the worst and is honest about its limits (9).
4. It can start this month, and it is reviewed as life changes (10).
5. It serves the next generation too (11), and we are planners who could do this across the boundary (12, 13).

**The strongest objection:** "33% for a HK$24.4M family? Your model is too pessimistic." Answered on 3 and 5: it is
the investments alone, with medical premiums the budget leaves out; the home and the business are 61% of the wealth and
pay nothing in retirement. The fix is decisions about what they own, not more risk.

## 4. Rules for every slide (the system)
The look (colour, type, decorative idea) is chosen in §6; these rules hold whatever it is. They exist because v7 had
40 font sizes, about ten navigation items a slide, and a different grid on every slide.

**Anatomy of a content slide (2 to 12):**
- kicker (the section: "Call 2 · Retirement"), then the title, two lines at most;
- **three panels whose headings read as one sentence across the slide** ("More shares barely help…" / "…four
  decisions on what they already own do…" / "…and each has a price"); **each panel is one step of the argument and
  adds a new fact**: no panel restates another panel or an earlier slide (Pete, 20 Oct: v8's first Call 2 spent a
  third of the slide repeating 33%);
- **every claim gets a shape, not a paragraph.** The defence of a call is **measured** where it can be: run the
  obvious alternative in the model and draw it beside the call (Call 2: more shares, `alone_eq*`); where it can't, a
  ledger with one figure a row. Each panel opens with its hero number or object and ends on a grey evidence note,
  the notes on one baseline (e-Sentry's pattern);
- one **conclusion bar**: the slide's point in one sentence, worded to lead into the next slide;
- footer: **one** navigation device (the deck's sections), the source, the page number. Nothing else.
- Not used (v7 lessons): a second tracker, question pills, "slide 5's…" references, tags inside sentences.

**Grid:** 1280 × 720; margins 48 px left and right, 34 px top; 12 columns, 24 px gutters. Panels are 4 + 4 + 4 or
5 + 4 + 3 columns. Every chart starts on a panel's left edge; panel bottoms line up.

**Type (look B, 20 Oct):** Archivo Narrow only for the title, panel headings, lane names and big numbers; **Inter** for
everything else, bold labels included (Pete: Inter for the small text; a condensed bold label looked smaller than
Inter's small text). **Scale, no other sizes:** title 32 · panel heading 20 · bold label and body 16 (Inter) · small 14
(Inter) · tag and source 12 · numbers 40 and 72. Nothing meant to be read is under 14 px.

**Density:** hybrid. 120 to 180 words on a call slide (titles and labels included), at most 150 on the summary; the
script carries the reasoning in 85 to 135 spoken words a slide.

**Charts:** one style. Grey series, the accent only on the value the title claims, labels on the data (no legends
where a label fits), axis text at 14 px. The ten futures, the staircase and the premium line come back from v7 (they
worked); everything else is redrawn in the system.

**Numbers:** tagged as a quiet "(measured)", "(case)" or "(sourced)" after the claim. MEASURED = the team model
(`numbers.json`); CASE = the case; SOURCED = a named public source.

**Builds:** each slide reveals in speaking order (`data-b`); present from the builds PDF; the PowerPoint gets native
"appear" animations.
