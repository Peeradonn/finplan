## The route: reverse mortgage vs downsizing (the trade-off the brief wants)

HKMC counts only 50% of the value above HK$8M, so a discount on the old flat hurts downsizing about five times more.

| Old flat in 2037 is… (newer flats hold) | Reverse mortgage: odds | Downsizing: odds | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Model path | 96.8% | 96.2% | `Final/model_runs.md` M6 (recommended base) |
| 10% lower | 96.3% (−0.5) | 93.6% (−3.2) | same |
| 20% lower | 95.8% (−1.0) | 89.8% (−6.9) | same |

**Inheritance, median (today's money):** downsizing leaves more at every discount (e.g. 10% lower: 15.32M vs 12.29M). So **reverse mortgage protects the odds; downsizing leaves the children more. The family chooses.** That is the required trade-off.

## Deadline and trigger (simple rules)

- **Rule:** release the home by the year the building turns 50 (completion year + 49). 1996 building → **2045**. A 1986 building → 2035.
    - Why: above 50, HKMC reverse mortgage is "case-by-case"; a buyer's HKMC-insured loan needs building age + term ≤ 75 years, so a 2045 buyer gets 26 years instead of 30 (about 9% higher repayments). (SOURCED rules, ESTIMATED arithmetic: `area4_property_scenario.md` §3.4)
- **Yearly trigger:** compare the flat's price per sq ft with a ~10-year-old flat in the target district. If the gap widens 10% or more vs today, decide the route that year. (our design, ILLUSTRATIVE)
- **Buying the smaller flat:** prefer a newer building for age and exit, not for a green label. Do not pay the 4.4% label premium (about HK$0.3M on HK$7M) for about HK$2K a year of energy saving.
