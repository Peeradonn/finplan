PETE: But will it last? Our written case measured investments alone: 33%. The fairer test is what a sensible family would do: sell the business when Carmen retires, keep the flat, and sell it only if they must. Across 10,000 simulated markets, in 51% they must. In a typical one, Carmen, at 83 and on her own, sells the home where they raised Ryan and Chloe. By 86 the money has run out again, and the wealth meant for their children pays the bills: of HK$24.4 million, just HK$2.3M is left to them. [Notes: Their own way: the business sold when Carmen retires; the flat kept and swapped for a HK$7M one only when the money runs out; the full budget after a death; the Flexi plan for life; equities 6% a year, inflation 2.5%, as in the written case. "Usually on her own": Adrian dies at 84 on the case's life expectancy, when Carmen is 79; she is alone in 83% of the forced sales. Even after the sale, the money lasts to 89 in only 62%. Why not a reverse mortgage at 83? It would let Carmen stay, but HKMC's fixed payout doesn't keep up with her bills and premiums: in Fahtai's test (HKMC's March 2026 table) the money lasts to 89 less often than selling, and the loan eats into what Ryan and Chloe inherit.]
