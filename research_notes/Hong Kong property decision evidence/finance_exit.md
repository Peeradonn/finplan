## Can green borrowing improve this family's finances?

### Takeaway

**Do not reject all refinancing simply because green rebates are small. Compare the best eligible ordinary refinance with the best eligible green refinance, after every fee.** The evidence supports a modest or zero *incremental label benefit* for the Wongs, not the universal claim that no green mortgage ever offers a better rate. Eligibility is product-specific; a flat-level certificate works for a particular HKMC promotion, not automatically for individual banks.

### Inferences

- A 1996 building is neither automatically eligible nor automatically ineligible for bank green mortgage. Obtain its actual directory entry and written bank approval. The case does not establish absence of certification. A new AC or green interior alone does not satisfy a bank's whole-building route.
- Recommended refusal wording: **“We would not refinance for this rebate alone. First verify eligibility, then require a positive all-in saving.”** The old brief's blanket “no bank offers a lower rate” exceeds the dated evidence. Its “HK$0–3,000 on a HK$1.8m mortgage” also blends products and programme eligibility: an RMP reward concerns specified property value, not the ordinary outstanding loan balance.
- Refinance calculation: for equal initial principal `P`, monthly rate `r`, same remaining months `n`, `payment=P*r/[1-(1+r)^(-n)]`. Incremental NPV at discount `d` is `rebates_new - rebates_baseline - legal - valuation - redemption - clawbacks - certification_increment + sum[(payment_old-payment_new)/(1+d)^m] + (balance_old_T-balance_new_T)/(1+d)^T`. Add offset/deposit-linked benefits net of foregone deposit earnings and account requirements. A longer tenor is not a saving without the residual debt term.
- **Worked example, nominal HKD**: illustrative HK$1.8m amortising over 132 months, rate falls 3.50% to 3.40%, unchanged tenor, HK$10,000 all-in switching costs, incremental HK$1,000 reward at time zero, annual nominal discount 3% divided monthly. Payments fall from HK$16,449.02 to HK$16,364.03; saving HK$84.99/month; PV HK$9,545.36; NPV **+HK$545.36**. Without rebate: **−HK$454.64**. This is a hypothetical rate discount, not a bank quote. Borrower qualification and insurance costs could overturn it. An isolated HK$1,000 rebate cannot justify HK$10,000 switching costs if rates/other benefits are identical.

## How do age and reverse mortgage terms change the retirement choice?

### Takeaway

**A 50-year-old building creates underwriting uncertainty, not a forced sale deadline.** For joint borrowing in 2037, Carmen's age controls the payout; use the two-person row, not Adrian's age or a single-person illustration. A reverse mortgage provides contractual income and residence protections but retains maintenance obligations, concentrated housing exposure and compounding debt.

### Cited Findings

- HKMC eligibility normally requires age at least 55 and Hong Kong identity card; residential security; permitted sole/joint ownership; properties above 50 years considered case by case. The Government confirms approved older-property precedents and possible inspection reports. Thus a 1996 completion reaches age 50 in **2046**, subject to exact completion/application dates; it is not a legal requirement to exit in 2045. [HKMC programme](https://www.hkmc.com.hk/eng/our_business/reverse_mortgage_programme.html), undated live page, accessed 8 Nov 2026; [Government LegCo reply, 14 May 2025](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202505/14/P2025051300589.htm), accessed 8 Nov 2026.

Full early repayment has no penalty; partial repayment is not accepted. On termination, owners/representatives may redeem, otherwise sale proceeds repay debt; surplus returns and insured shortfall is borne by HKMCI. It is not free money. Compound interest applies; insurance is **1.96% of specified value**, in seven **0.28%** instalments at months **37,49,61,73,85,97,109**, plus monthly premium at **1.25% p.a. of outstanding debt**. ## How should keep, retrofit, downsize and reverse mortgage be compared fairly?

### Takeaway

Use the **same start date, horizon, housing service, debt, spending and terminal valuation assumptions**, then show both liquid cash and remaining housing equity. A comparison of annual RMP income with downsizing's initial cash release alone is incomplete. Neither higher initial cash nor higher gross legacy establishes suitability.

### Cited Findings

- IRD's current AVD table effective **26 Feb2026** charges3% for values fromHK$6,642,861 toHK$9m; a hypotheticalHK$8m replacement therefore incursHK$240,000. This is a current-law illustration, not a2037 tax forecast. [IRD AVD FAQ](https://www.ird.gov.hk/eng/faq/avd.htm), current page publication date unstated, accessed8 Nov2026. Old historical Q&As on the same page do not override its dated current rate table.
- Case allows retain/refinance/monetise/downsize recommendations and states HK$780,000 annual retirement target in today's terms, excluding extraordinary medical costs. It does not quantify downsizing prices, costs or the value the family puts on space and location. `Primary Info/Award2026Case20260919.pdf`, pp.5–6,8–9, September2026.

### Inferences

Recommended model handoff (all new output must pass the repository export/check process before slides):

1. Start2037 with **common pre-decision liquid assets `L0`, home appraisal `V0`, outstanding debt `D0`**. Keep the mortgage schedule in every branch or dischargeD0 consistently. Show discharge as use of liquid funds in retain/RMP branches; net it from sale proceeds in downsize. Do not clear it twice.
2. **Keep**: mortgage payments, management, repairs/levies, insurance, cooling and eventual sale costs. **Retrofit**: keep plus incremental equipment/fabric cost, maintenance, measured savings and explicit residual value. The failed-equipment baseline buys a standard replacement; early replacement baseline keeps the working unit until failure.
3. **Downsize**: initial release=`net sale proceeds−D0−replacement purchase−AVD−agency/legal/moving/fit-out/bridging costs`. Then smaller home's costs, accessibility and terminal equity; any green purchase premium is an incremental cost, not guaranteed resale appreciation.
4. **RMP**: existing-debt discharge or financing lump sum, quoted monthly draw, premium/interest schedule, maintenance/insurance, terminal debt and redemption/sale option. Preserve conditional residence benefits and non-recourse protection as separate attributes.
5. Same monthly spending path and portfolio-return paths. AtT report liquid assets, cumulative consumption funded, home equity net of debt/sale costs and shortfall risk, plus time/inconvenience/access needs. For lifetime comparison use mortality scenarios and survivor housing, not just fixed84/89 death dates. Nominal cash flows need nominal discount/inflation; real results require explicit deflation.
