## 2. The single biggest finding: 15 points most teams drop

The official rules page awards Round One marks as:

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Effective use of client's information | 15 |
| Formulation of financial goals | 10 |
| **Personal statement** — *"Discussion of personal career goal and opportunities under the cross-boundary wealth management connect scheme"* | **15** |
| Financial proposal | 25 |
| Risk analysis | 15 |
| Presentation — clarity, organization, conciseness, grammar | 20 |

Round Two repeats it: **Content delivery – Personal statement (15)**, *"Presentation of personal
statement and coverage of the cross boundary wealth management connect scheme."*

Here is the catch. That personal-statement criterion appears identically on the 2022, 2023, 2025 and
2026 rules pages — it is the standing rubric. But:

- The **2025 workshop deck** presented a *different* Round One rubric: client info 15, goals 10,
  **comprehensive financial proposal 40**, risk 15, presentation 20 — **no personal statement at all.**
- The **2025 case study** never mentions a personal statement or Wealth Management Connect either.
  Its own submission checklist asks only for executive summary, analysis, portfolio allocation,
  risk/compliance, appendices.

So a team that works only from the case study and the workshop deck writes nothing about their own
career goals or WMC, and forfeits 15 marks in Round One and 15 more in Round Two — and Round Two
ranking is `0.5 × R1 + 0.5 × R2`.

**Action:** write the personal statement section regardless, and ask the committee to confirm the
live rubric during the workshop rules segment. If they confirm 40/no-statement, we cut a section
we already have. If they confirm the website, we are one of few teams that scored it.

The other rubrics:

- **Round Two (100):** communication skills 45 · personal statement 15 · financial proposal 20 ·
  PPT quality 15 · charisma & team spirit 5. Ranking `0.5 × R1 + 0.5 × R2`.
- **Final Round (100):** knowledge of topic 20 · logical thinking 20 · presentation skills 20 ·
  **innovation & independent thinking 40**. Ranking `0.15 × R1 + 0.15 × R2 + 0.7 × Final`.

The final round is 70% of the final ranking and 40% of *that* is innovation — but that weighting applies
only among the four teams who get there, and four finalists out of a field this size is low single-digit
odds. Do not use it to budget effort. Each round is scored on its own rubric, and because scores carry
forward, maximising each round's raw score is weakly dominant: there is no scenario where a lower Round
One helps us. Work the criteria, not the weighting — see §4.

## 3. Hard constraints — build the template around these now

- **Round One is a written report, not a slide deck.** Slides appear only in Round Two (PPT quality,
  15 marks) and the Final Round. The 20 Round One "presentation" marks are *prose* marks —
  "clarity, organization, conciseness, grammar" — earned across the whole document.
- English. **No more than 15 single-sided, single-spaced pages, 12-point Times New Roman**,
  **including cover pages, tables, charts, diagrams, references and appendices.** There is no
  appendix escape hatch.
- Excel spreadsheets may be submitted as separate electronic files. **Use this.** Push working
  calculations into the workbook and keep results-only tables in the 15 pages.
- Anonymity: team name must not contain the university name or related words, and the university
  must not be named anywhere in the proposal or the final presentation. Judges learn affiliations
  only after final results.
- Submission: email **info@hkrfp.org** (note: enquiries go to info@srfpns.org — the society has
  renamed itself Society of Registered Financial Planners **& Strategists**).

### Suggested page budget, mapped to marks

| Section | Pages | Marks served |
|---|---|---|
| Cover + contents | 1 | — |
| Executive summary | 1 | carries the thesis |
| Client situation, net worth & cash flow analysis | 2 | 15 |
| Financial goals (SMART, prioritised) | 1 | 10 |
| Personal statement + Wealth Management Connect | 1.5 | 15 |
| Recommendations + portfolio allocation & rationale | 4.5 | 25 |
| Risk analysis, compliance, safeguards | 2 | 15 |
| Implementation, monitoring & review; appendices | 2 | — |

The 20 presentation marks are not a section — they are earned document-wide through writing quality.
Write dense prose, not bullet fragments. Fifteen pages of single-spaced 12-point Times New Roman is
roughly 6,000–8,000 words once tables and charts are allowed for; deck-style bullets waste most of
that allowance and read badly against "clarity, organization, grammar."

## 4. Round One scoring playbook

Round One is scored on its own rubric, and maximising that raw score is **weakly dominant**: it decides
whether we are one of twelve teams, and it then carries into both later rankings (`0.5 × R1` in Round
Two, `0.15 × R1` in the final). There is no scenario in which a lower Round One score helps us. So work
the six criteria one at a time, to the top of each. Do not trade between rounds.

**Innovation is not a Round One criterion.** The six criteria below total 100 and none of them rewards
novelty, visual design or flair. Novelty pays here only through "most suitable" and "defend" — and it
costs us when it cannot be defended. Save originality for the Final Round's 40-mark criterion.

### 4.1 Effective use of client's information — 15

> *"Make use of all the information provided in the case to assess the current financial conditions of the client."*

Operative words: **all**, and **current**. This criterion is diagnosis, not recommendation — nothing we
propose belongs in this section.

Half marks looks like a tidy restatement of the case's figures. Full marks looks like statements, ratios,
the narrative facts, derived insight, and declared gaps.

- [ ] Net worth statement — assets, liabilities, net worth
- [ ] Cash flow statement — income, expenses, surplus
- [ ] Diagnostic ratios: savings ratio, liquidity in months of expenses, debt-to-income, debt-to-asset,
      mortgage LTV, investment assets as a share of net worth
- [ ] **Qualitative facts used explicitly** — risk attitudes, stated preferences, the children's plans,
      the character of the business. Most teams harvest the numbers and discard the story.
- [ ] **Derived** facts, not only given ones — e.g. property equity and LTV; the consultancy reframed as
      an illiquid, undiversified, key-person-dependent holding rather than an asset line
- [ ] Missing data named, each with the assumption we adopt
- [ ] Traceability table (§4.7) completed

### 4.2 Formulation of financial goals — 10

> *"Help the client establish realistic financial goals given his/her constraints."*

Operative words: **realistic**, **given constraints**. The case hands us the goals; restating them earns
roughly half. The marks are for testing them.

- [ ] Each goal carries an amount, a date, and a required capital sum at that date
- [ ] Feasibility tested against the balance sheet and the surplus
- [ ] **Goals that fail the test are named, and a revision proposed** — retire a year later, reduce the
      annual figure, convert a living foundation to a bequest. Pushing back on the client *is* the criterion.
- [ ] A prioritisation *principle* stated, not merely an ordering
- [ ] Constraints classified: liquidity, time, regulatory, behavioural (risk aversion is a constraint to
      design around, not a preference to educate away)
- [ ] Conflicts between goals made explicit

### 4.3 Personal statement — 15

> *"Discussion of personal career goal and opportunities under the cross-boundary wealth management connect scheme."*

Two halves, both mandatory. This is the only section that does not depend on the case, so draft it before
the case drops — and the only one no other team can converge on.

- [ ] Its own titled section, findable from the contents page
- [ ] Career goal is specific — a role, not "finance"
- [ ] WMC covered with current parameters, verified against HKMA
- [ ] An independent observation rather than a summary. For example: eligible products are **non-complex
      only**, which makes WMC a distribution channel rather than an advice channel — and that gap is where
      advisory value appears as the scheme widens.
- [ ] A link back to the case, including limitations — WMC's closed loop and non-complex scope cannot hold
      the tokenised or ESG-structured instruments the case invites
- [ ] Covers every team member (confirm singular vs per-member at the workshop — see §8)

Current WMC parameters, to verify before submission: aggregate quota RMB 150bn in each direction;
individual quota RMB 3m, or RMB 1.5m each if split between a bank and a securities firm; eligible products
non-complex only — deposits, funds, bonds and CDs; closed-loop one-to-one paired remittance and investment
accounts. Source: [HKMA](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/key-functions/international-financial-centre/wealth-management-connect/).

### 4.4 Financial proposal — 25

> *"Devise and defend a comprehensive financial proposal that is most suitable for the client to achieve his/her financial goals."*

Operative words: **devise and defend**, **comprehensive**, **most suitable**. Most teams devise and never
defend; the defence marks are in naming the alternatives we rejected and why.

Domain coverage — from the workshop deck's own list. A missing domain is a direct deduction against
"comprehensive":

- [ ] Emergency fund / cash reserve
- [ ] Debt, including mortgage restructuring
- [ ] Risk management and insurance — life, disability, health, critical illness, property and liability, long-term care
- [ ] Investment and asset allocation
- [ ] Tax
- [ ] Retirement
- [ ] Education funding
- [ ] Estate, succession, disposal of the business, philanthropy

Then:

- [ ] Portfolio allocation with percentages, expected return, and rationale against horizon and risk tolerance
- [ ] Every recommendation traced back to a specific client fact from §4.1
- [ ] Alternatives considered and rejected, with reasons
- [ ] **Numerical demonstration that each goal in §4.2 is met** — a proposal that never shows goal
      achievement has not shown suitability
- [ ] HK-specific instruments used where they fit: TVC, VHIS, QDAP, HKMC Life Annuity, reverse mortgage

### 4.5 Risk Analysis — 15

> *"Identify risks that may arise in the financial proposal and suggest safeguards to mitigate these risks."*

Read this carefully: risks arising **in the financial proposal** — risks our own recommendations create.
Not the client's general life risks. "They might fall ill, so buy insurance" is §4.4 material, not this.
Most teams will answer the wrong question here, which makes it a real separator.

Work through at least: sequence-of-returns risk on the drawdown; inflation against the retirement target;
concentration and liquidity risk in the business and the property; custody and counterparty risk on any
tokenised or virtual asset; regulatory and licensing change; currency risk on RMB products; greenwashing
in the ESG sleeve; longevity; key-person risk in the consultancy; interest-rate risk on the mortgage.

Use the register at §4.8. This is also where we earn the right to recommend anything novel: propose the
innovative instrument, then out-risk-analyse everyone on it.

### 4.6 Presentation — 20

> *"Clarity, organization, conciseness, grammar."*

Four named sub-criteria, all mechanical, all winnable, and together a fifth of the gate. Highest-leverage
move: **order the document to mirror the rubric**, with headings matching the criterion names, so a marker
working a six-item checklist never has to hunt.

- [ ] Section order and headings mirror the six criteria
- [ ] Contents page with page numbers
- [ ] A topic sentence opening every paragraph; one idea per paragraph
- [ ] Every sentence carries a fact or a judgement — hedging cut
- [ ] Consistent HK$ and number formatting; consistent tense
- [ ] Consistent table and figure captions, each referenced in the text
- [ ] Proofread by someone who did not write it; read aloud once
- [ ] Page count verified **in the exported PDF** at 12-point Times New Roman, single-spaced, single-sided
- [ ] University name absent from the text, the file name, **and the document metadata** (Word embeds
      author and company in file properties — an anonymity breach we would never see on the page)

### 4.7 Traceability table — template

Evidences the word "all" for §4.1, and doubles as a pre-submission completeness check. Keep it in an
appendix, or push it to the Excel file if pages are tight.

| # | Fact from case | Type | Where assessed | Recommendation it drives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rafael Lee, 52, head of fintech at a digital bank | Given | Report §1 profile | 13-year horizon; human capital strong |
| 2 | Combined income HK$1.8m p.a. | Given | Report §1 cash flow | Savings capacity |
| 3 | Property $8m, mortgage $2.5m | Derived | Report §1 net worth; LTV 31% | No restructuring needed |
| 4 | Rafael conservative, wary of risk | Narrative | Report §1 risk profile | Separate risk buckets; capped VA sleeve |
| 5 | *(one row per datum)* | | | |
| — | Expense breakdown not provided | Gap | Report §1, assumption A3 | Assumed figure stated |

Types: **Given / Derived / Narrative / Gap.**

Rule: every row needs an entry in both right-hand columns. A blank on the right means we have found either
an unused fact or an unjustified recommendation.

### 4.8 Risk register — template

| # | Risk | Recommendation that creates it | Likelihood | Impact | Safeguard | Monitoring trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Sequence-of-returns risk in the first 5 years of drawdown | Growth allocation carried into retirement | Medium | High | 3-year cash-and-bond ladder covering early withdrawals | Funded ratio below 100% at any annual review |
| R2 | Total loss of the digital-asset sleeve | 3% allocation to VA spot ETFs | Low | Low–Medium | Sleeve sized so total loss delays retirement by ~7 months; regulated wrapper; licensed custody | Sleeve exceeds 5% of liquid assets, or licensing change |
| R3 | Greenwashing — holdings fail the stated ESG screen | 20% ESG allocation | Medium | Medium | Stated screening methodology; funds with disclosed criteria; annual verification | Fund changes mandate or disclosure |
| R4 | Illiquidity and key-person risk | Consultancy retained as a going concern | Medium | High | Key-person cover; buy-sell agreement; succession plan; business excluded from retirement liquidity | Valuation change, or reduced owner involvement |
| R5 | *(continue)* | | | | | |

Rule: every recommendation in §4.4 should appear in at least one row. A recommendation that creates no
risk has probably not been thought through.

### 4.9 Before we submit

- [ ] All six criteria have a clearly titled home in the document
- [ ] Traceability table has no blank right-hand cells
- [ ] Every §4.4 recommendation appears in the risk register
- [ ] Every goal in §4.2 has a numerical demonstration that the plan meets it
- [ ] Assumptions collected in one titled list, each justified
- [ ] Excel workbook attached; workings out of the document, results tables in it
- [ ] Page count checked in the exported PDF
- [ ] Anonymity: text, file name, metadata
- [ ] Proofread by a non-author

## 7. Pre-workshop work, in priority order

**A. Build the financial model (case-agnostic).** One Excel workbook, tabs for: net worth statement ·
cash flow statement · retirement funding gap (expense-replacement, inflation-adjusted) · education
funding · insurance needs analysis · asset allocation and rationale · scenario/sensitivity table.
Hard-code inflation 2.5%, life expectancy M83/F88, HKD. When the case drops we type in numbers
instead of building formulas.

**B. Build two instrument reference sheets.** Both are case-independent, and both feed the
"comprehensive" and "most suitable" tests in §4.4.

*Traditional Hong Kong toolkit*, which recurs every year: MPF and Tax-Deductible Voluntary Contributions ·
VHIS · QDAP · HKMC Life Annuity · reverse mortgage · salaries tax progressive vs standard rate and
allowances · no capital gains tax · property stamp duty.

*Next-generation toolkit* — **done**, in [reference/instrument-sheet.md](reference/instrument-sheet.md).
Sixteen instruments with Hong Kong regulatory status, retail access and principal risk, plus the state of
play on stablecoins, tokenised products, VATPs, VA custody, digital green bonds and ESG disclosure as at
14 September 2026. The regulatory-status column is what lets us *defend* a recommendation and populate
§4.8. Re-verify it once the case is published — this area moved a lot during 2026.

Two findings from it worth carrying into our thinking now:

- **Tokenised money market funds are the strongest card we hold.** The SFC's 20 April 2026 circulars
  permit 24/7 secondary trading of tokenised SFC-authorised products, settled in regulated stablecoins or
  tokenised deposits. Genuinely novel, genuinely 2026, *and* inside the authorised perimeter — so it
  survives a suitability challenge in a way a DeFi recommendation does not.
- **Virtual asset custody is still a bill, not a law.** Consultation conclusions landed 24 December 2025
  and a draft bill was targeted for LegCo during 2026, but it does not appear to be enacted. Recommending
  a licensed custodian arrangement as though the regime were live is a factual error a practitioner judge
  would catch instantly.

**C. Draft the personal statement now.** It is the one section that does not depend on the case at
all — our own career goals, plus how Wealth Management Connect creates opportunity. 15 marks in each
of two rounds. Write it this week.

**D. Build the 15-page template.** Times New Roman 12pt, single-spaced, numbered headings, table and
chart styles settled, page budget from §3 pre-allocated. Fighting the page limit in the last 48 hours
is how good analysis gets cut.

**E. Do a full dry run on the 2025 case.** [2025-case-study.pdf](reference/2025-case-study.pdf) is a
real past paper. Writing a complete proposal against it before Sep 19 is the highest-value rehearsal
available: it tests the model, the template, the page budget and the division of labour while the
stakes are zero.

**F. Assign roles by rubric section**, not by topic interest — client analysis, goals, personal
statement, proposal/portfolio, risk, and one editor who owns the 20 presentation marks and the page
count. Pick the presenter early; Round Two is 45 marks of communication and there is an individual
Best Presenter award.

**G. Research the final-round style now.** Past themes were HK financial-policy essays, not family
cases: 2023 Belt and Road and the offshore RMB pool; 2022 defending Hong Kong as a private wealth
management hub post-COVID versus Singapore. Both asked candidates to advise *government* as an
industry executive, with data and cited sources. If we reach the final we get roughly 10 days — but
building a general view on Hong Kong's competitive position as a financial centre is something to
accumulate from now, not cram.
