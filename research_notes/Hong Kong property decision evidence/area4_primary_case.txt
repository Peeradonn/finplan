Case Study
The Wong Family Legacy
Balancing Retirement, Education, ESG Integration, and Digital Asset Exposure
Background
The Wong family resides in Hong Kong and consists of four members:
1. Mr Adrian Wong (54) — husband and father, Regional Operations Director of a logistics technology
company with business exposure across Hong Kong, the Greater Bay Area and Southeast Asia.
2. Mrs Carmen Wong (49) — wife and mother, co-founder and managing director of a sustainable
packaging distribution business in Hong Kong.
3. Ryan Wong (24) — elder son, recently started full-time employment as a data analyst at a virtual insurer
in Hong Kong.
4. Chloe Wong (16) — younger daughter, a secondary school student planning to pursue university studies
in Hong Kong or overseas.
The family currently lives in a self-owned residential flat in Tai Kok Tsui, with an estimated market value of
HK$11,500,000 and an outstanding mortgage balance of HK$1,800,000. The property was purchased 11 years
ago and serves as the family’s principal place of residence.
Over the years, the Wong family has accumulated a substantial pool of savings and investments. Nevertheless,
after experiencing the pandemic, higher interest rates, volatile equity and bond markets, and the emergence of
digital assets and tokenized investment products, they have become increasingly aware of the need for
professional financial planning advice.
Adrian is prudent by nature and places strong emphasis on capital preservation, retirement adequacy and
healthcare protection. Carmen is more forward-looking in investment matters and believes the family should
allocate part of its wealth toward environmental, social and governance (“ESG”) investments, reflecting both
her personal values and her belief in the long-term economic opportunities arising from sustainability trends.
Ryan, being part of a younger and more technology-oriented generation, has independently started investing in
Bitcoin, Ether, virtual asset spot exchange-traded funds and tokenized cash management products
through what he believes are regulated channels. He is optimistic about the long-term role of blockchain,
tokenization and Web3 infrastructure in the financial industry. Adrian remains skeptical of digital assets,
whereas Carmen is open to new opportunities but concerned about volatility, fraud, custody, valuation
uncertainty and regulatory change.
Chloe has expressed interest in pursuing undergraduate studies in business, psychology or design, and may
apply to universities in Hong Kong, the United Kingdom, Canada or Singapore. Her parents would like to
preserve flexibility and ensure that she can attend a suitable university without excessive reliance on debt.

Existing Protection Arrangement
The family’s existing protection position is summarised below:
1. Adrian holds a whole life insurance policy with death benefit of HK$2,000,000, with an estimated cash
value of HK$210,000.
2. Carmen holds a whole life insurance policy with death benefit of HK$1,800,000, with an estimated
cash value of HK$210,000.
3. Adrian and Carmen each have employer-provided group medical coverage. However, they are uncertain
whether the level of medical protection would remain adequate after retirement.
4. Neither Adrian nor Carmen has standalone long-term disability income protection.
5. The family maintains basic home insurance and motor insurance.
6. Ryan has no personal life, critical illness or medical insurance beyond employer coverage.
7. The family has not yet established wills, trust arrangements, powers of attorney, or advance
healthcare directives.

Family Concerns
The Wong family seeks professional advice on the following matters:
1. Whether Adrian and Carmen are on track to retire at their desired ages while maintaining their preferred
standard of living.
2. How best to prepare financially for Chloe’s university education under both local and overseas scenarios.
3. Whether the family’s current exposure to digital assets is appropriate, and if so, how such exposure
should be managed prudently.
4. How to integrate ESG factors into the family’s investment strategy in a meaningful and credible way.
5. Whether their existing insurance and protection arrangements are sufficient in the event of death, critical
illness, disability, hospitalization or business disruption.
6. How to manage intergenerational wealth transfer and financial responsibility between parents and
children.
7. Whether Carmen should gradually restructure, reduce, or monetize part of her business interest over time
as part of retirement and succession planning.
8. Whether the family should retain their current residence in retirement, downsize to a smaller property, or
relocate to another district.
Market Context and Developments
Hong Kong has continued to position itself as an international financial centre for both sustainable finance and
digital assets. In recent years, the market has seen growing investor interest in:
• ESG and sustainable investment products
• green bonds and sustainability-linked investments
• tokenized securities and digital investment infrastructure
• virtual asset spot ETFs and regulated digital asset channels
• retirement-oriented solutions that balance stability, inflation protection and long-term growth
At the same time, investors continue to face uncertainty arising from:
• global geopolitical tension
• inflation and interest rate fluctuations
• longevity risk and medical inflation
• technology disruption
• valuation volatility in both traditional and digital asset markets
• concerns relating to greenwashing and digital asset suitability
Against this backdrop, the Wong family wishes to construct a financial plan that is innovative yet prudent,
forward-looking yet practical, and aligned with both financial goals and family values.
Family Goals
The Wong family has identified the following major objectives:
1. Retirement Planning
• Adrian intends to retire at age 65.
• Carmen intends to retire at age 62.

7. Protection Planning
• To review the adequacy of life, medical, critical illness and disability protection.
• To consider business continuity and key-person risk in relation to Carmen’s company.
8. Legacy, Succession and Philanthropy
• To consider wills, trusts, powers of attorney, family governance and a possible philanthropic plan related to
education or environmental causes.

Required
Assuming your team has been appointed as the financial planner and strategist to the Wong family, you are
required to prepare a comprehensive financial planning proposal addressing the family’s financial
circumstances, risks, life-stage needs and long-term aspirations.
Your team’s report should cover the following:
1. Executive Summary
Provide a concise overview of the Wong family’s financial position, major issues, planning priorities and
recommended strategic direction.
2. Cash Flow and Net Worth Analysis
Assess the family’s current financial health, liquidity, leverage, debt servicing capacity, emergency reserve
adequacy and wealth concentration risks.
3. Retirement Planning Analysis
Evaluate whether Adrian and Carmen are on track to retire at their desired ages and maintain the target
retirement lifestyle. Recommend suitable accumulation, drawdown and liquidity strategies.
4. Education Planning
Prepare a funding strategy for Chloe’s university education under both local and overseas scenarios, including
timing, funding vehicles and contingency planning.
5. Investment Planning and Portfolio Allocation

Construct an appropriate investment proposal for the family covering:
• cash and deposits
• bonds and fixed income
• equities and funds
• ESG / sustainable investments
• digital assets / tokenized products
Your proposal should explain:
• target allocation
• investment rationale
• product suitability
• risk management
• monitoring and rebalancing approach
6. ESG Integration Framework
Explain how ESG principles may be integrated into the family’s investment portfolio in a robust and credible

• strategic role of ESG investments
• product selection criteria
• possible benefits and limitations

7. Digital Asset Recommendation
Assess the role of digital assets in the family’s wealth planning. Your analysis should consider:

• volatility and downside risk
• regulation and platform risk
• custody and security
• appropriate allocation limits
• implementation options for a prudent investor
8. Risk Management and Protection Planning
Identify potential insurance and protection gaps and recommend suitable solutions in relation to:
• mortality risk
• medical and hospitalization risk
• critical illness risk
• disability / income interruption risk
• business continuity or key-person exposure
• family emergency planning
