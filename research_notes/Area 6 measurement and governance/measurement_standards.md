# Area 6 measurement standards and attribution

Research cutoff and access date: **8 November 2026**. This is research support for the final Area 6 brief, not presentation-ready model output. Confidence: high on directly read standards provisions; medium on adapting corporate methods to this illustrative household; low on unobserved project performance. No family meter readings, provider assurance, accepted budgets or appointed family roles exist in the evidence read.

## 1. Which standards apply, and what do they establish?

### Takeaway

Use standards to discipline the evidence; do not imply the household has corporate reporting obligations. Keep inventories, portfolio allocation measures, forward-looking alignment and counterfactual impact separate. A smaller reported footprint alone does not establish a smaller real-world footprint caused by the advice.

### Cited findings

**Competition and case evidence, read locally.** The Final Topic, pp3–4, explicitly requires a dashboard covering financial, sustainability, impact and implementation outcomes; attribution, biases, decision rights, review triggers and funded giving. The Key Areas sheet pp1–2 requires measured, estimated and illustrative data to be distinguished. The case pp2,6 establishes Carmen's packaging business, the family's ESG wish and a possible educational/environmental philanthropic plan, but specifies no scholarship amount or governance roles. **GHG Corporate Standard — published March 2004; current document accessed 8 Nov 2026.** Chapter3 permits equity-share or control consolidation; operational-control reporting includes the whole controlled operation. Chapter5 requires a consistent recalculation policy for significant structural/method changes and errors; it does not prescribe a universal numerical significance threshold. Its corporate inventory is distinct from project accounting and it does not specify verification procedures. These provisions are in printed pp16–23,34–38 and the official description. [Standard PDF](https://ghgprotocol.org/sites/default/files/standards/ghg-protocol-revised.pdf); [official scope description](https://ghgprotocol.org/corporate-standard).

**Scope2 — published January 2015, with corrections listed December2022.** The guidance's pp8,12,59–61 requires location- and market-based reporting when operations are in markets with qualifying contractual information; contractual claims must meet its quality criteria. Missing site-specific contractual data has fallback hierarchies; a rooftop asset or green purchase does not itself establish a valid exclusive electricity-attribute claim. [Guidance PDF](https://ghgprotocol.org/sites/default/files/2023-03/Scope%202%20Guidance.pdf); [official edition/corrections page](https://ghgprotocol.org/scope-2-guidance).

**Scope2 revision — primary update dated16 October2026.** The public consultation ran20 Oct2025–31 Jan2026. The October update describes continuing development of a possible dual market-based framework; requirements, metrics, mandatory language and transition dates remain open. Hourly matching, deliverability and incrementality proposals must therefore be described as proposals, not current2015 rules. [Dated update, read in full](https://ghgprotocol.org/blog/scope-2-market-based-method-update-2025-2026-public-consultation-current-status).

**PCAF — PartA, third edition December2025, directly read relevant sections.** Financed emissions are accounting attribution, not proof of investor-caused reductions. Chapter6 pp163–168 recommends explaining fluctuations, including drivers unrelated to underlying decarbonisation. Asset-class methods govern attribution; quality scores run1(best) to5(worst), with weighted reporting. Disclose coverage, missing data and verification status; report investees' scopes1+2 separately from scope3. Appropriate attribution reduces double counting, but overlap across value chains remains and is disclosed. These are methods for financial institutions, not household obligations. [PartA2025 PDF](https://carbonaccountingfinancials.com/files/standard-launch-2025/PCAF-PartA-2025-V3-December.pdf); [official edition listing](https://www.carbonaccountingfinancials.com/en/resources).

**PCAF — supplemental guidance December2025, pp4–9.** Financed avoided-emissions reporting is optional, separate from financed generated emissions; the two cannot be compared or deducted. Separate specific use-of-proceeds from general corporate financing and report quality and reported-versus-estimated shares. The supplement expressly addresses financial institutions; it is not a corporate solution-calculation method. [Supplement PDF](https://carbonaccountingfinancials.com/files/standard-launch-2025/PartA-PCAF-Supplement-Avoided-Emissions-FLM.pdf).

**WBCSD — v2.0 published24 July2025.** The official release identifies a comparison with the alternative without the solution; expanded reference-scenario, data and contribution-validation guidance; eligibility gates and reporting/review templates. This is guidance, not a household law. FullPDF download required a contact form and was **not obtained**; the exact v2.0 normative non-netting clause remains unverified here. Do not reuse the2023 wording as though checked against2025. PCAF2025 above independently establishes separate/non-netted financed reporting. [Official v2.0 release, read](https://www.wbcsd.org/resources/guidance-on-avoided-emissions-helping-business-drive-innovations-and-scale-solutions-toward-net-zero/).

**IFRS/ISSB — amendments issued11 December2025.** The actual amendment text, pp4–13, narrows permitted Category15 reporting to financed emissions, permits exclusion of derivatives under that limitation with disclosure, clarifies jurisdictional methodological relief and GWP rules, and permits alternative industry classifications for relevant disaggregation. AppendixC1B sets annual periods beginning on/after1 Jan2027, with early application disclosed. These are neither a new household obligation nor evidence that the Wongs must comply with2027 amendments in2026. [Actual amendment PDF](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/publications/amendments/english/2025/issb-2025-1-amendments-ifrs-s2.pdf); [official announcement](https://www.ifrs.org/news-and-events/news/2025/12/issb-issues-targeted-amendments-ifrs-s2/).

**Impact Frontiers — current undated Norms pages, accessed8 Nov2026; investor-contribution page identifies2024 consultation.** Impact concerns a caused change in outcomes, with what/who/how much/contribution/risk. Investor contribution is distinct from investee contribution. Its strategies include signalling and active engagement; signalling alone is less likely to advance outcomes than other contributions. This supports a claim ladder, rather than either "all ESG investing creates impact" or "secondary-market investing changes nothing". [Five dimensions, read relevant substantive text](https://impactfrontiers.org/norms/five-dimensions-of-impact/); [investor contribution, read relevant substantive text](https://impactfrontiers.org/norms/investor-contribution/).

## 2. What did the plan change, and how should the arithmetic reconcile?

### Takeaway

First reconcile the observable change; then estimate how much is plausibly caused by the intervention. These are different tasks. Show demand, weather/activity, tariff and emissions-factor effects separately; keep an unexplained residual when the data cannot identify causes.

### Cited findings

Weather-normalised analysis has a primary methodological precedent: Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory's BETTER documentation fits monthly energy use to temperature, separates weather-sensitive cooling/heating from baseload and uses change-point models. This supports adjustment rather than attributing every bill reduction to a retrofit. The methods page is undated/current; it links pre-cutoff2019,2021 and2024 publications. It is **not** evidence that the Wong warehouse fits its model or has a particular saving. [LBNL method description](https://better.lbl.gov/how_it_works/).

PCAF's fluctuation-analysis recommendation above provides a parallel warning for portfolios: a reported fall may reflect attribution denominators, market values, allocation, coverage or data changes rather than physical decarbonisation. [PartA2025, printed pp163–166](https://carbonaccountingfinancials.com/files/standard-launch-2025/PCAF-PartA-2025-V3-December.pdf).

### Inferences: exact exploratory arithmetic, not Wong readings or official estimates

All amounts below are **ILLUSTRATIVE arithmetic** in nominal HK$, one annual period, with no inflation/FX conversion. They are not shared-model outputs and must not enter slides as measured results. Set energyQ in kWh, flat tariffT inHK$/kWh and factorF inkgCO2e/kWh. Real bills with fixed charges, demand charges, blocks, fuel adjustment, subsidies and taxes require the actual tariff function, not simplyQ×T.

For a simplified variable billB=QT:

`B1 − B0 = T0ΔQ + Q0ΔT + ΔQΔT`.

For electricity emissionsE=QF:

`E1 − E0 = F0ΔQ + Q0ΔF + ΔQΔF`.

Omitting the interaction fails reconciliation. If two bars are preferred, a symmetric split is exact:

`ΔB = ((T0+T1)/2)ΔQ + ((Q0+Q1)/2)ΔT`.

This allocates half the interaction to each factor. It is an accounting convention, not a causal estimate. State which convention is used and preserve it across reviews.

**Worked raw comparison.** BaselineQ0=100,000; currentQ1=80,000; T0=1.50,T1=1.65; F0=0.50,F1=0.45. Bills fallHK$150,000→132,000, a savingHK$18,000. Emissions fall50→36tCO2e, a decline14t.

| Reconciliation | Quantity effect | Price/factor effect | Interaction | Total change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Bill, baseline-weight terms | −HK$30,000 | +HK$15,000 | −HK$3,000 | −HK$18,000 |
| Emissions | −10t | −5t | +1t | −14t |

Do not say the project savedHK$18,000 or14t. That conclusion requires a counterfactual energy estimate at the current weather/activity.

**Add a counterfactual.** Suppose a separately fitted and documented baseline model predicts **110,000kWh without the project at current-year activity/weather**. This110,000 figure is invented solely to demonstrate the method. Estimated intervention saving is110,000−80,000=30,000kWh. Weather/activity adds10,000 relative to the old raw year. At current prices the project's gross variable-bill saving isHK$49,500; its inventory-factor equivalent is13.5t. No embodied/lifecycle conclusion follows.

| Sequential reconciliation, order disclosed | Bill change | Emissions change |
|---|---:|---:|
| Change tariff/factor on oldQ0 | +HK$15,000 | −5t |
| Add weather/activity at current tariff/factor | +HK$16,500 | +4.5t |
| Apply project at current tariff/factor | −HK$49,500 | −13.5t |
| **Observed total** | **−HK$18,000** | **−14t** |

The sequential waterfall absorbs interactions into later steps. Do not add an extra interaction row to this waterfall. The13.5t is an adjusted operational estimate, not necessarily marginal system-wide avoided emissions; an avoided-emissions claim needs the appropriate system boundary, reference alternative and time-specific factors.

For blocks/fixed charges, calculate the counterfactual invoice as`Tariff1(Qcounterfactual) − Tariff1(Qactual)`. For fleet decisions retain kilometres, payload/service delivered, diesel litres, charging kWh, routes and downtime: compare the same logistics service. For ordinary replacement use a compliant plausible diesel/efficient alternative, not perpetual old-asset operation.

**Practical adjustment protocol (proposed, not adopted):** obtain a full operating/weather cycle of bills and operating records, preferably monthly/submeter data; fit baseline with calendar duration, weather and relevant activity; freeze the baseline model before evaluation; inspect errors, missing/estimated bills and changed operations; calculate counterfactual at reporting-period conditions; report prediction/meter/factor uncertainty. Do not impose a standard12-month/95%confidence rule as if universally required. A small project may warrant equipment-level measurement rather than an unstable whole-building regression. Rebound, changed hours and other concurrent improvements belong in the adjustment/residual record. No observational regression alone proves causality.

**Financed-emissions attribution miniature.** Illustrative issuer emissions100,000t, EVIC HK$1,000M, household holdingHK$1M→100t attributed. If EVIC rises toHK$1,250M while emissions and the household holding stay the same, attributed emissions become80t:20% lower, zero issuer physical reduction. This is a denominator demonstration, not a prediction; if share prices change, the household holding value may change too and must be reflected. It explains why actual numerator/denominator and holding changes must all be reconciled. Applying the same-currency PCAF formula is accounting attribution, never proof of household-caused reductions.

## 3. Which evidence system should the project use?

### Takeaway

Keep the client page small and the technical dictionary complete. A useful measure points to a decision; missing data should cause a data action or a weaker claim, not a fabricated green result or automatic sale.

### Cited findings

The Final Topic pp3–4 requires an actionable four-outcome dashboard, and the existing team dashboard already attempts one. Its source definitions and thresholds remain drafts, not an accepted family mandate. [Local primary topic](../../Final/research/area6_support/text/topic.txt); [team draft](../../Final/research/area1_6_framework.md).

The business/property context read is `Final/research/area4_property_scenario.md`, together with `Final/Final_Plan.md`, `Final/Decision_List.md` and the measurement/attribution sections of `area1_6_framework.md`. **`Final/research/area4_assets_update.md` did not exist at the time checked.** Candidate property metrics from the older brief are electricity, estimated retrofit cash savings, insurance terms, relative old/new-flat exit values and buyer-ready lease/data records; they require revalidation and actual inputs. No existing property scenario result was rerun or promoted to a verified claim.

### Inferences: recommendations and handoff

**Five separate ledgers, with cross-referenced project IDs.**

1. Household and full-business operational inventories: sources, consumption, factor year/method, boundary, whether estimated activity is used; keep the entities separate.
2. Portfolio attributed emissions: eligible holdings, look-through, scopes, attribution denominator, dates, coverage and method; avoid duplicate positions/look-through and keep issuer scopes separate.
3. Portfolio alignment/exposure: WACI, taxonomy alignment and any forward-looking temperature methodology. The methods cannot be combined as a single impact score.
4. Project/counterfactual results: functional service, baseline alternative, life, geographic/temporal boundary, activity/weather adjustment, direct and lifecycle effects, uncertainty, additionality/contribution evidence. Cross-reference inventory reductions; do not sum both as distinct atmosphere benefits.
5. Philanthropic chain: irrevocable capital received, provider fees, activities, verified grants/outputs, beneficiary outcomes, counterfactual/contribution limits. A count of funded students establishes outputs, not long-run causal education effects.

**A small client-facing measurement panel** can combine other researchers' financial/governance rows with the following three environmental/implementation rows:

| Row | Show on client page | Baseline/latest and quality | Decision trigger and action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating intervention | Adjusted kWh/service or litres/km; net HK$cash result; separately estimated tCO2e | Actual invoices/operating records versus pre-agreed counterfactual; label missing, estimated and predicted inputs | A saving shortfall beyond the measurement uncertainty or actual debt covenant breach prompts equipment/operations review; investigate before repeating the impact claim. No arbitrary10% target. |
| Portfolio sustainability | Like-for-like WACI versus conventional twin, with same-scope coverage and reported/estimated split | Same dates, emissions scopes, issuer revenue period/currency and look-through rules; no made-up starting value | Change in product mandate, data method, costs, exclusion/alignment compliance or material coverage loss prompts data review and then a suitability decision.30%/80%cutoffs remain candidate family choices, not regulatory thresholds. |
| Delivery and evidence | Next critical action, due date, nominated-but-unaccepted owner, evidence status | Signed approval/quote/data record rather than a vague completion percentage | Missed contractual/subsidy/legal deadline prompts immediate action; other dates set from dependency and lead time. No universal six-month grace period. |

Include impact/grant and retirement funding rows from the other research streams to complete the four outcome types; do not burden the client with every technical metric. Keep financed emissions in the technical annex if data costs exceed decision value. Show **not measured** rather than zero.

**Dictionary record for every metric:** ID; question/decision served; entity/boundary; unit; stock/flow/time horizon; sourceURL/invoice/version; source publication and effective date; observed period/access date; data lag; baseline and counterfactual rationale; calculation incl currency conversion; eligibility denominator; observed versus estimated split; coverage; uncertainty/material limitations; verification scope/provider/date; target/limit and who approved it; preparer/reviewer/decision-maker (all subject to acceptance); cadence; trigger; specific action; evidence link; restatement history. Owners are proposals until accepted.

**Coverage example and comparability.** If80% of eligible equity market value has usable emissions+revenue data, report80%coverage and20%unknown. A covered-only WACI uses`sum(wi × Ei/Revenuei)/0.80`, with original portfolio weightswi over covered issuers. State this rebasing. Do not imply missing issuers have zero emissions. Compare against the twin on the same definition and preferably common issuer/period data; show the unmatched share. WACI's revenue unit can be tCO2e/HK$M revenue (convert issuer revenue using a stated common FX rule) or the conventional US$M, but do not change currency silently between reports. PCAF financed emissions and WACI are different metrics and cannot substitute for one another. Fund/index-rule target reductions are not measured actual family holdings results.

**Restatement workflow:** preserve original baseline and current series with version/date; classify revised data versus revised method/boundary versus actual change; record old/new, magnitude, reason, affected decisions and reviewer; apply consistent recalculation symmetrically to favourable and unfavourable changes. A factual meter error is corrected; a genuine tariff change is a driver, not a reason to rewrite the past. Structural business changes need both comparable-boundary series and a clear bridge to actual operations. Set significance by whether the correction could alter the family's decision or claim, with any separately applicable reporting requirement respected. Do not adopt an arbitrary5%threshold as a GHG rule. Never move a target after missing it without preserving the old target and approval rationale.

**Engagement evidence:** ask the fund manager for the named objective, issuer baseline, dated actions, votes/escalations, issuer response, observed outcome and other plausible causes. Record that the family selected a manager whose process may support change; do not attribute all investee reductions to this household or divide tonnes by household ownership as proof of causal contribution. No evidence→report policy/process only. Escalation can be an evidence request, voting-process review or replacement consideration, according to suitability and costs, not instant divestment by default.

**Strongest counterargument:** a small family could spend more verifying emissions than it can improve. This worries me materially: a corporate-style dashboard with12technical metrics may obscure cash-flow risk. Use readily available bills, holdings reports and charity statements, prioritise decisions with material costs/risks, disclose gaps and commission specialist measurement only when the decision or contractual claim justifies it. A credible "not established" answer is useful.

**What would change the recommendation:** validated equipment/site data and a sound counterfactual could support a project result; bond-by-bond aligned impact reporting and credible investor-contribution evidence could strengthen an investment claim; a demonstrably decision-relevant data contract could justify more precise portfolio emissions; actual family-approved risk/values budgets could replace draft thresholds.

Pete exports/checks any selected shared-model outputs before presentation use. Nothing here edits shared models, plans, slides or frozen submissions.
