## 1. Strategy

### Thesis: "Built to Last"
> *The Wongs have already done the hard part: a HK$24.4M balance sheet, over 40% of after-tax income saved each year
> (parents only, HK$754K of HK$1.74M), and only 10% leverage. Our plan makes it last, for four people with four
> different views of risk.*

| Pillar | Meaning | Sections |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Protect what has been built** | Protection gaps; wills, EPAs, advance directives; liquidity that survives a death | §2, §8, §9 |
| **2. Turn assets into lifelong income** | Retirement income floor, property option, Carmen's business transition | §3, §9 |
| **3. Give every voice a place** | Adrian's security, Carmen's values, Ryan's conviction, Chloe's choices | §4–§7, Ryan |

**Stance: a prudent core with an innovative edge.** The case asks for "innovative yet prudent"; the Final gives 40
points to innovation; the judges combine fintech/Web3, ESG and practising advisers. Put the innovation in the
*method and the vehicle*, never in the *size of the risk*.

### Tone rules (the plan is read by the client first, the judges second)
- **Lead with strengths, then next steps.** Write "most of the family's wealth is invested in their home and Carmen's
  business", not "61% concentrated".
- **Put risks in numbers, not alarm words.** Give them their full numbers in §2 and §11, in neutral language.
- **Treat digital assets proportionately.** HK$500K is ≈2% of net worth, and part of it is a tokenised MMF (cash). It
  gets structure (form, size, controls), not a warning. Never recommend *increasing* it, and never lecture about it.
- **Quantify every recommendation** and trace it to an assumption.

**Risk profile has three parts.**
- **Tolerance** is given: Adrian L–M, Carmen M–H, Ryan H.
- **Capacity** is high: strong surplus, low leverage.
- **Need** is low: ≈2% real, see §3.3.

We recommend the **lowest** of the three. That is the core suitability argument for a moderate allocation.

**Value-of-advice chart:** start from the status-quo retirement success probability and legacy value, then add one
change at a time (tax election → protection → IPS/guardrails → income floor → business transition). It proves each
innovation earns its place. Drop any that don't.

### 3.3 §3 Retirement (1.75 pages)
- **Target:** HK$780K a year in today's money, for 29 years (Carmen 60 → 89) → **≈ HK$17.0M** needed at 2037
  (2% real).
- **Risk need:** the parents' liquid assets and MPF, less a conservative overseas education reserve, plus HK$718K a
  year of surplus, reach ≈ HK$17.4M by 2037 at **2% real (≈4.5% nominal)**. That is *before* counting the flat or the
  business. **They need little risk.** Fahtai to confirm.

**① The income floor.**
- **Floor size, derived from data:** the C&SD 2024/25 Household Expenditure Survey's top quartile spends
  HK$65,210 a month (≈ HK$782K a year, ≈ the Wongs' target). On a cash basis for a retired owner that splits into
  essentials 36.9% / lifestyle 45.0% / discretionary 18.2%. Adding rates and government rent (≈ HK$27.6K) gives a
  **floor of ≈40% = HK$312K a year today**, ≈ HK$430K nominal in 2039. HES is a proxy; state it as one.
- **Funding:**
  - MPF annuitised through the HKMC Annuity. Projected MPF is Adrian ≈ 2.31M at 65 and Carmen ≈ 1.81M at 62, which
    gives **≈ HK$268K a year**.
  - An optional reverse mortgage: ≈ HK$230–260K a year.
  - Silver Bonds (CPI-linked) as available.

  | Floor | MPF annuity covers | + reverse mortgage |
  |---|---|---|
  | **40% (base)** | **62%** | **116–123%** |
  | 60% | 41% | 77–82% |
  | 80% | 31% | 58–61% |

- **Sizing rule:** annuitise only enough to close the gap left after other guaranteed income, because annuities
  cannot be reversed. Payments are fixed in nominal terms and lose ~45% of their real value over 24 years, so the
  portfolio still matters.

**Key product facts (verified 23 Sep):**
- **HKMC Annuity:**
  - Age 60+; HK$50K–5M per person.
  - Payout per HK$1M: at 60, M 6.1% / F 5.6%; at 65, **M 6.9% / F 6.3%**; at 70, M 7.8% / F 7.0%.
- **Reverse mortgage (RMP):**
  - Age 55+. Fixed rate 4% for 30 years, then Prime − 2%.
  - Payouts are based on the **younger borrower's age**.
  - **Valuation cap:** 100% of value up to HK$8M plus 50% of the excess, so the HK$11.5M flat counts as **HK$9.75M**.
  - Life payout per HK$1M a month: 55 → HK$1,660; 60 → HK$2,020; 70 → HK$3,070 (joint-borrower rates are lower).
  - Costs: 1.96% upfront insurance spread over 7 years, plus 1.25% a year on the loan.
  - Lets them stay in the home for life.
- **Policy reverse mortgage (PRMP):** relevant to the HK$420K of policy cash values.
- **Silver Bond 2026:** 3-year, minimum 4.25%, age 60+. Adrian qualifies from about 2032.

**Also in §3:**
- Bucket strategy with 2–3 years of spending in cash.
- Monte Carlo: probability of sustaining HK$780K to age 89 and to 95.
- Sensitivity: returns, inflation, longevity.
- Post-retirement **medical as a separate, age-banded line** (methodology §1): Adrian ≈ HK$74K at 65 → ≈ HK$349K at

### 3.4 ② The family Investment Policy Statement (feeds §3, §5, §11)

  - no inflation increase after a year of negative returns;

- **Triggers:**

### 3.6 §9 Succession and legacy (my part; Lookbua does the property NPV)

**Estate duty was abolished for deaths from 11 Feb 2006**, so the case for succession planning is about control,
liquidity, staging and governance, not tax. Say so; it marks the proposal as Hong Kong-literate.

**Intestacy in numbers (Cap. 73 s.4:** spouse takes chattels + HK$500K + half the residue; the children share the
rest on statutory trust):
- **Adrian dies first (joint-tenancy base):** his estate is ≈ HK$5.1M. Carmen gets ≈ HK$2.9M from it (the flat and
  the HK$2.0M policy pass outside the estate). **Ryan and Chloe get ≈ HK$1.09M each**; Chloe's is held on trust only
  until 18.
- **Both die:**
  - The younger is presumed to survive the elder (**Cap. 219 s.11**), so the flat routes **through Carmen's estate**,
    where her creditors can reach it (*Ting Kam Yuen v Cheung Wing Kin* [2011]).
  - **Each child gets ≈ HK$11.9M, unstructured.**
  - **No guardian for Chloe.** Cap. 13 s.6(1)–(2) allows one to be appointed by will or deed.

**Ownership: assume, test, then design.** The case is silent. Assume joint tenancy (the most likely arrangement), test
the alternatives, and verify title in Month 1.

| Title | If Adrian dies intestate |
|---|---|
| **Joint tenants (base)** | Carmen keeps the whole flat plus ≈ 2.9M; each child ≈ 1.1M |
| Tenants in common | Carmen ≈ 5.3M; each child ≈ 2.3M; the children co-own part of the home |
| Adrian sole | Carmen ≈ 7.75M from a 14.8M estate; she must pay **≈ HK$1.95M** to keep the home (Cap. 73 s.7) |

- **Robust to ownership:** the tax result, retirement adequacy, and tax on investments (HK taxes none).
- **Depends on ownership:** the death outcomes, survivor liquidity (sole-name accounts and MPF are frozen until the
  grant, and MPF has no nomination), incapacity (one co-owner can't deal with the flat without an EPA), and creditor
  exposure.

**Target structure (default):**
- Joint tenancy.
- **Mirror wills** with a children's trust (staged at 25/30/35) and a guardian for Chloe.
- **EPAs for both**: required before any reverse mortgage or downsizing.
- Advance directives.
- A joint account holding ≈ 12 months' spending (≈ HK$1M).
- Policy beneficiaries checked.
- The business ring-fenced from the home.

**Values choice for the family:** tenants in common with a life-interest trust protects the children against a
remarriage, at the cost of complexity.

**Joint-client duty:** keep the recommendations symmetric; each spouse may take separate legal advice.

**Three documents:**

| Document | Covers | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| Will | The estate | Cap. 30 |
| EPA | Property and finance only | Cap. 501; signed before a solicitor **and** a doctor together; registered on incapacity |
| Advance directive | Refusing life-sustaining treatment | **Cap. 651, in force 31 Jul 2026**: new statute, a currency hook. Not basic or palliative care |

- **④ Business transition:**
  - Now: shareholders' agreement, an insurance-funded buy-sell, key-person cover, confirm the loan guarantee.
  - Ages 55–58: build management.
  - Ages 58–62: staged sale (no capital gains tax).
  - Realisable value ≈ HK$3.0–3.5M, treated as a **buffer**.
- **⑤ Family governance:** charter, annual meeting, matched savings for Ryan.
- **⑥ Scholarship:** HK$30K a year, deductible up to 35% of assessable income, net cost ≈ HK$25K. It links Carmen's
  mission, Chloe's interest in design and the family's ESG goal.
