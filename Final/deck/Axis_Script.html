Sustainable is a test the whole plan passes, not a label its products carry.

Pete · 1:05 · 139 spoken words (≈62 s at 135)

PeteGood morning, we're Team Axis. I'm Pete. What makes a family's plan sustainable? Our case family asks for it as a share: twenty to twenty-five per cent of their savings in ESG products. But a label describes a product, not a plan. So we test the whole plan, twice. Outside in: does the plan last when climate hits? Inside out: what does the family's money change in the world? Take our client, the Wongs: a home, a packaging business, ten and a half million saved. In a sudden carbon shock, nearly half of what they'd lose lands on their home and business, where no fund share reaches. And every carbon cut we can measure is in the business, none in the funds. So our answer: sustainable is a test the whole plan passes, not a label its products carry.

Every missing price is a decision, and the plan knows when it's wrong

Win · 0:45 · 204 spoken words (≈91 s at 135) · over its time

WinThe M in PRISM is Monitor. Our Climate Facts dashboard asks seven questions every year. Not a score — a set of answers, each with a trigger that says when the plan is wrong. Financial protection: ninety-seven percent odds to eighty-nine, checked against material life changes. Values price: the HK$0.57 million gap we just showed, reconciled whenever fees or mandates change. Operations: Carmen's baseline in 2027 — we can't claim a reduction until we've measured the starting point. Alignment: the portfolio's WACI and coverage, checked whenever the fund's method changes. Outcome: no causal result yet, and we say so. When evidence is missing, we qualify the claim — we don't drop it. And every missing price becomes one of three decisions: buy the answer now, accept a range, or set a trigger. The fund call is a buy: we check EDMW quarterly. ESG under stress is a range: the CTB index should lose less in a carbon-price shock, but nobody's measured it, so we give it no credit. Recycled PET is a trigger: at parity we switch; at a ten percent premium we pay; above that, we refuse. Pete will show you what happens when the plan meets reality.
