# Risk, Regulation and Greenwashing: a screen for any client

**Purpose.** A planner can use this with any Hong Kong client to show what a sustainability decision does to (A) the client's money and (B) the world, which rules apply, and whether the green claims hold up.
**Use:** fill it in per client. The Wongs appear only as one-line examples (marked **Example**).
**Tags:** SOURCED (named source) · ESTIMATED (our arithmetic) · ILLUSTRATIVE (assumption) · MODEL (planning model run) · CHECK (confirm before use).

---

## 1. The one-page logic

| Step | Question | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Scope | What does this client own, earn and give? | Exposure map (section 2) |
| 2. Screen | Which environment → client and client → world routes exist? | Risk grid (sections 3–4) |
| 3. Size | Which few matter in HK$ and in the plan's odds? | Price tags (section 6) |
| 4. Rules | Which rules bind whom, and do they reach this client? | Rules map (section 7) |
| 5. Claims | Is every green claim sourced, baselined and conservative? | Claims audit (section 8) |
| 6. Monitor | What do we track, and what triggers a review? | Dashboard (section 9) |

**Rule of thumb:** check every route, price only the few that matter, and say which ones were not priced.

## 2. Step 1: exposure map (intake)

Ask for these. They decide which routes matter.

| Client holds | Ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Home or property | Location, completion year, floor, owners' corporation insurance, mortgage balance, exit plan | Physical risk, building-age rules, liquidity |
| A business | Sector, customers (listed?), premises (owned/leased), vehicles, emissions data, sale plans | Transition risk, customer reporting, sale price |
| Portfolio and MPF | Holdings, fees, who decides, any "ESG" label | Alignment vs impact, label risk |
| Retirement income | Fixed-HK$ income (annuities, reverse mortgage) vs inflation-linked | Inflation sensitivity |
| Insurance and health | Medical, home, disability; ages | Heat, physical-risk cover |
| Giving | Donations, pledges, tax status | Additionality, tax relief |
| People | Who decides, who disagrees, values vs return | Conduct and mandate |

**Exposure share check.** Compute what share of net worth sits in each row. A client with most wealth in one illiquid asset (home, business) has a concentration risk first, a climate risk second.

## 3. Level 1A: environment → client (outside-in)

Use this grid per asset. Fill "client size" from the client's own numbers.

| Route | What happens | Where it lands | How to size | Typical defence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Late or sudden policy** (transition) | Carbon price or rules arrive fast: markets fall, inflation rises, recession | Portfolio, business, fixed-income retirement | Planning-model scenario with year-specific shocks to returns, CPI, asset values | Diversification, inflation-linked income, spending guardrails, cash buffer |
| **Customer and lender pull** (transition) | Listed customers must report supply-chain carbon; banks ask borrowers for data | Business sale price, loan terms | Sensitivity: sale price lower by X, customer worth Y% of sales lost | Emissions baseline, supplier data, lease security |
| **Product or sector rules** (transition) | Bans or labels change what can be sold (e.g. plastics) | Business stock, revenue | Stock value exposed | Short stock, no unsupported claims |
| **Energy prices** (transition) | Power tariffs and fuel charges rise | Household, premises | kWh × price change | Efficient equipment at replacement |
| **Heat** (physical) | More very hot days and hot nights | Health, cooling cost, insurance use | Days vs baseline; health costs sit in medical cover, not property price | Medical cover, review of cover tier |
| **Storm, rain, flood** (physical) | Damage to building, stock, access | Property (owners' levies), premises, insurance | Check official lists; ask insurer; repair levy from building inspection | Property cover at owners' corporation level, contents cover, upper floors, racking |
| **Sea level, surge** (physical) | Coastal flood, long horizon | Property exit | Official coastal-risk lists; horizon beyond planning date | Release before risk rises |
| **Old-building discount** (valuation) | Older buildings lose price against newer ones as heat and repair risk rise | Property exit | Price-gap test against a newer comparable; model route with and without a discount | Choose exit route by measured gap; deadline by building age |

**Evidence anchors (Hong Kong, SOURCED):**
- HKMA research (RM05/2023): warming and more severe typhoons could lower housing prices; ageing property raises the effect; district-wide, not a forecast.
- HKMA has a free physical-risk platform banks can use to assess a building by address under climate scenarios. It is exploratory and not mandatory.
- HKMA stress tests: the pilot found about a third of property lending sits in vulnerable areas; later tests assume a "substantial correction" in prices with no published percentage.
- Insurance: premiums more likely to rise than cover to disappear; the Insurance Authority launched a Climate Insurance Lab on 8 Sep 2026.
- Heat: hospitalisation and mortality studies show higher risk for the elderly after hot nights (Lancet Regional Health WP 2024; CUHK 2020).
- Do not use the retracted NGFS damage figures; do not apply the HKMA "more than 50%" figure to a specific flat.

## 4. Level 1B: client → world (inside-out)

For every client action ask: **does it change something real, or only who owns it?** (additionality)

| Client action | World effect | Real or label? | Evidence rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buy listed ESG funds | Lowers what the client holds, funds nothing new | **Alignment, not impact** | Check the fund exists, has assets and documents, and measure carbon intensity against a plain twin |
| Buy a green bond fund | Funds green projects, may have little price benefit | Partly real, unproven without an impact report | Ask for the impact report; compare fee gap with the greenium (about 0–8bp in studies) |
| Fund a scholarship or charity | Pays for outcomes that would not exist | **Real and additional** | Fund it, set a term, check tax rules |
| Cut the client's own business emissions (fleet, energy, materials) | Measured tonnes | **Real**, and often pays | Measure with bills and fuel receipts; estimate the rest |
| Green mortgage or label | Rebate only | **Label** | Check building certification and whether refinancing is allowed |
| Retrofit a flat | Small energy saving | Small real effect | Payback and subsidy check |
| Carbon offsets or credits | Real only if verified and additional | Usually label | Verification and additionality test |
| Divestment | Usually little cost-of-capital effect | Mostly signalling | Say so |

**Four-corner verdict (use for every action):**

| | Changes something real | Changes nothing real |
|---|---|---|
| **Good for client's money** | Do it | Do it, but don't call it green |
| **Costs the client money** | Pay for it knowingly, within a budget the client sets | Refuse |

## 5. Level 2: how it lands in the plan

| Plan risk | Why sustainability makes it worse | Check |
|---|---|---|
| **Liquidity** | Illiquid assets (home, business) may lose buyers or lenders as they age or lose data | Share of wealth in one illiquid asset; building age rules for reverse mortgage and buyers' loans |
| **Valuation** | Old-building discount; buyers discounting a business without data or lease security | Route choice with and without a discount; sale price sensitivity |
| **Liability** | Inspection notices ignored; false green claims | Notices and penalties; trade-description risk |
| **Data** | Missing building facts, Scope 3 of holdings, ratings that disagree | Tag every number; show ranges; keep a "can't measure yet" list |
| **Concentration** | One asset or one region carries most of the risk | Exposure map |
| **Inflation** | Policy shocks raise prices; fixed-HK$ income loses value | Share of retirement income that is fixed |

## 6. Step 3: pricing the few that matter

**Method (same for every client):**
1. Pick one base plan and state it (e.g. with or without a medical-tier review).
2. Run each route alone, then all together. Report the change in (a) odds the money lasts, (b) median inheritance including the home, (c) worst 10% of futures.
3. Report each route in HK$ and in plan odds. Never one without the other.
4. Label every number MODEL, ESTIMATED or ILLUSTRATIVE.
5. Run at least one scenario on the business, the property and the portfolio.

**Scenario template ("late and sudden"):**

| Variable | How to set | Source type |
|---|---|---|
| Equity return shock | One to two years, sized between a mild and a severe published stress | Bracket with central-bank and regulator stress tests |
| Bond return shock | Yield rise × duration | Short-term scenario sets |
| Inflation | Spike of 1–2 points, price level stays higher | Scenario sets |
| Property | Shock plus a small yearly drag for old buildings | HKMA research, range not forecast |
| Business | Lower sale price, lower draw during the recession | Illustrative, flag clearly |
| Fund label | No credit for a smaller hit unless carbon exposure is measured | Rule |

**Read the result in three lines:** where the loss comes from (usually ordinary market and inflation risk), which decision changes the outcome, and what defends against it.

**Example (Wongs, MODEL, recommended base):** scenario −7.3 points of odds (portfolio −3.8, flat −0.7, business −0.8); old flat 10% lower costs the reverse mortgage 0.5 points and downsizing 3.2 points.

## 7. Step 4: rules map (who binds whom)

**Test for each rule:** (1) Who must comply? (2) Is it mandatory or voluntary? (3) Does it reach this client directly, or only through their bank, fund or customers?

| Rule | Binds | Status | Reaches the client through |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HK Taxonomy** (HKMA) | Voluntary tool for banks, issuers, corporates | Phase 2A 22 Jan 2026 (25 activities, adds transition and adaptation); Phase 2B prototype 39 activities | Loan labelling; which assets count as green |
| **HKMA GS-1 Climate Risk Management** (30 Dec 2021) | Banks | Expected practice | Collateral and borrower assessment |
| **HKMA GS-2 Transition Planning** (issued 22 Sep 2026) | Banks | "Should": show progress from 22 Sep 2028; documentation in place by 1 Jul 2030; risk-management document, not a business plan, need not be disclosed | Lender asks business clients for emissions data |
| **HKMA stress tests and physical-risk platform** | Banks | Supervisory tools | Mortgage and property lending terms |
| **HKMA green and sustainable product sales rules** (Nov 2023) | Banks selling products | Asking sustainability preferences is not mandatory | Adviser asks anyway, voluntarily |
| **SFC ESG funds circular 21EC27** (in force 1 Jan 2022) | ESG funds authorised in Hong Kong | Naming, disclosure, annual assessment; failing funds removed; the list is not an endorsement | Fund choice and counting rule |
| **HKEX ESG Code Appendix C2** | Listed companies | Scope 1+2 mandatory FY2025; full climate Part D (incl. Scope 3) mandatory for LargeCap from FY2026, reports 2027 | Business client's listed customers ask for data |
| **HKFRS S1/S2** (ISSB-aligned) | Large public entities by 2028 | Phased | Indirect |
| **Climate Action Plan 2050** | Policy direction | Carbon neutral before 2050; halve (vs 2005) before 2035 | Energy prices, buyer expectations |
| **Plastics and product rules** (Cap. 603 and Phase 2) | Sellers | Phase 2 undated | Business stock |
| **Trade Descriptions Ordinance** | Sellers | No green-claims case found | Business claims |
| **Building inspection (MBIS/MWIS)** | Building owners | Penalty bill proposed; timing slipped to latter half of 2026, not confirmed introduced | Property owners' repair costs |
| **IRD charity rules** | Donors | Cash gifts to s.88 charities; deduction up to 35% of income; only while earning | Giving plan |
| **EU CBAM** | EU importers | Covers cement, iron and steel, aluminium, fertilisers, electricity, hydrogen; downstream extension still a proposal (talks continuing, next round reported 30 Nov 2026) | Only exporters of steel or aluminium goods to the EU |
| **EU SFDR, MiFID/ESMA** | EU firms | Not Hong Kong law | Used as a voluntary reference structure; CHECK SFDR text before quoting articles |

**Applicability decision tree:**
1. Does the client export steel or aluminium goods to the EU? If no, CBAM does not apply (say so).
2. Is the client's business a supplier to listed companies? If yes, expect Scope 3 data requests.
3. Does the client hold a bank loan with a building as collateral? If yes, expect climate-risk questions from the bank.
4. Does the client hold products labelled ESG? If yes, run the claims audit.
5. Does the client give to charity? If yes, check s.88 status and the tax clock.

**Adviser conduct (every client):** advice on funds, mortgages and insurance goes through SFC-licensed, HKMA-registered and IA-licensed parties. The planner coordinates, discloses fees and conflicts, and uses one risk profile per person.

## 8. Step 5: greenwashing defence

**Fact:** Hong Kong has no specific greenwashing law and no enforcement case found. Rules exist for ESG funds and banks' sales. The Consumer Council has asked for stronger oversight of green claims. The EU SFDR is the reference framework.

**Four controls:**

| Control | Rule | Check |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Baseline** | Fix the "before" in writing; compare to a plain twin | Same weights, plain funds; plain option for every green option |
| **2. Third-party verification** | Named outside source for every claim; product exists before its label | Product has assets, offering document, tracking record; two ratings for equity ESG funds |
| **3. Conservative claims** | "Aligned" not "impact" for listed funds; never net avoided emissions against footprint; count only the value you can defend; claim no price premium without evidence | Wording review |
| **4. Restatement** | If data or sources change, show old and new and say why | Dated log |

**Red-flag checklist (ask of any green claim):**
- Is the product on sale, with assets and documents, or only on a list?
- Does the label describe use of proceeds, the issuer, or the outcome?
- Is the carbon figure measured, estimated or modelled, and who says so?
- Does the claim depend on a rating that other agencies would score differently (ratings from different agencies correlate about 0.54)?
- Is "impact" claimed where the client's action would have happened anyway?
- Is the cost of the label (fees, tracking) shown next to its benefit?
- Is a rebate or discount real after fees and conditions?

**Claims audit template (one row per claim):**

| Claim | Source | Date | Tag | Confidence | Checked by | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | SOURCED / ESTIMATED / ILLUSTRATIVE | H / M / L | | OK / fix / drop |

**Example (Wongs):** a green mortgage rebate of HK$0–3K once; a fund on the SFC ESG list with US$0 in assets; "compostable" PLA that counts as plastic under Cap. 603. Each failed a control until reworded.

## 9. Step 6: dashboard and triggers

One page, reviewed once a year, with event triggers between. Four types:

| Type | Metric | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Financial | Odds money lasts | Falls more than 5 points or below a floor |
| Financial | Asset-specific test (e.g. property price gap vs newer comparable) | Gap widens 10% or more |
| Financial | Cost of values (fees plus tracking vs plain twin) | Above the client's budget |
| Sustainability | Carbon intensity of holdings vs plain twin, with data coverage | Not clearly below twin or coverage low |
| Sustainability | Client's own emissions (Scope 1+2 measured; Scope 3 as a range) | Customer or lender requests data |
| Impact | Tonnes cut by the client's own projects | Behind plan |
| Impact | Giving funded ratio | Below 100% |
| Milestones | Dated actions (legal documents, equipment orders, exit decision) | Missed by more than 6 months |

**"Can't measure yet" column (state it openly):** Scope 3 of holdings; ESG ratings; avoided emissions (report separately, never netted); additionality of listed funds; implied temperature rise (show a range or nothing).
**Event triggers:** equities fall 20%; holding drifts 5 points; fund leaves a list or changes method; insurance renewal change; death or disability; sale talks; new regulation.

## 10. What counts as a "changed decision"

Count a decision only if the framework changed money or tonnes. Typical categories:

| Category | Typical change |
|---|---|
| Refuse | Green mortgage, label-only products, unproven credits |
| Reroute | Counting rule from list membership to measured outcome |
| Add | Yearly trigger and deadline for an illiquid asset; funded giving; emissions baseline |
| Keep | Plain, low-cost options that already do the job |

---

## 11. Limits and what to check before using

- Several Hong Kong data points are still missing: repair cost per flat, flat-level insurance pricing, ground level of specific districts, HKMA's property price path in the second stress test.
- Model results depend on the chosen base plan; always state it.
- CHECK: SFDR text; HKMC payout tables for current ages; exact Taxonomy Phase 2B status; whether the green-mortgage insurance rebate applies to refinancing.
- Do not use: NGFS Phase V GDP damage figures (source paper retracted); the HKMA "more than 50%" as a flat-level number.
- Preferences: Hong Kong does not require advisers to ask about sustainability preferences; the framework asks voluntarily and records the answer.
