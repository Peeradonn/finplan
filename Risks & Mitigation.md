# 8. Risk Management and Protection Planning

# Risk Assessment

## **1. Method: FMEA (Failure Mode and Effects Analysis) adapted to household financial planning**

A protection review is really a failure-mode exercise: for each way the plan could fail, how bad is it (**Severity**), how likely (**Occurrence**), and how well would the family see it coming and act before the damage is done (**Detection**)? Multiplying the three gives a **Risk Priority Number (RPN = S × O × D, max 1,000)** that ranks gaps objectively, rather than by which one feels most dramatic — the same discipline used elsewhere in this proposal to keep tone neutral and evidence-based.

| **Score** | **Severity (impact on the family's goals)** | **Occurrence (likelihood over the relevant horizon)** | **Detection (how well existing arrangements let the family foresee/prepare — high score = poor warning)** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–2 | Negligible; absorbed by surplus without changing any goal | Rare (<1% over the horizon) | Highly visible well in advance (e.g. market prices, scheduled reviews) |
| 3–4 | Noticeable; delays a goal or draws down a buffer | Unlikely (1–5%) | Some warning signs typically precede it |
| 5–6 | Material; a stated goal (education, retirement date, home) is put at risk | Possible (5–15%) | Warning is partial or easy to miss without a formal review process |
| 7–8 | Severe; forces a major lifestyle or asset sale, or leaves a dependent unprotected | Likely over the horizon (15–30%), or a certainty conditional on another event | Little to no warning; onset is sudden |
| 9–10 | Catastrophic; threatens the home, a minor's welfare, or multiple goals at once | Frequent / near-certain over a long horizon (>30%), or the current control is a **known, confirmed gap** (e.g. "no DI cover" is stated in the case) | No detection mechanism exists at all today |

Occurrence bands are anchored, where possible, to standard patterns rather than guesswork: HK life-table mortality at ages 49–65 sits in the low single digits over a 10–13 year window; long-term disability incidence over the same window is conventionally taken as **3–4× the mortality rate** at working ages (a standard actuarial rule of thumb); critical-illness incidence sits between the two. These relative orderings, not the absolute numbers, drive the ranking below.

### 3.2 Life insurance — needs-based gap, not a round multiple of income

**Method:** debts to clear + income-replacement shortfall (PV of the gap between reduced household expenses and the
survivor's own after-tax income, for the years until the survivor's *own* retirement/income floor takes over) + final
expenses − existing cover. Discounted at **2% real**, the same conservative real return this proposal already uses
for the retirement risk-need check (§3.3 of the working brief), for consistency.

**Adrian's death (needs = should Carmen be short of income until her own retirement at 62, i.e. 13 years):**

- Household need if Adrian dies: 984,000 × 80% = **HK$787,200/yr** (assumption: a surviving two-person household
needs ~80% of prior expenses — a standard needs-analysis rule of thumb, not case data).
- Carmen's own after-tax income continues: 720,000 − 71,335 tax − 18,000 MPF = **HK$630,665/yr**.
- Shortfall = 787,200 − 630,665 = **HK$156,535/yr for 13 years**.
- PV at 2%: annuity factor = [1 − 1.02⁻¹³] / 0.02 = (1 − 0.7730) / 0.02 = **11.35**.
- PV of shortfall = 156,535 × 11.35 ≈ **HK$1,776,700**.
- + Mortgage clearance: **HK$1,800,000** (removes the debt-service burden from the survivor).
- + Final/estate settlement expenses: **HK$150,000** (assumption; typical funeral/legal/probate placeholder).
- **Gross need ≈ HK$3,726,700.**
- − Existing whole-life cover: HK$2,000,000. *(Adrian's MPF, ≈HK$1.05M, is deliberately **not** netted off here — it
stays earmarked for Carmen's own retirement income floor (①); netting it off here would rob that goal to fund this
one.)*
- **Net gap ≈ HK$1,726,700 → recommend an additional HK$1.75M of term cover on Adrian** (total death benefit
HK$3.75M).

**Carmen's death (needs = should Adrian be short of income — he is not):**

- Household need: 787,200/yr; Adrian's own after-tax income continues at 1,200,000 − ~110,435 tax − 18,000 MPF =
**HK$1,071,565/yr**, which already exceeds the reduced household need. **No income-replacement shortfall.***(Adrian's tax figure here is taken from the current separate-taxation calculation as an approximation; it is not
re-run under the changed family circumstance.)*
- + Mortgage clearance: HK$1,800,000.
- + Business-loan guarantee exposure (if the guarantee survives her and is called): HK$900,000.
- + Final expenses: HK$150,000.
- **Gross need ≈ HK$2,850,000.**
- − Existing whole-life cover: HK$1,800,000.
- **Net gap ≈ HK$1,050,000 → recommend an additional HK$1.05M of term cover on Carmen** (total personal death
benefit HK$2.85M). *Carmen's much larger exposure — the business — is not a personal life-insurance need; it is
covered separately in §3.4 below.*

### 3.3 Critical illness (CI)

**Sizing rule:** CI pays a lump sum on diagnosis, used for treatment gaps VHIS doesn't cover, income loss during
recovery, and any lifestyle adjustment — commonly sized at **2–3 years of household expenses plus a treatment/
recovery buffer**, rather than an income multiple (which double-counts the DI/life cover above).

- 2 years' expenses: 984,000 × 2 = 1,968,000
- Treatment/recovery buffer (illustrative; VHIS caps and co-pays vary by plan): HK$800,000
- **Need ≈ HK$2.75M per person.** No existing CI cover is stated in the case (only whole-life and group medical are
listed), so we treat this as a full gap for both parents, to be confirmed.

**Premium:** CI premiums rise far more steeply with age than life premiums, because morbidity curves are much more
convex than mortality curves — the AIA published rate card for this product only extends to age 25 in our source, so
we cannot extrapolate reliably to ages 49/54 without materially overstating precision. **State as an open item
rather than inventing a number:** obtain an actual quote for HK$2.75M CI cover, ages 49 and 54, non-smoker — this is
consistent with the working brief's own item 7/8 ("insurance product costs... pending").

### 3.4 Key-person insurance and buy-sell funding — Carmen's business (RPN #4, 168)

- Going-concern value: HK$5.0M (case). Ownership split not stated — **assumption: 50% held by Carmen**, consistent
with "co-founder," pending confirmation of the cap table.
- **Key-person cover** (funds the cost of disruption and replacement while a successor is found or trained):
50% × HK$5.0M = **HK$2.5M**, owned by and payable to the company on Carmen's death or long-term disability.
- **Buy-sell (cross-purchase) funding**: sized to the *same* HK$2.5M equity stake, so the co-founder can buy Carmen's
shares from her estate at full going-concern value — avoiding the 30–40% illiquidity haircut that a forced sale
would otherwise apply (methodology already prices the haircut into the HK$3.0–3.5M "buffer" figure used elsewhere;
a funded buy-sell is what lets the family realise the *undiscounted* HK$2.5M instead).
- **Symmetry point:** the co-founder should carry equivalent cover on their own life/shares, funding a matching
buy-out in the other direction — this is a two-way agreement, not a one-sided cost on Carmen.
- **Illustrative premium:** using the same female-rate benchmark as §3.2 (≈HK$121/month per HK$1M) applied to
HK$2.5M ≈ **HK$3,630/yr**, payable by the business as an operating cost, not from the household's personal
surplus (tax deductibility of the premium/proceeds should be confirmed with Win — treatment differs between
key-person and buy-sell structures).
