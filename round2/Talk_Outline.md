## The story in one line
The Wongs look secure. Left to manage it their own way (sell the business, keep the flat, sell it only if they must),
the flat has to go in {{own_sold_pct}} of futures: Carmen, at about {{own_sale_age}} and usually on her own, sells the
home where they raised Ryan and Chloe, and the wealth meant for the children pays the bills. Five calls make the home
their choice and keep the inheritance, without more risk; the plan survives every test we ran, starts this month, and
passes on in stages. Then: what this means for planners like us.

## Hard questions this framing invites
- **"Couldn't they take a reverse mortgage at {{own_sale_age}} instead of selling?"** Yes, and they could stay. That is our plan done
  late: fewer years of income from the home, and the loan still eats the inheritance. Not yet measured: it needs
  HKMC's payout rates for borrowers in their 80s (Fahtai to source). Until then, the talk says "sold" (what we measured)
  and the inheritance line carries the point either way.
- **"Why not rent, or buy cheaper than HK$7M?"** Either releases more. We use the same flat as our own downsizing
  option, so the two ways are compared on the same terms.
