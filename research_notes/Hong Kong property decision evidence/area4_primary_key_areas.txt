Key Areas for the 2026 Final Round Topic
Theme: Beyond Green Labels: Integrating Sustainable Finance and Green Finance into
Financial Planning — Financing the Transition, Pricing Climate Risk and Measuring
Real Impact
1. Sustainable Finance as a Planning Discipline
• Distinguish green finance, sustainability-linked finance, transition finance, impact
investing and ESG integration, and place each within the client’s plan.
• Profile the client’s values, return requirements, liquidity and complexity tolerance; record
the resulting mandate in the investment policy statement.
• Quantify the price of values, and resolve conflicts between decision-makers with different
objectives.
2. Financing the Transition
• Build an emissions baseline (Scope 1, 2 and 3) and a marginal abatement cost curve for
the client’s business.
• Structure the instrument: green loan, sustainability-linked loan, transition finance, green
lease or supply-chain finance, with attention to covenants, verification and subsidies.
• Test bankability, enterprise value, buyer due diligence and the link to succession and the
owner’s retirement date.
3. Portfolio Integration
• Set and justify the sustainability allocation by instrument, currency, duration and liquidity.
• Weigh the evidence on greenium, performance, fees and tracking error rather than
repeating marketing claims.
• Measure carbon intensity, taxonomy alignment and data quality, and screen products for
greenwashing.
4. Real Assets and Climate Resilience
• Assess green mortgage, retrofit and BEAM Plus certification economics for the client’s
home and business premises.
• Price physical climate risk into insurance, mortgage and exit decisions.
• Address stranded assets, the timing of downsizing and the landlord–tenant split incentive.
5. Risk, Regulation and Greenwashing
• Map the Hong Kong and international rules that affect the client, from the Hong Kong
Taxonomy and ISSB-aligned disclosure to SFC ESG fund rules and the EU’s Carbon
Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM).
• Run a climate scenario and translate it into financial consequences for the business, the
property and the portfolio.
• Build anti-greenwashing controls: baselines, verification or assurance, conservative claims
and a restatement policy.

6. Measurement, Engagement and Governance
• Design a one-page dashboard covering financial, sustainability, impact and
implementation outcomes.
• Separate attribution from narrative, and address the behavioural biases that distort client
decisions.
• Set governance, review triggers and a legacy or philanthropic allocation that is funded
rather than aspirational.
7. The Ten-Minute Deliverable
• Lead with a recommendation, the capital required and the expected financial and
sustainability outcomes.
• Include a quantified structure, a portfolio with weights and costs, a scenario, a dashboard,
a trade-off and a refusal.
• Show your assumptions and sources, and defend them under questioning.
Evidence and Assessment
• Cite authoritative sources and distinguish measured, estimated and illustrative data.
• Use Hong Kong dollars, state all assumptions, and avoid fabricated or unverifiable
statistics.
• Innovation and independent thinking carry 40 of the 100 marks in the Final Round.
What Distinguishes a Strong Answer
• A clear recommendation with a number attached to it.
• An honest treatment of trade-offs, data limits and what you would not do.
• Evidence that the sustainable finance element changes the client’s financial outcome,
not just the vocabulary.
Reference Points for Research
• HKMA — Hong Kong Taxonomy for Sustainable Finance; Sustainable Finance Action
Agenda; climate risk guidance (GS-1).
• HKEX — climate disclosure requirements under the Listing Rules; Core Climate.
• SFC — guidance on ESG fund disclosure; suitability and product due diligence
requirements.
• HKSAR Government — Climate Action Plan 2050; Green and Sustainable Finance Grant
Scheme.
• HKGBC and EMSD — BEAM Plus certification; building energy codes and consumption
data.
• ISSB, NGFS and IEA — IFRS S1 and S2; climate scenarios; energy and technology
outlooks.
• Climate Bonds Initiative, BloombergNEF and MSCI — issuance data, market pricing
and index methodology.
