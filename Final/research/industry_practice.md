# What leading firms do with ESG for clients, and the gaps PRISM fills (Claude, 11 Nov)

**Why this brief:** Pete asked us to be accurate about what firms actually do, so "the usual way" in our talk is a fair
description and not a strawman (Final_Plan.md §2–4). The two documents are in `Final/Relative Research/` (Pete, 11 Nov).
They are **not in git**: JPMorgan's is marked "For institutional and professional investors only. Not for retail use or
public distribution", so quote it briefly and with attribution, never as a screenshot.

| Document | What it is | Use |
|---|---|---|
| J.P. Morgan Asset Management, *ESG integration at J.P. Morgan Asset Management*, April 2023 (printed 08/23), 14 pp. | How a large fund manager puts ESG into its funds | Inside a fund |
| Morgan Stanley, *2025 Sustainability Report* (data at 31 Dec 2025; the file is dated 24 Oct 2026), 56 pp. Wealth Management pp. 13–14; Investment Management pp. 15–16; climate risk pp. 28–29 | What a leading wealth manager offers individual clients, and how it manages climate risk for itself | For a client |

**Verdict:** both firms are serious and well resourced, and both point ESG at **the portfolio**: inside a fund (JPMorgan)
or at matching a client's funds to the client's preferences (Morgan Stanley). In what they publish, neither tests what
climate does to a client's **whole household** (the home, a business, the plan's big moments), neither shows the client
what ESG did to their money, and the climate scenarios Morgan Stanley does run protect **the firm's own book**. That is
the gap PRISM fills. (Inference from two documents; see the caveats.)

## 1. What they do (SOURCED, with pages)

**JPMorgan Asset Management (inside its funds)**
- ESG integration is "the systematic inclusion of ESG issues in investment analysis and investment decisions", for
  "financially material" factors, "with the goals of managing risk and improving long-term returns" (pp. 1, 3).
- It certifies each investment team's **process**: a 10-metric framework (research, documentation, monitoring); a team
  needs at least 30 of 50 points and at least 2 on each metric, re-checked at least every three years (pp. 6–7, 13).
- Its own ESG score feeds the analysts; an independent risk team flags strategies whose ESG scores differ from their
  benchmark (pp. 11–13).

**Morgan Stanley Wealth Management (for individual clients)**
- The Investing with Impact Platform, since 2012: 490 products, US$103bn of client assets, more than 325,000 clients.
  "Our mission is to help clients align their portfolios with their sustainability preferences while striving for
  competitive performance" (p. 13).
- Morgan Stanley IQ lets clients "define their impact objectives in an impact profile, assess their current portfolio
  alignment and work with their Financial Advisor to seek to improve portfolio alignment over time": 115+ environmental
  and social metrics, nine themes, about US$68bn of client assets with a profile (p. 14).
- Impact Signal scores investment strategies "on the strength of their environmental and social impact" (p. 14).
- The "Three I's" (Intentionality, Influence, Inclusion) sort strategies: screening, ESG integration, thematic and
  impact-first; active ownership and market building; diverse- or employee-owned managers (p. 14).
- A Climate Action Investing Toolkit helps advisers "engage with clients about the risks and opportunities of climate
  action investing" (p. 20).

**Morgan Stanley's climate risk management (for the firm)**
- Firm Risk Management identifies and manages climate-related financial risks "in order to limit their potential impact
  on Morgan Stanley", with transition and physical scenario analysis (pp. 28–29).
- It names what climate does to clients' assets: physical events "could affect the ability of certain clients or
  customers to repay their obligations, reduce the value of collateral, increase costs (including the cost or
  availability of insurance coverage)" (p. 28).

## 2. What they say ESG integration does **not** do (their own words, JPMorgan)
- It "does not change a strategy's investment objective, exclude specific types of companies or constrain a
  strategy's investable universe" (p. 3).
- Securities "may be purchased and retained, without limit, regardless of potential ESG impact" (pp. 1, 5).
- "The impact of ESG integration on performance is not specifically measurable" (pp. 3, 5).
- "ESG factors are not considered for each and every investment decision" (p. 3).
- "We can make no assurance that the integration ... will align with the individual beliefs or values of a particular
  client" (p. 4); integrated products "are not designed for clients who ... are looking for products that meet
  specific ESG goals" (p. 5).
- It "should never result in sacrificing portfolio returns" (p. 8).
- "There is no standard ESG scoring system", and providers' conclusions "may differ significantly" (p. 11).

Morgan Stanley, on its own scenarios: their outcomes "continue to be inherently uncertain and not comparable among
companies" (p. 29); and "current policies, technology adoption and consumption habits are not aligned with a 1.5˚C
outcome" (p. 20), which supports a late, sudden transition as a scenario worth testing.

## 3. The gaps for a family, and what PRISM does (our inference)

| What they do | The gap for a family like the Wongs | PRISM | Where we show it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Align the **portfolio** with the client's preferences (MS IQ, IIP) | The home and the business carry most of the climate risk, and neither is in the portfolio (the Wongs: 61% of assets) | **R and I** look at every asset the family owns, both ways | Slides 1, 4 |
| Run climate scenarios **for the firm's own book**, and know that climate can cut collateral values and raise insurance costs (MS pp. 28–29) | Nobody runs that storm on the client's own plan | **R** runs one storm through the family's whole plan (the pre-mortem) | Slide 1; backup 12 |
| ESG integration whose effect on performance "is not specifically measurable" (JPMAM) | The client never sees what ESG did to their money | **S** prices every decision in HK$, in a normal future and under stress | Slides 5–8 |
| Start from **preferences**; assume no cost ("should never result in sacrificing portfolio returns") | Values are either assumed free or never priced; disagreements in the family aren't settled | **P** sets each person's limits and one values budget first; **S** prices values against them (−HK$0.57M against plain twins) | Slides 3, 8 |
| Score **alignment and impact** of funds (115 metrics; Impact Signal) | Owning aligned shares isn't the same as changing anything | **I** claims only what the family's decisions change (the vans), and reports the rest as a footprint | Slides 4, 6–8 |
| Certify the **process** (10 metrics) and review it | Nobody checks the client's outcome or says when to decide again | **M** tracks money and tonnes, with triggers and attribution | Slide 9 |
| Ratings that "may differ significantly" | A label can mean very different things | **S**'s product check reads the fund's binding rule, not its rating | Slides 1, 8 |

**The one-line version:** *the best firms make ESG part of the portfolio; PRISM makes climate part of the plan.*

## 4. Caveats (how far this evidence goes)
- **Two firms, both global, both leaders.** Hong Kong practice may be thinner: we found no Hong Kong rule that makes
  advisers ask about sustainability preferences (Final_Plan.md §9, CHECK). So "even the leaders" is the safer claim, not
  "every planner".
- **A sustainability report isn't an advice manual.** Morgan Stanley's private wealth advisers may well discuss a
  client's home or business in holistic planning; the report doesn't describe it. Say "in what they publish", never
  "they don't".
- **JPMorgan's document is from April 2023.** Check the current version on am.jpmorgan.com before quoting it on stage.
- **No fund-level judgement.** Nothing here says their products are bad; the point is the unit of analysis (the
  portfolio, not the household).

## 5. What this changes in the talk (proposals for Pete)
1. **The opening's first line is too blunt for the leaders.** "Are we putting enough into ESG funds?" fits a product
   sale, not Morgan Stanley's impact profiles. More accurate, and still plain: *"Today, ESG in a family's plan is about
   the portfolio: do the funds match the family's values?"* The rest of the opening stands: the label can't tell two
   funds apart, and it says nothing about what climate does to the family (the home and the business).
2. **Slide 2's "instead of" lines are now sourced:** asking preferences (MS IQ's impact profile), rating products
   (JPMAM's integration), alignment as impact (IQ metrics, Impact Signal), the portfolio as the unit. Name the source in
   the notes, not on the slide.
3. **A backup slide, "What the leaders publish, and what PRISM adds"** (§3's table, five rows), for the question every
   panel asks: *"Who does this already?"*
4. **Hard question to add to §8:** *"Morgan Stanley already lets clients set impact profiles. What's new?"* Answer: their
   tool measures how the portfolio lines up with the client's values; we start from the family's limits, test every asset
   the family owns in one storm, and price each decision in HK$. We'd use a tool like theirs inside our Impact step.
