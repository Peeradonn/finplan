## Which premises can qualify, and which current support actually applies?

### Takeaway

The first job is to identify the asset, electricity account and decision rights. A small flat-level certification route exists; a whole-building green rating is a different undertaking. The warehouse can potentially claim a utility equipment subsidy, while the residential EV subsidy is closed to new applications. **CLP Electrical Equipment Upgrade Scheme (EEUS).** The official NRT guideline, version **17 Jan 2024**, applies **1 Jan 2024–31 Dec 2028**, subject to funding. An eligible account holder can apply for qualifying equipment installed for business use at its registered supply address; tenancy is not itself an exclusion. NRT cap is **HK$10,000 per account for the entire scheme period**, not yearly. Eligible LED tubes receive the lower of HK$60 each and 80% of net unit purchase price; Grade 1 room AC receives the lower of HK$1,000 and 80%. Apply within six months of purchase; product categories can be claimed once per year. Proof of purchase, eligibility/installation checks and funding availability matter. Do not assume the full account cap is an entitlement or upfront cash. The 2026 support page identifies HK$40M allocation; it separately lists a new RCx pilot whose detailed terms were still to be released. [Guideline](https://www.clp.com.hk/content/dam/clphk/documents/my-business-site/low-carbon-solutions/eeus/EEUS_NRT%20Application%20Guideline_bilingual.pdf), [2026 support page](https://www.clp.com.hk/en/help-support/subsidies-support/sme). Accessed 8 Nov 2026; support page undated.

**Do not confuse EEUS with CLP Eco Building Fund.** The latter concerns **communal** building installations, generally through owners/OC or authorised representatives. Its FAQ says retrofit funding is around 30–50% depending on circumstances, excludes recurrent maintenance, is reimbursed after the recipient pays, and other funding is deducted. It is not automatically a subsidy for the warehouse tenant's own lights. [Fund FAQ](https://www.clp.com.hk/en/business/low-carbon-solutions/funds-and-subsidies/eco-building-fund/faq), undated current page, accessed 8 Nov 2026. High confidence on scope; actual approval unverified.

## Which projects pass a reproducible financial test?

### Takeaway

Use incremental lifecycle cash flow, not a generic percentage saving or simple payback alone. Replace failed equipment against an ordinary replacement; compare early replacement against keeping it until failure. A lease can turn a sound long-life asset into a bad tenant investment. Company savings must be kept separate from Carmen's household cash flow.

### Cited findings

EMSD recommends efficient cooling, correct sizing/airflow and maintenance, avoiding overcooling, and switching equipment off when unoccupied. Its commercial advice gives technology comparisons, not a measured saving for Carmen's warehouse. [EMSD commercial energy advice](https://ecib.emsd.gov.hk/index.php/en/energy-saving-advices/commercial-sector), undated current page, accessed 8 Nov 2026. Therefore no generic EMSD savings percentage is applied to unknown family bills.

### Inferences and worked examples

**Method recommended for this project (opinion, high confidence).** For every project define a service-equivalent baseline and cash owner. Compute:

`NPV = -incremental installed cost + sum[(avoided bills + avoided maintenance + approved grants - extra O&M - verification - financing-related fees)/(1+r)^t] + (residual proceeds - removal/reinstatement costs)/(1+r)^N`.

Use nominal HK$ cash flows and a matching nominal discount rate; price escalation is a scenario. If borrowing is modelled explicitly, use equity cash flows (purchase less loan proceeds; repayments thereafter), or use unlevered project cash flow and a suitable capital cost. Do not subtract the purchase price and count loan principal repayment without loan proceeds. Do not count a rebate twice or assume a tax deduction without confirming tax treatment. Include disposal, permits, downtime and safety/compliance work in actual quotes. For shared fabric/cooling measures, estimate interactive effects; summing isolated savings can double-count cooling-load reductions.

**Reproducibility:** run `python "research_notes/Hong Kong property decision evidence/area4_retrofit_calcs.py"` from repository root. Actual run output is saved in `area4_retrofit_results.json`. The calculator uses only standard Python and does not import or edit the shared model. All inputs below other than the identified tariff/conditional subsidy rule are invented **scenario parameters**, not estimates of the Wongs' actual equipment or budget. Examples have no taxes or causal property premium. Flat nominal annual cash flows, annual end-year discounting, **5% nominal discount rate**, zero salvage unless stated.

**A. Warehouse LED: survey-dependent conditional approval.** Hypothetical **100** old tubes at **42 W whole-circuit** replaced by **18 W** LEDs, same useful illumination, **3,000 h/year**, installed capex **HK$22,000**, avoided maintenance **HK$500/year**, **3-year enforceable remaining lease**. Assume no reinstatement cost/residual. Current November NRT marginal-price scenario **HK$1.58/kWh**, held constant solely for calculation:

- Electricity saved: `100 × (42−18)/1000 × 3000 = 7,200 kWh/year`.
- Net annual benefit: `7200 × 1.58 + 500 = HK$11,876`.
- Three-year company NPV without subsidy: **+HK$10,341**. Gross simple payback **1.85 years**.
- If all 100 tubes meet EEUS criteria, unit purchase prices support the cap, no prior quota use and funding/application approval succeeds: nominal subsidy **HK$6,000**, assumed received at end year 1; company NPV becomes **+HK$16,056**. It remains positive without a grant.
- With **one year** left, no grant, NPV **−HK$10,690**. With **1,200 h/year and HK$1.20/kWh**, three-year NPV **−HK$11,227**; even the conditional grant leaves **−HK$5,513**. With an extra **HK$5,000 reinstatement** payment at year 3, base NPV falls to **+HK$6,022**. With **4,500 h/year and HK$1.90/kWh**, NPV is **+HK$35,243**.
- At base wattage/cost/maintenance/price/three-year lease and no grant, operating-hours break-even is about **1,999 h/year**. This is a far better procurement condition than “do LED whatever the lease says.” No area multiplier is used.
- At an illustrative **8% annual amortising loan**, three annual repayments on HK$22,000 are **HK$8,537/year**; base saving/debt-service ratio **1.39** before wider business debt. This is a cash coverage check, not a quoted loan and not the company's overall DSCR. Low-use savings do not cover it.

**Carmen's share:** at the user's illustrative 50% ownership, half the base company economic NPV is **HK$5,171**, or **HK$8,028** with conditional grant. This is not extra household salary/dividends and not guaranteed distributable cash. Company tax, debt covenants, co-owner decision rights, retained earnings and actual distributions intervene. Do not add company savings to household income and also capitalise the same saving into a business sale price.

**B. Home AC at failure: compare replacements.** Independent hypothetical example: ordinary service-equivalent replacement HK$5,000, efficient alternative HK$7,000; incremental cost HK$2,000; saving **500 kWh/year**; price scenario **HK$1.50/kWh**; extra maintenance **HK$50/year**; common life **8 years**, equal zero residual. Annual incremental saving HK$700; NPV **+HK$2,524**. Annual electricity-saving break-even is **240 kWh**. This supports a conditional efficient-replacement rule, not a claim that every Grade 1 option pays.

**C. Home AC before failure: compare now versus wait.** Another independent hypothetical example: retain functioning unit for **3 years**, then buy the same HK$7,000 efficient unit, versus buy it now. Old-to-efficient saving **400 kWh/year** at HK$1.50 during years 1–3; thereafter both paths have the same operating cash flow. Matched **8-year horizon**; assume efficient equipment lasts 10 years and straight-line remaining-service value (early path HK$1,400 at year 8, waiting path HK$3,500). NPV of replacing early versus waiting is **−HK$741**. The residual assumptions matter; this is not a market resale valuation. A hotter/longer-use home, higher breakdown cost or health/comfort benefit could change the result. Conversely, comparing full early cost with only the efficiency premium at failure is an unfair comparison.

**D. Film/building fabric:** no verified flat orientation, glazing, façade rights, ventilation or cooling loads. Hypothetical film capex HK$3,000, saving 100 kWh/year at HK$1.50, 10-year life gives NPV **−HK$1,842**; break-even about **259 kWh/year**. Seek measured solar gain and installed quotations before recommending film, glazing or insulation. Mandatory repairs, weatherproofing and occupant health may justify work with negative energy-only NPV; state that purpose separately. No current evidence supports an across-the-board HK$1,900/year combined package for this family.

**E. Solar: cash receipts and emissions claims differ.** The government announcement **26 Apr 2022**, effective **27 Apr 2022**, set rates at HK$4/kWh ≤10 kW, HK$3 above 10–200 kW, HK$2.50 above 200 kW–1 MW. These are capacity tiers, not progressive blocks. Verify the actual application/completion-letter rate before procurement. [Government announcement](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202204/26/P2022042600448.htm), accessed 8 Nov 2026.

CLP's operative linked FiT terms dated **10 Jun 2023** require the applicant's electricity account at the premises; a non-owner must obtain owner consent. Contract §3.3 ends **31 Dec 2033**, subject to earlier termination; §§5.1–5.3 transfer all associated environmental attributes to CLP, prohibit the seller/affiliates claiming the premises use that solar electricity or its emissions reductions, and require payment of gross electricity consumption. Therefore do not add retail bill savings to FiT income or claim tenant Scope 2 cuts from the same sold attributes. [FiT terms](https://www.clp.com.hk/content/dam/clphk/documents/fit/Terms%20and%20Conditions%20%28Eng%29_10%20Jun%202023.pdf), accessed 8 Nov 2026. This settles two items previously marked unverified in the old brief.

Hypothetical per-1-kWp project installed at start **2027**: cost HK$20,000, 1,000 kWh year-1 yield, 0.5% annual degradation, HK$200/year O&M, HK$4/kWh FiT scenario. With seven annual payments through 2033, zero later cash or residual credit, NPV **+HK$1,666**, maximum break-even capex **HK$21,666**. With only three enforceable roof/lease years and no compensation on departure, NPV **−HK$9,704**; capex ceiling **HK$10,296**. Quotes, yield and right-to-income are unverified. Future post-2033 self-consumption is possible but must be modelled separately under its eventual terms; no extension is assumed. A flat owner cannot presume unilateral roof access, but “solar is impossible” overstates the evidence: consented landlord/OC arrangements can exist. Roof allocation, structure, wind loading, fire access, waterproofing, insurance and end-of-lease obligations need professional review.

## How can lease terms make the decisions implementable and measurable?

### Takeaway

A green lease is useful if it allocates actual costs, rights, measurements and exit consequences. A long term, reinstatement waiver or savings cap may improve economics, but none makes an uneconomic project profitable automatically. Preserve flexibility and avoid committing this SME to a 2039 warehouse merely to support a green story.

### Cited findings

HKGBC's Hong Kong Green Office Guide pp130–132 discusses metering/consumption records, agreed methodologies, audit access, cost sharing, longer terms to support investment and flexibility around reinstatement. These are Hong Kong guidance examples, not mandatory clauses or evidence of warehouse savings. The guide references the 2014 Green Tenancy Driver and 2015 material; its exact cover publication date was not established. It cites overseas office savings that must not be transplanted into Carmen's warehouse. [Guide PDF](https://www.hkgbc.org.hk/eng/resources/publications/HKGBC-Publication/Guidebook/Hong_Kong_Green_Office_Guide_English_Optimised.pdf), accessed 8 Nov 2026. A standalone 2014 guide landing page was found in search but returned 404 on opening, so it is not relied on for contractual detail.

### Inferences and recommended project methods

**Draft commercial requirements for legal review (our design):** obtain consent for specified equipment and installation; define ownership of improvements, meters/data, approved access and tenant operational control; fix a 12-month weather/activity-adjusted baseline; establish who receives grants and prevents duplicate recovery; pay any landlord recovery only from independently supported tenant net savings, with annual true-up and dispute mechanism; separate maintenance from capex charges; waive unnecessary reinstatement for accepted improvements; state buyout/unamortised-cost compensation on landlord break, assignment or sale; allow removal of portable equipment where lawful. Add roof/solar rights only if actually available, with FiT account/attributes/disclosure terms explicit. Check the lease's repair, access, damage, rent-abatement and termination wording with the risk researcher. Make any renewal option/assignment right enforceable and priced; include appropriate break flexibility. Do not promise a 2039 renewal or a valuation premium without the landlord and buyer.

**Savings-sharing feasibility test:** hypothetical landlord capex HK$50,000; annual tenant net saving HK$7,000; landlord takes 70% (HK$4,900/year), tenant keeps HK$2,100. Over five years at 5%, landlord NPV is **−HK$28,786**, tenant NPV **+HK$9,092**. Landlord would need HK$11,549/year to recover capex over five years, more than all savings. A cap can protect the tenant while making the owner's investment unattractive. Resolve via lower cost, genuine grant, longer independently valuable term or documented residual/other benefits; otherwise refuse the project. Calling it a green lease does not solve this arithmetic.

**Priorities and ownership:** Fahtai/Carmen first obtain the bills/lease/equipment audit, then request itemised service-equivalent quotations and written grant checks. An engineer/electrician verifies savings and permissions; licensed advisers/solicitor check finance/lease. Fahtai replaces the old area-based warehouse calculation with the circuit-based sensitivity above. Pete/model owner alone decides whether validated project cash flows enter the shared model; use only Carmen's attributable distributions and avoid sale-value double counts. **Strongest counterargument:** energy-only NPV misses comfort, worker safety, customer requirements, mandatory works and willingness to pay for values. This worries me moderately: a financially small project can still be suitable. The solution is to show an explicit non-financial objective and cap the cost, not label speculative savings as verified. A project can be acceptable with negative energy NPV if the decision-makers knowingly fund the difference.

**What would change these conclusions:** measured high cooling/lighting use or firm ordinary-versus-efficient quotes could favour early replacement; enforceable roof rights, a long enough useful lease and a competitive solar quote could favour solar; a named bank/customer benefit may justify certification; a lower installed cost or grant may make savings sharing bankable. None is yet observed for this family.
