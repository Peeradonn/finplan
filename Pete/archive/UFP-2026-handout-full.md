## 1A. Strategy: thesis, tone and signature innovations

### Why both rigor and innovation
- **The case asks for both.** It wants a plan that is "innovative yet prudent, forward-looking yet practical" (case p.5)
  and lists "client suitability awareness, innovation, implementation practicality" together (Note 4).
- **The rounds weight them differently.** Round One scoring is mostly rigor and clarity. The Final (70% of the final
  ranking) gives **40 of 100** points to innovation and independent thinking.
- **The judges are mixed:** a fintech/Web3 case author, ESG strategists, and practising advisers.

So: **a prudent core with an innovative edge.** Put the innovation in the *method and the vehicle*, never in the *size
of the risk*.

### Thesis: "Built to Last"
> *The Wongs have already done the hard part: a HK$24.4M balance sheet, over 40% of after-tax income saved each year
> (parents only, HK$754K of HK$1.74M), and only 10% leverage. Our plan makes it last, for four people with four
> different views of risk.*

(Use the restated 43% after-tax figure, not the workshop's pre-tax "55%", so the thesis agrees with §2.)

Three pillars. Every section and recommendation hangs from one of them:

| Pillar | Meaning | Sections |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Protect what has been built** | Close the protection gaps; wills, EPAs, advance directives; liquidity that survives a death | §2, §8, §9 |
| **2. Turn assets into lifelong income** | A retirement income floor, the property option and Carmen's business transition | §3, §9 |
| **3. Give every voice a place** | Adrian's security, Carmen's values, Ryan's conviction and Chloe's choices, each with its own structure | §4–§7, Ryan's plan |

### Tone rules (the plan is read by the client first, the judges second)
- **Lead with strengths, then next steps.** Say "most of the family's wealth is invested in their home and Carmen's
  business, both valuable and neither easy to turn into income without a plan". Do not write "61% concentrated".
- **Put facts in numbers, not alarm words.** Give risks their full numbers in §2 and §11 (15 points for risk analysis),
  but in neutral language. Avoid "problem", "worrying" and "poor" in headings.
- **Treat digital assets proportionately.** HK$500K is about **2% of family net worth** and part of it is a tokenised
  money-market fund (cash-like). It is Ryan's conviction, and it gets structure (form, size, controls), not a warning.
  This is what the workshop asked for: "not just yes or no".

### Signature innovations: four in the proposal, two supporting
Each passes four tests: it answers a stated family concern, suits the specific member, can be done in HK today
through a regulated channel, and is quantified.

#### ① A lifetime income floor: "the essentials are paid for life, whatever markets do" (§3; pillar 2)
- **What:** separate retirement spending into *essential* and *discretionary*. Fund essentials, as far as practical,
  from **guaranteed lifetime income**:
  - MPF partly annuitised through the **HKMC Annuity Plan** (at 65: M 6.9% / F 6.3% a year for life; age 60+;
    up to HK$5M each);
  - optionally, a **reverse mortgage** on the flat, keeping the family in the home;
  - the **Silver Bond** for Adrian from about 2032.

  The portfolio funds discretionary spending.
- **Why it suits the client:** it answers Adrian's "capital preservation, retirement adequacy" directly and removes
  longevity risk from the essentials. This is the floor-and-upside approach used in retirement-income planning.
- **How big is the floor? Derived from data, not a rule of thumb.**
  - The benchmark is the C&SD **2024/25 Household Expenditure Survey** (released 9 Jun 2026). Its highest expenditure
    quartile averages **HK$65,210 a month, about HK$782K a year**, almost exactly the Wongs' HK$780K target.
  - Restate that group's spending on a cash basis for a retired, mortgage-free owner: remove imputed rent (group 23),
    education (67) and textbooks (54). That leaves HK$38,743 a month, which splits into three tiers:

    | Tier | Share | HES groups |
    |---|---|---|
    | **Essential** (needs) | **36.9%** | Basic food, management fees, utilities, medicines, health services, communications, public transport, personal care |
    | **Lifestyle** (wants) | 45.0% | Meals out, domestic help, car running costs, clothing, durable goods |
    | **Discretionary** (wishes) | 18.2% | Holidays, travel fares, entertainment, jewellery, vehicle purchases, other services |

  - Add the owner's **rates and government rent** (≈ HK$27.6K a year, estimated at 5% + 3% of a rateable value of about
    3% of HK$11.5M). The floor becomes **≈ 40% of HK$780K = HK$312K a year in today's money**, about HK$430K nominal in
    2039.
  - **Caveat:** in a real engagement this comes from a needs/wants/wishes budget built with the family. HES is the
    best available proxy, so state it as one.
- **What funds the floor (Fahtai to model):**
  - **MPF annuitised through the HKMC Annuity Plan.** Projected MPF is ≈ HK$2.31M for Adrian at 65 (2037) and
    ≈ HK$1.81M for Carmen at 62 (2039), at 5% net plus mandatory contributions. Annuitised at 6.9% (M, 65) and ≈6.0%
    (F, 62, interpolated), that gives ≈ **HK$268K a year** for life.
  - **Optional reverse mortgage** from 2037, on the flat's capped value (≈ HK$10.8M if it grows 1.5% a year), paid for
    life, based on the **younger borrower's** age (Carmen, 60). At about HK$2,000 per HK$1M a month (single-borrower
    rate; joint rates are lower), that is ≈ HK$230–260K a year.
  - **Silver Bonds** (CPI-linked, with a minimum rate) as available: the only inflation-linked piece of the floor.

  | If the floor is… | MPF annuity alone covers | MPF annuity + reverse mortgage |
  |---|---|---|
  | **40% (HES-derived base)** | **62%** | **116–123%** |
  | 60% (sensitivity) | 41% | 77–82% |
  | 80% (needs + most wants) | 31% | 58–61% |

  **Sizing rule, so the amount is not arbitrary:** annuitise only as much as is needed to close the floor gap left
  after other guaranteed income. Annuities cannot be reversed, so keep the rest liquid for flexibility and legacy.
- **Be honest about the limits:** annuity and reverse-mortgage payments are **fixed in nominal terms**. At 2.5%
  inflation their real value falls ~45% over 24 years, so the portfolio (②) still matters, and the Silver Bond is the
  inflation-linked part. The reverse mortgage is an **option the family holds**, decided at 65 against downsizing
  (Lookbua's NPV), not a commitment now.
- **Risk required (supports the Low–Medium stance):** a rough real-terms check puts the parents' liquid assets and
  MPF, less a conservative overseas education reserve, plus HK$718K a year of surplus, at ≈ HK$17.4M by 2037 at a
  **2% real return**. That matches the ≈ HK$17.0M needed. **The family needs only about a 2% real return (≈ 4.5%
  nominal)**, before counting the flat or the business. They do not need to take much risk to meet their goals: a
  strong, reassuring message for Adrian. Fahtai to confirm in the full model.

#### ② A family Investment Policy Statement with spending guardrails and pre-agreed decision rules (§3, §5, §11; pillar 3)
- **What:** a one-page policy the whole family signs, containing:
  - **Guardrails on discretionary spending**, from Guyton & Klinger (2006, *Journal of Financial Planning*):
    - no inflation increase after a year of negative returns;
    - if the withdrawal rate rises >20% above its starting level, cut discretionary spending 10%;
    - if it falls >20% below, raise it 10%.

    The floor in ① is never touched.
  - **Rebalancing bands:** ±5pp around each target weight.
  - **Pre-agreed triggers:**
    - an equity fall of 20% means rebalance, not sell;
    - Chloe accepting an offer means convert the first two years' fees to that currency the same month;
    - a death, disability, business sale, or a rate move of more than 1.5pp means a full review.
  - An **annual family review**.
- **Why it suits the client:** it turns the household's differing risk appetites (Low–Medium / Medium–High / High)
  from a source of conflict into agreed rules made *before* markets move. It also raises sustainable spending
  compared with a fixed HK$780K.
- **Evidence:** Guyton & Klinger (FPA Journal, 2006). An IPS is standard CFA/CFP practice. Monte Carlo shows the
  success probability with and without guardrails.

#### ③ Conviction with structure: a digital-asset framework per person (§7, §9; pillar 3)
- **Classify by economic exposure, not by wrapper.** The tokenised MMF inside the HK$500K is **cash**. Only BTC, ETH
  and the VA spot ETFs carry crypto price risk. Get the split.
- **Ryan has his own digital-asset policy:**
  - Today his crypto is roughly **74% of his known investable wealth** (HK$500K of HK$680K with his MPF).
  - **Glide path:** ≤35% by age 30, ≤20% by 35, reached mainly by **directing new savings elsewhere, not by forced
    selling.**
  - **Why a 30% savings rate:** his cash income is ≈ HK$277K (after tax and MPF). If he lives at home, HES data for
    4-person households gives personal consumption of ≈ HK$6.7K a month excluding housing, ≈ HK$80K a year. After a
    household contribution and his own new insurance premiums, he can plausibly save **~40% of gross**. We plan on
    **30% (HK$90K)** as a conservative base. It is his decision, so show the sensitivity:

    | Ryan saves (of gross) | Crypto share at 30 | at 35 |
    |---|---|---|
    | 20% (HK$60K) | 37% | 24% |
    | **30% (HK$90K), base** | **32%** | **20%** |
    | 40% (HK$120K) | 28% | 17% |
    | 50% (HK$150K) | 25% | 15% |

    Even at 20%, no forced selling is needed; he just reaches 20% a few years later.
  - **The tokenised-MMF split matters:** if 20% of the HK$500K is the tokenised MMF, his real crypto exposure today is
    59%, not 74%. At 40%, it is 44%. The glide path above assumes all HK$500K is crypto, the conservative case.
  - No new money goes into digital assets while he is above the path.
  - **Trim only when a rally pushes him >10pp above the path**, which locks in gains, never panic-sells.
- **Custody:** hold only through the **13 SFC-licensed trading platforms** (as at Aug 2026) or **HKEX-listed spot
  BTC/ETH ETFs**. Any self-custody needs a documented key-recovery arrangement in his estate papers: **no key, no
  inheritance**.
- **Parents:** Adrian **0%** (Low–Medium). Carmen *optional* **≤2% of her own sleeve**, through HKEX spot ETFs only.
  That addresses her stated worries (fraud, custody, valuation) through the wrapper, while the size limit contains the
  volatility.
- **Why it suits the client:** it respects Ryan's High profile and conviction rather than lecturing him, matches
  each member's suitability, and uses only regulated channels. It is also what the case author asked for.

#### ④ Carmen's business: from key person to planned transition (§8, §9; pillar 2)
- **Now:**
  - a shareholders' agreement with her co-founder;
  - a **buy-sell agreement funded by insurance** on each founder (so her family receives value, and the business
    survives, if she dies or is disabled);
  - key-person cover;
  - confirm, and plan to release, any personal guarantee on the HK$0.9M loan.
- **Ages 55–58:** build the management layer and document the business's processes.
- **Ages 58–62:** staged sale (e.g. a tranche at 58 and the balance, or a retained minority for dividends, at 62) to
  the co-founder, management or a strategic buyer. HK does not tax capital gains on the sale.
- **Salary/dividend mix:** optimise only after her disability cover is in place (§6.2).
- **Planning value:** realisable ≈ HK$3.0–3.5M after a 30–40% illiquidity haircut. Treated as a **buffer, not core
  retirement funding**.
- **Why it suits the client:** it answers concern 7 directly, removes the key-person and concentration risk together,
  and matches her retirement date.

#### Supporting ⑤: family governance (§9)
- A one-page **family charter** (values, how decisions are made, principles for education support, inheritance at
  staged ages) and an **annual family meeting** held after the review in ②.
- **Matched saving for Ryan:** parents add 50 cents per dollar Ryan saves, up to HK$24K a year (≈3% of the parents'
  HK$718K surplus). A behavioural nudge for goal 3 that builds his independence rather than replacing it.

#### Supporting ⑥: a scholarship that carries the family's values (§9)
- A named **sustainable-design scholarship** of about **HK$30K a year**, given through an existing s.88 charity (for
  example a named fund at a university), not a private foundation.
- Donations to s.88 charities are deductible up to **35% of assessable income**. At a 17% marginal rate the net cost is
  ≈ HK$25K a year.
- It links Carmen's sustainable-packaging mission, Chloe's interest in design and the family's ESG goal.
- Ryan and Chloe (from 18) sit on a small **giving committee**, which trains the next generation in stewardship. The
  wills can add an optional legacy gift later.

### Considered and rejected (say so briefly; judges reward the judgement)
- **Government tokenised green bonds: not investable for the family yet.** All three issues (2023, 2024, and HK$10bn
  in Nov 2025) went to institutions. The government's LegCo reply of 4 Feb 2026 says the HKMA is "assessing whether
  the current form of tokenised bonds can meet the needs of retail investors". Put them on a **watch-list** with a
  review trigger.
  - **What retail investors *can* hold:** SFC-authorised **tokenised funds** (13 products as of Mar 2026, mostly
    money-market funds; Ryan's tokenised MMF is likely one of them). Since the SFC's **20 Apr 2026 circulars** they can
    also be traded on licensed trading platforms.
  - For green fixed income today, use **SFC-authorised green/ESG bond funds** (Win's list). HK retail green bonds
    (2022, 2023) are not tokenised; the last matures in Oct 2026 and no new retail issue has been announced.
- **Increasing digital-asset exposure:** fails suitability for the household objective of "moderate growth with
  controlled downside".
- **A private trust company or family office:** disproportionate cost at HK$24M. Will-based trusts do the job.
- **Assuming any ESG return premium:** not evidenced. ESG funds are modelled at their asset-class returns.

### Process check: are we following the professional sequence?
The international standard is the **FPSB six-step financial planning process** (FPSB sets the CFP standard; IFPHK is
its Hong Kong member, and ISO 22222 is aligned with it). The judges come from a financial planning society and will
recognise it, so **structure the proposal around it**.

| FPSB step | What a world-class planner does | Where we are | Rubric link |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Establish and define the relationship** | Scope, who the client is, joint-client duties, limitations | Joint-client duty noted (§6.4). **Missing:** a 3-line scope statement: household advice plus Ryan's own plan, assumptions, limitations | Presentation |
| **2. Collect information** (numbers **and** values) | Fact-find; a data-gap register; attitudes and values | Case data captured; gaps logged (ownership, salary/dividend, tokenised-MMF split). **Missing:** a one-page client profile of each member's values and priorities | Client information (15) |
| **3. Analyse and assess** | **Status-quo projection first** ("if nothing changes"); goal feasibility; risk **tolerance vs capacity vs need** | Pieces done (tax, intestacy, assumptions, risk-need check above). **Missing: the full baseline projection** | Client information (15), Goals (10), Risk (15) |
| **4. Develop and present recommendations** | Alternatives considered → recommendation → quantified improvement over baseline | Innovations chosen as **hypotheses**. They must be *validated* against the baseline; any that don't improve outcomes are dropped | Financial proposal (25) |
| **5. Implement** | Sequenced actions, owners, products, dates | §10 roadmap (Lookbua) | Financial proposal |
| **6. Monitor and review** | Review cadence, triggers, rebalancing | Covered by innovation ② (IPS, guardrails, triggers) | Risk analysis |

**What to fix in our order of work:**
1. **Baseline before recommendations.** Fahtai runs the *status-quo* projection first (by 25 Sep): cash flow, net
   worth, retirement success probability, education funding, protection gap, estate outcome, all with no changes.
2. **Goals: feasibility and priority (10 points).** Classify each goal as a need, want or wish; test feasibility
   against the baseline; state trade-offs. For example, overseas education at the top of the range versus the
   retirement floor.
3. **Risk profile has three parts, not one.** *Tolerance* is given (Adrian L–M, Carmen M–H, Ryan H). *Capacity* is high
   (strong surplus, low leverage). *Need* is low (≈ 2% real, above). The recommended risk is the **lowest of the
   three**, which is why the allocation is moderate even though Carmen and Ryan could tolerate more. This is the core
   suitability argument.
4. **Show the value of the advice.** A step chart from baseline to recommended plan: retirement success probability
   and legacy value, adding one change at a time (tax election → protection → IPS/guardrails → income floor →
   business transition). This is the single most persuasive chart for judges, and it proves the innovations earn
   their place.

## 2. Team structure

Organised as an advisory firm — by role in the planning process, **not by asset class**.
Splitting by asset class fails because the same holding serves multiple goals, and nobody
ends up owning the overall allocation.

| Member | Role | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| **[You]** — accounting & finance, AI/tech literate | Lead Advisor & Engagement Partner | Executive summary · Cash flow & net worth (incl. restated balance sheet + tax) · Retirement · Education · Succession & legacy · Ryan's standalone plan · Final integration |
| **Win** — financial technology | Investment Strategist & Digital/ESG Specialist | Investment allocation & buckets · ESG framework · Digital asset recommendation · Regulatory limb of risk & compliance |
| **Fahtai** — data science & CS | Head of Quant & Analytics | The model · tax engine · Monte Carlo · sensitivity analysis · ESG screening data · all charts |
| **Lookbua** — engineering | Chief Risk Officer & Delivery Lead | Risk management & protection · property decision (NPV) · implementation roadmap · document production, layout, 15-page budget, anonymity check |

### Three single-owner rules
1. **One assumptions file** — frozen after the lock meeting. Nobody uses a number not on it.
2. **One model** — Fahtai owns it. Others *request* numbers; they do not build side spreadsheets.
3. **One allocation table** — Win owns it. The only place asset-class decisions are made.

### Critical hand-off
**Lead Advisor → Win: the bucket sizes** (how much each goal needs, and when).
If this slips, the investment section stalls. Schedule it no later than **28 September**.

### Four-eyes review (before submission)
- Lead Advisor checks financial logic in Win's and Lookbua's sections
- Win checks regulatory and product statements in the Lead Advisor's sections
- Fahtai checks every number in the text against the model
- Lookbua checks formatting, consistency, page count, anonymity

### 6.2 **KEY FINDING — separate taxation beats joint assessment by exactly HK$18,000**

Recomputed on the ownership assumption in §5: the flat is held as joint tenants, so under separate taxation each
spouse deducts half the home-loan interest. Reproduce this in Fahtai's tax engine.

| | Joint assessment | Separate taxation |
|---|---|---|
| Adrian | — | HK$110,435 |
| Carmen | — | HK$71,335 |
| **Couple** | **HK$199,770** | **HK$181,770** |
| Ryan | HK$8,000 | HK$8,000 |
| **Household total** | HK$207,770 | **HK$189,770** |

**Why exactly HK$18,000:** the married person's allowance (HK$290,000) is exactly two basic allowances
(2 × HK$145,000), so joint assessment gains nothing on allowances. It does force the couple through *one* set of
progressive bands instead of two. Each set taxes the first HK$200,000 at HK$16,000 instead of 17% × 200,000 =
HK$34,000. Losing one set therefore costs 34,000 − 16,000 = **HK$18,000**. The result does not depend on how deductions
are split between the spouses, as long as both stay in the 17% marginal band. Joint assessment only wins where one
spouse cannot absorb their own allowances.

**Working (Adrian, separate):**
- Salary 1,050,000 + bonus 150,000 = 1,200,000
- Less MPF mandatory 18,000 and half the home-loan interest 31,500 (1.8M × 3.5% ÷ 2) = 1,150,500
- Less allowances: basic 145,000 + child 140,000 + dependent parents 110,000 = 395,000
- NCI = 755,500 → tax = 16,000 + (17% × 555,500) = **110,435**
- Standard-rate check: 15% × 1,150,500 = 172,575 → progressive is lower ✓

**Working (Carmen, separate):**
- 720,000 less MPF 18,000 and home-loan interest 31,500 = 670,500; less basic 145,000 = NCI 525,500
- Tax = 16,000 + (17% × 325,500) = **71,335**
- Standard-rate check: 15% × 670,500 = 100,575 → progressive is lower ✓

**Working (joint):** 1,920,000 − MPF 36,000 − home-loan interest 63,000 = 1,821,000; less married 290,000 + child
140,000 + dependent parents 110,000 = NCI 1,281,000 → 16,000 + 17% × 1,081,000 = **199,770**.

**Ryan:** 300,000 less MPF 15,000 (5% × HK$25,000 × 12) less basic 145,000 = NCI 140,000 → 1,000 + 3,000 + 10% × 40,000 = **8,000**.

**Assumptions to state in the proposal:**
- **Dependent parents:** HK$110,000 = two parents aged 60+ (2 × HK$55,000), all claimed by Adrian. The case says only
  "support for elderly parents", so state the number and ages.
- **Carmen's salary/dividend split:** the base case treats all HK$720,000 as salary, the conservative choice. A dividend

### 6.4 Succession and legacy

#### Intestacy — run the numbers, don't just warn

Under **s.4, Intestates' Estates Ordinance (Cap. 73)**, where the deceased leaves a spouse and
children, the surviving spouse takes:
1. all personal chattels;
2. a statutory legacy of **HK$500,000** (with interest from date of death); and
3. **half** the residuary estate.

The children divide the other half equally, on statutory trusts.

**Correction:** an earlier draft applied the formula to HK$24.4M. That is the **whole family's** net worth, not
Adrian's estate. It includes Carmen's business and MPF, Ryan's MPF and crypto, and a flat that passes outside the
estate. Recomputed on the ownership assumption in §5:

**Scenario A: Adrian dies first.**
- The flat (HK$11.5M) passes to Carmen **by survivorship** as joint tenant, outside the estate.
- His HK$2.0M whole-life benefit is paid to Carmen as named beneficiary, also outside the estate.
- His estate is therefore: his 50% of non-MPF financial assets, ½ × (2.5M liquid + 5.2M funds, stocks and bonds) =
  3.85M; plus his MPF 1.05M (paid to the estate); plus the car 0.22M; less half the card balance 0.04M.
  Total **≈ HK$5.1M**.

| Beneficiary | Intestacy entitlement (s.4, Cap. 73) |
|---|---|
| Carmen | car (personal chattel) 0.22M + statutory legacy 0.5M + half the residue 2.18M ≈ **HK$2.9M**, plus the flat and insurance outside the estate |
| Ryan (24) | ≈ **HK$1.09M** |
| Chloe (16) | ≈ **HK$1.09M**, on statutory trust until 18, **two years away** |

**Scenario B: both parents die (e.g. a common accident). This is the stronger argument.**
Everything except Ryan's own HK$0.68M (his MPF and digital assets) passes to the two children in equal shares: about
**HK$11.9M each**, with Carmen's business shares included and no one named to run it.
- **Route of the flat:** where the order of deaths is uncertain, the younger is presumed to have survived the elder
  (Conveyancing and Property Ordinance, Cap. 219, s.11). Carmen (49) is presumed to survive Adrian (54), so the flat
  passes by survivorship **into Carmen's estate** and is exposed to her creditors before reaching the children. In
  *Ting Kam Yuen v Cheung Wing Kin* [2011] HKEC 1151, jointly held properties passed into the estate of the spouse
  found to have survived and became available to his creditors. This matters if Carmen has guaranteed the HK$0.9M
  business loan.
- Chloe's share is held on statutory trust only until 18.
- **No testamentary guardian is appointed for a 16-year-old.** Guardianship of Minors Ordinance (Cap. 13),
  **s.6(1)–(2)**, lets a parent appoint a guardian by deed or will to act after their death. A surviving parent can
  object, and disputes go to court under s.6(3) (CLIC; Law Reform Commission, *Guardianship of Children*).

**That is the argument for wills, stated in numbers.** Scenario A shows the spouse receives far less than the family
assumes. Scenario B shows an unstructured HK$12M inheritance to a 24-year-old just starting his career and to an
18-year-old, a business with no successor, and a minor with no guardian.

Also note the **hotchpot** provision (s.5(1)(c)): lifetime advances to children may be brought into account.

#### Ownership: assume, test, then design

The case never states title. Treat ownership in two parts:
1. **Current ownership** is a *fact we lack*. Assume the most likely arrangement (not the most convenient), state it,
   and make verifying it Action 1 of the roadmap: a Land Registry title search, account mandates, policy beneficiary
   nominations, the company's shareholder register, and any personal guarantee on the business loan.
2. **Target ownership** is a *decision we advise on*. Recommend a structure that works whatever today's title turns
   out to be.

**Step 1: which conclusions depend on ownership?**

| Conclusion | Depends on ownership? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Separate taxation saves HK$18,000 | No | Proven for any split while both spouses are in the 17% band (§6.2) |
| Couple's total salaries tax | No | The home-loan interest split moves tax between spouses, not the total |
| Tax on investments | No | HK does not tax dividends, interest or capital gains, so investment title has no tax effect |
| Net worth, surplus, retirement adequacy | No | Measured at household level |
| **Outcome on a death** | **Yes, heavily** | See Step 2 |
| **Cash available to the survivor** | **Yes** | Sole-name accounts are frozen until the grant; MPF has no beneficiary nomination and is paid to the personal representative |
| **Ability to sell, downsize or take a reverse mortgage** | **Yes** | One co-owner losing mental capacity blocks dealings without an EPA |
| **Exposure to business creditors** | **Yes** | Depends on title and on whether Carmen has guaranteed the loan |

**Step 2: Adrian dies first without a will, under each title**

| Title to the flat | Carmen receives | Each child receives | Problem |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Joint tenants** (base case) | The whole flat outside the estate, plus ≈ HK$2.9M from the estate | ≈ HK$1.1M | Little; the home is secure |
| **Tenants in common, 50:50** | ≈ HK$5.3M in total from the estate, including his half of the flat | ≈ HK$2.3M | The children (one a minor on statutory trust) hold part of the family home |
| **Adrian's sole name** | ≈ HK$7.75M from a ≈ HK$14.8M estate | ≈ HK$3.5M | Carmen can take the home under **s.7, Cap. 73** only by paying ≈ **HK$1.95M** in cash for the shortfall against the HK$9.7M net equity |

Only joint tenancy leaves the surviving spouse secure in the home. That is why it is the base assumption (and the
usual arrangement for a married couple's home).

**Step 3: stress-test joint tenancy against events**

| Event | What happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Both die together | Cap. 219 s.11: the flat routes through Carmen's estate (creditor exposure), then to the children with no structure | Mirror wills: a trust for the children with staged ages (e.g. 25/30/35), and a named guardian for Chloe (Cap. 13 s.6) |
| Carmen's business fails | A guaranteed HK$0.9M loan becomes a claim on her assets | Confirm any guarantee. Never use the home as security for the business. Plan the guarantee's release |
| Either spouse loses mental capacity | The other cannot sell, downsize or take a reverse mortgage alone; the fallback is a court application under the Mental Health Ordinance | **EPAs for both, now**. This is a precondition for the §9 property decision |
| First death | Sole-name accounts and MPF are frozen until the grant | A joint account holding ~12 months' spending (≈ HK$1M). Both life policies to name the spouse |
| Survivor remarries | The flat belongs wholly to the survivor; the children's inheritance depends on the survivor's later will | Values choice, below |
| Chloe under 18 | Anything she inherits sits on statutory trust until 18, then passes outright | Testamentary trust with staged ages |

**Step 4: target structure (default recommendation)**
- The home held as joint tenants.
- Mirror wills with a children's trust and a guardian for Chloe.
- EPAs and advance directives for both spouses.
- A joint account holding the emergency reserve.
- Beneficiary nominations checked on both policies.
- The business ring-fenced from the home.
- Reviews at Adrian's retirement, at any downsizing or move, and when Chloe turns 25.

**Values choice for the family (not for us to decide):** if protecting the children's inheritance against remarriage
matters more than simplicity, convert to **tenants in common**. Each half then passes by will into a trust giving the
surviving spouse a **right to live in the flat for life**, with the remainder to the children. The costs are probate
on each half and more complexity. Present both, with the default above as our recommendation.

**Joint-client duty (for §11):** we advise both spouses at once, and on ownership their interests can diverge. The
recommendations are therefore **symmetric** (mirror wills, both EPAs, equal liquidity), and we note that each spouse
may take separate legal advice before signing.

**Text for the proposal's assumptions box:**

> Adrian and Carmen as joint tenants, financial assets are split equally in individual names, policy death benefits

#### Three documents, three distinct jobs

| Document | Covers | Key mechanics |
|---|---|---|
| **Will** | Distribution of the estate | Without it, the s.4 formula above applies. Wills Ordinance (Cap. 30) |
| **Enduring Power of Attorney** | **Property and financial affairs only** — cannot delegate health care decisions | EPAO (Cap. 501). Must be signed before a solicitor **and** a registered medical practitioner, both present at the same time. Must be registered with the Registrar of the High Court once the donor is or is becoming mentally incapable |
| **Advance Medical Directive** | Refusal of specified life-sustaining treatment | See below — **new statute** |

#### The currency hook — AMD Ordinance

The **Advance Decision on Life-sustaining Treatment Ordinance (Cap. 651)** came into force on
**31 July 2026**. Passed by LegCo 20 November 2024; commencement notice gazetted 22 May 2026 after
an ~18-month preparatory period.

It gives statutory effect to advance medical directives and do-not-attempt CPR orders, allowing an
adult with an advanced and irreversible disease — while mentally capable — to refuse specified
life-sustaining treatments (e.g. CPR, artificial ventilation, artificial nutrition and hydration)
in advance. It does **not** permit refusal of basic or palliative care. It also protects healthcare
professionals acting on a valid directive, under an "if in doubt, save lives first" principle.

**Why this matters:** citing a statute that commenced eight weeks ago demonstrates current,
practising-level knowledge. The case explicitly notes the family has no advance healthcare
directives. Most teams will write "consider an advance directive" with no legal grounding.

#### Estate duty
Hong Kong abolished estate duty for deaths on or after **11 February 2006** (Revenue (Abolition of Estate Duty)
Ordinance 2005; confirmed via IRD and CLIC). The succession case therefore rests on
**control, liquidity, staging and family governance** — not tax. Say so explicitly; it distinguishes
a HK-literate proposal from one that imports UK/US inheritance-tax thinking.

#### Digital asset succession — bridge to Win's section
If Ryan self-custodies any of his HK$500,000, then **no private key means no inheritance**,
regardless of what any will says. Key recovery, custodial arrangements and executor access must be
addressed in the legacy section. Few teams will connect §7 (digital assets) to §9 (succession).
**This is a differentiator — coordinate with Win.**

### 6.5 Retirement — HKMC gives you three real instruments

**"HKMC Retire 3"** comprises:
1. **Reverse Mortgage Programme (RMP)** — operated by HKMC Insurance Ltd
2. **Policy Reverse Mortgage Programme (PRMP)** — uses life policies as security
3. **HKMC Annuity Plan** — underwritten by HKMC Annuity Ltd

**RMP key features:**
- Eligibility from **age 55** using a HK residential property as security
- Monthly payouts over a fixed 10, 15 or 20 years, **or for life**
- Lump-sum payouts available at application or during the term
- **No repayment during the borrower's lifetime**
- **Lifetime right to stay in the property**, even after the payment term ends
- Property may be rented out under specific conditions (e.g. after declared retirement)
- HKMC cut the RMP fixed rate to **4% p.a. for the first 30 years, then Prime − 2%**, and raised payouts by up to
  5%, funded by social bond proceeds (HKMA, 9 Mar 2026; confirmed on hkmc.com.hk)
- **Valuation cap:** payouts are based on 100% of value up to HK$8M plus 50% of value above it. The Wongs' HK$11.5M flat
  therefore counts as **HK$9.75M**. Costs: upfront mortgage insurance of 1.96% of the specified property value
  (spread over 7 years), plus 1.25% a year on the outstanding loan. **Lookbua's NPV must use these figures, and must
  confirm how the existing HK$1.8M mortgage is treated** (e.g. repaid from the RMP lump sum)

**Why this is your best answer to the property question:** the case asks retain / downsize /
relocate. The RMP is a **fourth option** — retain the Tai Kok Tsui flat, keep living in it, and
convert part of its HK$11.5M value into lifetime income without selling or moving. Adrian is
already 55 and eligible today.

The **PRMP** is separately relevant given their HK$420,000 of life policy cash value.
The **HKMC Annuity Plan** converts a lump sum (including withdrawn MPF benefits) into guaranteed
lifetime income. It is the natural home for part of the MPF at 65 and addresses longevity risk directly.
- Eligibility: age 60+, HK$50K–5M premium per person.
- Guaranteed monthly payout per HK$1M (hkmca.hk):

  | Entry age | Male | Female |
  |---|---|---|
  | 60 | HK$5,100 (6.1%) | HK$4,700 (5.6%) |
  | 65 | HK$5,800 (6.9%) | HK$5,300 (6.3%) |
  | 70 | HK$6,560 (7.8%) | HK$5,840 (7.0%) |

  Worked example: annuitising HK$1M of Adrian's MPF at 65 gives HK$69,600 a year for life, about 9% of the HK$780K
  target in today's money, before inflation erodes it. The payment is fixed, so the annuity hedges longevity, not
  inflation. Say so in the proposal.

The **Silver Bond** (2026 issue: 3-year, minimum 4.25%, open to those aged 60+) is also relevant to the retirement
bucket. Adrian qualifies for issues from about 2032, if the programme continues.

## 10. Model specification (Fahtai)

Excel core (transparent, checkable by the finance side) + Python for Monte Carlo.

- **Inputs sheet** — every assumption in one place, nothing hard-coded elsewhere. Scenario switch:
  base / stress / optimistic
- **Timeline** — annual rows 2026 → ~2066; each member's age in its own column so events trigger
  automatically (retirement at 65 / 62, Chloe at 18–21, deaths at assumed ages)
- **Cash flow waterfall** — gross income → tax → household expenses → mortgage → education reserve
  → emergency reserve top-up → invest surplus. *The ordering encodes your priorities and is itself
  a planning statement.*
- **Tax module** — salaries tax per §6.1, progressive vs standard comparison, separate vs joint
  election, all deductions; profits tax if modelled separately
- **Asset buckets** — cash / bonds / equities / REITs / digital / MPF per person, rolled forward with

  mortgage payoff

- **Sensitivity** — tornado chart: which assumption moves the retirement outcome most
- **Monte Carlo** — 5,000 paths, correlated lognormal returns, annual rebalancing rule applied.

## 11. Differentiators — the arguments most teams will miss

1. **Separate taxation saves exactly HK$18,000/year** (§6.2), the value of one set of progressive bands. Quantified, actionable, verifiable
2. **Local tuition is HK$47,000, not HK$150–250K** (§6.3) — decompose the case's own figure
3. **Restated investable pool** (~HK$8.2M, not HK$10.47M) — MPF and policy cash values aren't
   freely allocable
4. **Restated surplus** (~HK$700K, not HK$1.3M) — after tax, excluding Ryan's income
5. **Intestacy modelled in numbers, under every possible title.** If Adrian dies, Carmen's estate share is only
   ~HK$2.9M, and if he owned the flat alone she would need ~HK$1.95M in cash to keep her home. If both parents die,
   the flat routes through Carmen's estate (Cap. 219 s.11), and Ryan and Chloe each receive ~HK$11.9M with no
   structure and no guardian for Chloe (§6.4)
6. **AMD Ordinance commenced 31 July 2026** — current statute, not generic advice
7. **HKMC RMP as a fourth property option** — retain, live in it, and monetise, without selling
8. **Digital asset succession** — no private key, no inheritance; bridges §7 to §9
9. **Conviction with structure:** a digital-asset policy for each person (Ryan's glide path without forced selling;
   Adrian 0%; Carmen optional ≤2% through ETFs), classified by economic exposure (§1A ③)
10. **No ESG return premium assumed** — intellectual honesty judges will respect
11. **Assumptions derived, not asserted.** Medical trend via the actuarial three-stage method with a separate VHIS
    age curve. Cash return from the forward curve and the neutral rate. FX stress from 20 years of data by currency.
    Few teams will show where their numbers come from ([methodology](assumptions-methodology.md))

## 12. Cautions

- **Do not recommend increasing crypto** because it sounds innovative. Equally, **do not lecture about it.** The
  answer is form, size and controls by family member (§1A ③): regulated wrappers, a glide path rather than a fire sale,
  and custody tied to succession. The family objective is *moderate growth with controlled downside*; Adrian is
  Low–Medium.
- **Frame the report around the standard financial planning process** (understand → analyse →
  recommend → implement → monitor). Judges from a financial planning society will recognise it.
- **Every recommendation must be quantified** and traced to a stated assumption.
- **Tailor advice to each member's risk profile** — Adrian L–M, Carmen M–H, Ryan H. The divergent
  risk appetites within one household is itself a suitability and behavioural risk to name in §11.
- **Save the interactive prototype for Round Two.** A half-page dashboard mockup is enough for Round One.
