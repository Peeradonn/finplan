# Carmen can claim her MPF at 62, after she stops all work

> **What to tell Pete**
>
> **Verdict: yes, with one condition.** Under the rules in force in October 2026, Carmen can withdraw her MPF at 62 on the "early retirement" ground. The condition is that **all her paid work has ended by the time she claims**. That means no job, no self-employment and no paid consultancy, and she must swear a statutory declaration that she does not intend to work again.
>
> **What it means for the plan.** The 2039 annuity stands if the staged sale (2035–39) ends with **no paid role for Carmen**. If the deal keeps her on as a paid consultant, employee or working director (for example during a handover or earn-out), the annuity waits until that role ends, or until she turns **65 in 2042**, when she can withdraw without any declaration. There is no direct MPF-to-annuity transfer: she withdraws the cash, then pays the HKMC premium. Do not assume any premium discount.
>
> **Slide 6 (speaker notes):** "Carmen takes her MPF at 62 under the early-retirement rule (age 60+, all paid work stopped, sworn declaration), tax-free, then buys the HKMC annuity with the cash. There's no direct transfer, and we assume no discount."
>
> **Slide 11 (timeline, "2039 Carmen's annuity"):** add "once her last paid role in the business sale ends; otherwise at 65 in 2042".

The answer is a clear yes on age and a conditional yes on work status. The MPF Authority's rule is short: a member must be **at least 60 and have ceased all employments and self-employments**, and must swear she has **no intention of becoming employed or self-employed again** ([MPFA: Early withdrawal, reviewed 16/10/2026](https://www.mpfa.org.hk/en/mpf-system/withdrawal-of-mpf/early-withdrawal)). Carmen easily meets the age test at 62. The only real risk to the 2039 date is the staged business sale. Any paid role she keeps with the buyer or the business is work, and she cannot honestly sign the declaration until it has ended. Once she claims, the money is tax-free whether she takes a lump sum or instalments. She then buys the HKMC Annuity Plan with cash like any other buyer. All of this is current law as of October 2026, 13 years before she would use it.

## The rule: 60 or over, all work stopped, and a sworn declaration

The declaration is the legal trigger. Carmen signs Form MPF(S)-W(SD1), which states that she has "permanently ceased all employment with no intention of becoming employed or self-employed again" and "ceased all self-employment" ([HSBC copy of SD1, Version 14, March 2019](https://forms.hsbc.com.hk/content/dam/hsbc/hk/docs/mpf/mpfs_wsd1.pdf)). A 2015 LegCo paper explains that a member **"is deemed to have permanently ceased employment or self-employment if a declaration is made"** ([LegCo CB(1)414/14-15, 8 Jan 2015](https://www.legco.gov.hk/yr14-15/english/hc/papers/hc20150109cb1-414-e.pdf)). The trustee does not look into her business affairs. The legal risk falls on Carmen. Under s.36 of the Crimes Ordinance, a knowingly false statutory declaration carries **up to 2 years' imprisonment and a fine**. Under s.43E of the MPF Schemes Ordinance, it is also an offence to make a false statement to a trustee ([HSBC SD1](https://forms.hsbc.com.hk/content/dam/hsbc/hk/docs/mpf/mpfs_wsd1.pdf)).

The process now runs through the **eMPF Platform**. Carmen signs the declaration before a Commissioner for Oaths, a notary public or a JP. She then files the claim on eMPF, and the trustee generally pays **within 30 days** of receiving all documents ([MPFA](https://www.mpfa.org.hk/en/mpf-system/withdrawal-of-mpf/early-withdrawal)). A company retirement age below 60 does not help: early withdrawal still waits until 60 ([WTW, Dec 2022](https://www.wtwco.com/en-HK/Insights/2022/12/can-i-take-my-mpf-benefits-if-i-retire-early)).

## How she ran the business decides what she has to stop

The same ground applies whether Carmen is self-employed or an employee of her own company. What changes is the thing she must stop. If the business is a **sole proprietorship or partnership**, she is a self-employed person ([MPFA SEP FAQ, 30/04/2026](https://www.mpfa.org.hk/en/info-centre/faq/self-employed)) and must end that self-employment. If it is a **limited company** and she is a paid executive director involved in day-to-day running, the MPFA treats her as an employee of that company ([MPFA employee FAQ, 16/10/2026](https://www.mpfa.org.hk/en/info-centre/faq/employee/mpf-employee)). In that case she must end that employment. The model should state which structure the business uses. No MPFA source deals with owner-directors withdrawing early, so this reading combines two separate MPFA rules.

During the staged sale, the common arrangements sort into three groups. **Blocks the claim:** a paid handover, consultancy or earn-out role that requires her to keep working. The MPFA treats a consultant on a contract for services as possibly self-employed, and one on a contract of employment as an employee ([MPFA SEP FAQ](https://www.mpfa.org.hk/en/info-centre/faq/self-employed)). WTW is blunt: if paid work of more than 60 days is anticipated, the person "is not eligible for early payment" ([WTW 2022](https://www.wtwco.com/en-HK/Insights/2022/12/can-i-take-my-mpf-benefits-if-i-retire-early)). **Probably fine:** an earn-out that is only deferred sale price with no work attached, and dividends from shares she keeps. Both are investment or sale proceeds, not work, although no source says so explicitly. **Grey area:** a non-executive board seat with director's fees. The MPFA calls such a director "an office holder only, not an employee" for MPF purposes ([MPFA employee FAQ](https://www.mpfa.org.hk/en/info-centre/faq/employee/mpf-employee)), but nothing confirms that it is not "self-employment" for the declaration.

The declaration is judged at the moment she signs it. If she later takes up work she had not planned, nothing claws back the withdrawal. A new employer would simply have to enrol her if she is under 65 ([MPFA employee FAQ](https://www.mpfa.org.hk/en/info-centre/faq/employee/mpf-employee)). A consultancy already agreed when she signs would make the declaration false.

## She can take it all, take it in parts, or leave it invested

Since **1 February 2016**, early retirees can take a **lump sum or instalments** ([MPFA](https://www.mpfa.org.hk/en/mpf-system/withdrawal-of-mpf/early-withdrawal); [IRD DIPN 23, para 40](https://www.ird.gov.hk/eng/pdf/dipn23.pdf)). Trustees must process at least **four withdrawals a year free of charge**, and there is no legal minimum amount ([LegCo 2015](https://www.legco.gov.hk/yr14-15/english/hc/papers/hc20150109cb1-414-e.pdf)). Whatever she does not withdraw stays invested and keeps paying fund fees ([MPFA: Withdrawal upon retirement, 05/10/2026](https://www.mpfa.org.hk/en/mpf-system/withdrawal-of-mpf/withdrawal-of-mpf-upon-retirement)). She does not have to claim at 62. She can wait and claim on early retirement at any point before 65, or at 65 with no declaration at all.

Voluntary money splits two ways. **Tax-deductible voluntary contributions (TVC)** are locked in like mandatory contributions, so they come out on the same early-retirement ground. **Ordinary voluntary contributions** follow each scheme's own rules, not the law ([MPFA employee FAQ](https://www.mpfa.org.hk/en/info-centre/faq/employee/mpf-employee)). If her role in the sale runs past 2039, she might be able to take ordinary voluntary money out earlier, depending on her trustee's rules.

## Buying the annuity: withdraw first, then pay the premium

No direct route from MPF or eMPF into the HKMC Annuity Plan exists. A 2021 idea of transferring MPF balances into annuities, mandatory or automatic, was never adopted ([The Standard, 29 Sep 2021](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/34621/MPF-annuity-push-takes-hit)). The 2025 MPF regulation deals with portability, not annuities ([info.gov.hk, 26 Sep 2025](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202509/26/P2025092500592p.htm)). In practice, she claims, the trustee pays her bank account (within about 30 days), and she books an HKMCA appointment and pays a single premium.

The eligibility terms come from the earlier fact check and remain unchanged. She must hold a **Hong Kong permanent ID card** and be **60 or over**, with no health check. The premium runs from **HK$50,000 per policy up to HK$5M per person** ([info.gov.hk, 31 May 2022](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202205/31/P2022053100383.htm); [IFEC](https://www.ifec.org.hk/web/en/financial-products/insurance/product-types/annuity/compare-public-and-private-annuities.page)). A woman aged 62 currently gets **HK$4,940 a month per HK$1M** ([HKMCA brochure](https://www.hkmca.hk/eng/files/Product_brochure_E.pdf)). The plan should confirm that Carmen holds a permanent ID card.

**Do not assume a discount.** MPF members have received time-limited premium discounts: **1%** in June–December 2022, **up to 2%** plus a 25% payout booster on the first HK$300K in April–December 2024 ([HKMA, 26 Apr 2024](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/news-and-media/press-releases/2024/04/20240426-3)), and **3%** in June–September 2026 ([info.gov.hk, 29 May 2026](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202605/29/P2026052900565.htm)). Promotions recur, but each has a fixed window. If one is running in 2039, it may only apply while she is still a registered MPF member, so the timing of her claim could matter.

If she has to wait until 65, the delay has a small upside. The brochure rate for a woman at 65 is **HK$5,300 per HK$1M**, against HK$4,940 at 62 ([HKMCA brochure](https://www.hkmca.hk/eng/files/Product_brochure_E.pdf)). That is about 7% more a month, but she loses three years of payments. In the meantime she would also need other income to cover those three years.

## Tax: nothing on the MPF, no relief on the premium

MPF benefits withdrawn on retirement or early retirement are **not chargeable to salaries tax**. That covers employee and employer mandatory contributions and voluntary contributions, as a lump sum or in instalments ([IRD DIPN 23, paras 24–25, 40, 42](https://www.ird.gov.hk/eng/pdf/dipn23.pdf); [IRD MPF FAQ](https://www.ird.gov.hk/eng/faq/mpf.htm)). The HKMC premium is **not tax-deductible**. IFEC lists the plan's tax incentives as "Nil", because only certified deferred annuities (QDAPs) qualify ([IFEC](https://www.ifec.org.hk/web/en/financial-products/insurance/product-types/annuity/compare-public-and-private-annuities.page)). Before 2039, while she still has taxable income, TVC and QDAP premiums can save tax, with a combined deduction of up to **HK$60,000 a year** ([IFEC QDAP/TVC FAQ](https://www.ifec.org.hk/web/en/managing-your-money/qdap-tvc/faq.html)). No primary source says whether the **annuity payouts** are taxed. They are almost certainly tax-free, because Hong Kong taxes only salaries, profits and property income, but that is an inference.

## Conclusion

The real constraint on Carmen's annuity is the sale contract, not MPF law. Business buyers routinely ask the seller to stay on for a paid transition period. If the 2035–39 sale includes one that runs into 2039, the plan's annuity date moves with it. The model owner can fix this by adding one assumption, "no paid role after the final stage of the sale", or by making the claim date the later of 2039 and the end of her role. Waiting until 65 is a fallback with no legal risk. It needs no declaration and pays a slightly higher annuity rate, at the cost of three years without annuity income.
