## 1. HKMA Green and Sustainable Finance Grant Scheme: loans

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** Every number matches. Two precisions matter on stage. (a) The caps apply **per eligible issuance
(per loan)**, not per entity. (b) The grant pays for **external review and advisory fees** (the cost of proving the loan
green), **not interest**. It does not make the loan cheaper to service.

**Source:** HKMA, *Guideline on the Green and Sustainable Finance Grant Scheme (For Applications On/After 24 April 2026)*,
effective 24 April 2026. https://www.hkma.gov.hk/media/eng/doc/key-functions/ifc/bond-market-development/GSFGS_Guideline_(Effective_from_20260424).pdf
The Guideline has no numbered paragraphs: it is a set of two-column tables (Track I | Track II). I cite by page and row.
I checked which column each figure sits in from the PDF's text positions.

- **p.1 (extension):** "As announced by the Financial Secretary in the 2024-25 Budget, the GSF Grant Scheme, which was due
  to expire in May 2024, has been extended by three years to 2027, with an expanded scope of subsidies to cover transition
  bonds and loans."
- **p.2 (Overview, Track II):** "Sustainability External Review and Advisory Service Costs: covering transaction-related
  sustainability external review fees (including pre-issuance and post-issuance services) and sustainability advisory fees
  (only pre-issuance) for eligible green, social, sustainability, sustainability-linked and transition bond issuers and
  loan borrowers, including first-time and repeated issuers and borrowers."
- **p.3 (Eligible instrument types):** Track I: "Green, social, sustainability, sustainability-linked and transition
  bonds". Track II: "Green, social, sustainability, sustainability-linked and transition bonds and loans". So loans can
  only use Track II.
- **p.3 (Eligible bond issuers and loan borrowers, Track II):** "Loans: First-time and repeated borrowers (each entity can
  apply for subsidy for two eligible loans at most)". Footnote 5: "Including all eligible loans where the entity acts as
  borrower or guarantor."
- **p.3 (Minimum issuance size for eligible issuances (bonds and loans), Track II):** "At least HK$100 million (or the
  equivalent in foreign currency)". For comparison, Track I (bonds only), pp.3–4: "For bonds issued on or after 24 April
  2026: At least HK$2.5 billion (or the equivalent in foreign currency)".
- **p.6 (Grants per eligible issuance, Track II):** "For bonds and loans issued on or after 24 April 2026: o Up to 75% of
  eligible expenses, if the bond/loan is aligned with the latest Hong Kong Taxonomy for Sustainable Finance published by
  the HKMA ("Hong Kong Taxonomy"); or o Up to 50% of eligible expenses, if otherwise".
- **pp.6–7 (caps, Track II):** "Each eligible issuance is also subject to: ⚫ HK$800,000 in total for all pre-issuance
  sustainability external review and advisory services and post-issuance external review services; ⚫ HK$250,000 for all
  pre-issuance sustainability external review and advisory services; and ⚫ HK$200,000 per year for all post-issuance
  external review services".
- **pp.7–8 (eligible expenses):** post-issuance review is covered "for the first three years from the date of the eligible
  issuance or up until the maturity of the issuance, whichever is shorter".
- **p.9 (Glossary):** "A loan is considered issued in Hong Kong if at least half of the loan amount is borrowed from Hong
  Kong-based lenders."

**Slide line the source fully supports:** "Hong Kong's green-finance grant starts at a HK$100 million loan, and it pays for
the green review, not the interest." (HKMA GSF Grant Scheme Guideline, 24 Apr 2026)

## 2. "Compostable" PLA counts as plastic under Hong Kong law

**Verdict: CORRECTED.** The legal core is confirmed: the law defines plastic by its polymer. EPD's own letter to LegCo says
"biodegradable or compostable" plastics made of chemically modified polymers "fall within the meaning of 'plastic'" and
are regulated. EPD's 2021 consultation names PLA as the common example. "Phase 2 has no date" is confirmed as of the
latest official statement I found (7 May 2025). The sentence "O·PARK doesn't accept PLA" is **not** in any EPD source I
found. EPD's own food-waste guidance excludes packaging and utensils/containers in general, without naming PLA. Reword it
(below).

Two precisions:
- **Regulated as plastic is not the same as banned today.** Phase 1 bans only some items. PLA takeaway cups and food
  containers are still lawful until Phase 2 (LCQ22 reply below).
- **e-Legislation (Cap. 603) serves only a JavaScript shell to automated fetches.** I used the enacted Amendment Ordinance
  on legco.gov.hk instead. It is the gazetted text that inserts the definition into Cap. 603.

### 2a. The law's definition
**Source:** *Product Eco-responsibility (Amendment) Ordinance 2023*, Ord. No. 27 of 2023, s.33, adding s.3A to Cap. 603
(Gazette p. A3355). https://www.legco.gov.hk/yr2023/english/ord/2023ord027-e.pdf. LegCo passed the Bill on 18 Oct 2023
(https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202310/18/P2023101800622.htm).
- **s.3A(1):** "For the purposes of this Ordinance, plastic is a material consisting of a polymer (other than a natural
  polymer that has not been chemically modified), whether or not an additive or any other substance has been added to the
  material."
- **s.3A(2):** "For the purposes of this Ordinance, a product is made of plastic if it is wholly or partly made of a
  material mentioned in subsection (1)."
- The Ordinance contains no exemption for "biodegradable" or "compostable" plastics. The words do not appear in it. It
  separately bans oxo-degradable plastic (s.3B).

### 2b. EPD's own statement that biodegradable/compostable plastics are covered
**Source:** EPD (Waste Management Division), letter to the Clerk to the Bills Committee, 30 May 2023, LC Paper No.
CB(1)558/2023(02). https://www.legco.gov.hk/yr2023/english/bc/bc02/papers/bc0220230606cb1-558-2-e.pdf. It is a scanned
letter. I removed two stray scan marks (".any", ",'plastic").
- **Para 2:** "Under the Amendment Bill, "plastic" is a material consisting of a polymer, whether or not an additive or any
  other substance has been added to the material, unless the polymer is a natural polymer that has not been chemically
  modified, or is used merely as an additive and does not function as a major structural component of the product.
  Therefore, among the various market-available plastic products that claim to be "degradable" (including biodegradable
  or compostable ones), those consist of chemically modified polymers (e.g. plastics that are chemically synthesised with
  raw materials such as natural starch or bacterial cellulose) still fall within the meaning of "plastic" under the
  Amendment Bill even if their raw materials are natural, and are thus subject to regulation under the Amendment Bill."
- **Para 3:** "[...] waste treatment in Hong Kong currently relies on landfilling as the major outlet, and there is a lack of
  evidence that various market-available plastic products that claim to be "degradable" will fully degrade in the actual
  and anaerobic landfill environment, or in the marine environment."

**PLA named by EPD:** EPD, *Public Consultation: Regulation of Disposable Plastic Tableware* (9 Jul – 8 Sep 2021),
Appendix 3, p.26.
https://www.epd.gov.hk/epd/sites/default/files/epd/english/environmentinhk/waste/pub_consult/files/tableware-con-doc-en.pdf
- "Biodegradable plastic tableware*": "It is synthesised from renewable resources (e.g. corn starch, cassava roots and
  sugarcane), and a common example is polylactic acid (PLA). Biodegradable plastic tableware is degradable only under
  certain conditions. The quality of the recycled plastics will be affected if biodegradable plastic tableware is added in
  the existing recycling system." Footnote: "＊ The proposed Regulation Scheme will cover these types of disposable plastic
  tableware."
- Para 3.2: "[...] we suggest that the Regulation Scheme should cover disposable tableware made of all kinds of plastics,
  including oxo-degradable plastics and biodegradable plastics."
- (This is the proposal stage. The enacted definition in 2a plus EPD's 2023 letter in 2b carry the legal weight.)

**Regulated is not banned yet:** LCQ22 "Non-plastic disposable tableware", reply of 8 May 2024.
https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202405/08/P2024050800233.htm. "[...] the first phase of the regulation only prohibits
the use of expanded polystyrene cups and food containers but not other disposable plastic cups and food containers [...]
As we are adopting a progressive regulatory approach, there are no specific requirements to reduce the use of
biodegradable disposable plastic tableware at this stage."

**Plastic lining counts:** LCQ14, reply of 7 May 2025 (below), part (2): "should relevant disposable tableware be wholly or
partly made of plastic (including plastic lining), it will be regarded as regulated disposable plastic tableware."

### 2c. Phase 1 date; Phase 2 has no date
- LCQ16, reply of 16 Apr 2025. https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202504/16/P2025041600273.htm. "[...] the regulation on
  disposable plastic products (the Regulation) commenced its first phase on April 22, 2024, and the six-month adaptation
  period ended on October 21, 2024."
- LCQ14, reply of 7 May 2025. https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202505/07/P2025050700299p.htm. Part (4): "Before
  implementing the second phase of the Regulation, the Government will thoroughly consider the maturity, availability and
  affordability of the relevant non-plastic alternatives [...] While there is no implementation timetable at the moment,
  we will consider the progress of further regulation in light of the prevailing circumstances."
- Secondary, consistent: The Standard, 24 May 2025, reports the Secretary for Environment and Ecology saying "there is no
  fixed timeline for the implementation". I found **no 2026 official statement** either way (searched 5 Nov 2026).

### 2d. Composting route / O·PARK
No EPD or O·PARK page I found names PLA or "compostable" packaging. What EPD does say:
- **EPD food-waste flyer and poster (commercial & industrial).** Linked from EPD's "Food Waste Challenge" page
  (https://www.epd.gov.hk/epd/english/environmentinhk/waste/prob_solutions/food_waste_challenge.html). File:
  https://www.epd.gov.hk/epd/sites/default/files/epd/tc_chi/environmentinhk/waste/prob_solutions/images/CI_Flyer.pdf. Under
  "General Waste": "Plastic bags, packaging, utensils/containers, aluminum foils, cans, glass bottles, ropes". Reminder:
  "Food packaging must be removed before recycling." The Food Waste Challenge page itself says food waste from EPD's
  collection Pilot Scheme "will be transformed into energy, and compost as a by-product at O·PARK1".
- **EPD domestic poster** (…/images/Poster_DOMESTIC.pdf), under "不接受 NO": "Packaging, straws, cans, utensils/containers,
  glass bottles, teabags, tissue paper, toothpicks", labelled "General Waste".
- **O·PARK1 "Process" page** (https://www.opark.gov.hk/en/process.php): "A grab crane is used to transfer food waste to the
  crusher, which tears up the packaging materials [...] Food waste suspension is then transferred to the Trommel Sieve Drum
  to remove the over-size impurities of about 16mm in size or greater, such as plastic bag fragments."
- **O·PARK2:** its site (https://www.opark2.com.hk/) carries only environmental-monitoring documents, with no acceptance
  rules. Not checked further.
- **Inference (not a quote):** PLA cutlery and containers fall under "utensils/containers" and "packaging", which EPD
  sends to general waste. EPD states no exception for compostable ones.

**Slide line the sources fully support:** "In Hong Kong, 'compostable' PLA is legally plastic, and the food-waste bins that
feed O·PARK turn away packaging and utensils." (Cap. 603 s.3A; EPD to LegCo, 30 May 2023; EPD food-waste poster)
Avoid saying "O·PARK rejects PLA" or "it all goes to landfill" unless EPD confirms it.

## 3. Green mortgages are worth little to this family

**Verdict: CORRECTED.** "No lower rate" is confirmed. At a HK$1.8M loan the **published** one-off green rebate is **HK$0 to
HK$3,000**, not HK$0–4K: BEA HK$0, Standard Chartered HK$888, HKMC HK$1,000 or HK$3,000. HSBC publishes no amount. The
building-rating requirement is confirmed for the three banks. Three facts we had missed:
1. **Standard Chartered and HSBC exclude refinancing.** HSBC covers only purchases of first-hand flats. An existing
   mortgage like the Wongs' cannot move into either plan. BEA does accept refinancing.
2. **HKMC accepts a flat-level certificate,** BEAM Plus Interiors (Residential). Its 2026 Green Promotion runs only from
   17 Mar to 31 Dec 2026.
3. **Standard Chartered's real perk is a deposit cap, not a rate.** Its deposit-linked account cap is 60% (instead of 50%)
   for 3 years, and it helps only a borrower who keeps idle cash with the bank.

## 4. Listed companies must report Scope 3 from FY2026

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** Three precisions:
- (a) Scope 3 is not singled out. Para 17(2) makes **all of Part D** mandatory for Hang Seng Composite LargeCap Index
  (HSCLI) constituents, and Part D includes Scope 3 (para 28(c)). The rule applies to issuers that were constituents
  **throughout the previous year**, so the first mandatory FY2026 reports are **published in 2027**.
- (b) Scope 3 carries the **Reasonable Information Relief** (Note 1 to para 29).
- (c) Other Main Board issuers have been on comply-or-explain **since FY2025**. GEM issuers are voluntary for Part D, but
  Scope 1 and 2 are mandatory for all issuers from FY2025.

**Sources:**
- **Live Listing Rules, Appendix C2 (Environmental, Social and Governance Reporting Code), Part D.** The HKEX rulebook's
  latest version is "Jan 01 2025". https://en-rules.hkex.com.hk/rulebook/appendix-c2-environmental-social-and-governance-reporting-code-0
  - **16(1):** "Subject to paragraph 17, an issuer must report on the climate-related disclosures set out in this part in
    the ESG report on a "comply or explain" basis. An issuer who has yet to disclose information required under any of
    the provisions must provide considered reasons for non-disclosure."
  - **17(1):** "An issuer must disclose its Scope 1 greenhouse gas emissions and Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions pursuant
    to paragraphs 28(a), 28(b) and 29 on a mandatory basis."
  - **17(2):** "An issuer that is a constituent of the Hang Seng Composite LargeCap Index (HSCLI) must report on the
    provisions set out in this part on a mandatory basis in respect of financial years commencing on or after 1 January
    2026." Note: "This paragraph 17(2) applies to an issuer that is a HSCLI constituent throughout the year immediately
    prior to the reporting year. Once an issuer becomes subject to mandatory disclosure of Part D of this Code, it must
    continue to be subject to mandatory disclosure of Part D of this Code even if it subsequently ceases to be a HSCLI
    constituent."
  - **28:** "An issuer shall disclose its absolute gross greenhouse gas emissions generated during the reporting period,
    expressed as metric tons of CO2 equivalent, classified as: (a) Scope 1 [...]; (b) Scope 2 [...]; and (c) Scope 3
    greenhouse gas emissions."
  - **29, Note 1:** "An issuer is required to use all reasonable and supportable information that is available to it at
    the reporting date without undue cost or effort when the issuer selects the measurement approach, inputs and
    assumptions it uses in measuring Scope 3 greenhouse gas emissions."
  - Introduction: "ESG reports prepared in compliance with the IFRS S1 [...] and IFRS S2 Climate-related Disclosures are
    considered to have complied with Part D of this Code."
- **HKEX Consultation Conclusions, *Enhancement of Climate-related Disclosures under the ESG Framework*, April 2024.**
  https://www.hkex.com.hk/-/media/HKEX-Market/News/Market-Consultations/2016-Present/April-2023-Climate-related-Disclosures/Conclusions-Apr-2024/cp202304cc.pdf
  - **Para 8 table:** LargeCap Issuers, disclosures other than Scope 1 and 2: ""Comply or explain": in financial years
    commencing on or after 1 January 2025 - Mandatory disclosure: in financial years commencing on or after 1 January
    2026". Main Board Issuers (other than LargeCap): ""Comply or explain" (Financial years commencing on or after 1
    January 2025)". GEM: "Voluntary disclosure".
  - **Para 51:** "[...] issuers that are constituents of the HSCLI throughout their entire financial year 2025 will be
    required to make mandatory climate disclosure in respect of their ESG reports for the financial year 2026, and such ESG
    reports will be published in 2027."
  - **Para 187:** "[...] we will make available the Reasonable Information Relief for the disclosure of scope 3 GHG
    emissions [...] For the avoidance of doubt, disclosure of scope 3 GHG emissions will be subject to the Phased
    Approach."
  - **Para 55:** HKEX "may consider mandating climate-related disclosures for all listed issuers when we consult the
    market on the implementation of the HK Standards (when available)." This means other issuers are not yet mandatory.
- HKEX news release, 19 Apr 2024 (same phasing table).
  https://www.hkex.com.hk/News/Regulatory-Announcements/2024/240419news. Implementation Guidance (April 2024), pp.17–19,
  lists the reliefs:
  https://www.hkex.com.hk/-/media/HKEX-Market/Listing/Rules-and-Guidance/Environmental-Social-and-Governance/Exchanges-guidance-materials-on-ESG/guidance_enhanced_climate_dis.pdf

**Slide line the sources fully support:** "From financial year 2026, Hong Kong's LargeCap listed companies must report their
value-chain (Scope 3) emissions; other Main Board companies must comply or explain." (HKEX Listing Rules App. C2, paras
16–17, 28(c))
