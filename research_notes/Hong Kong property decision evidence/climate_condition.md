# Physical climate risk, insurance and building condition

Research date and evidence cutoff: **8 November 2026**. All external sources below were opened and read on that date; a publication date is stated where visible. An undated live page is evidence of what was accessible, not proof of when its policy changed. Confidence is high for directly stated official rules and historical observations; site-specific loss probabilities and premiums remain **unverified**. Lenses: practitioner adviser, insurance/compliance, family and ESG strategist.

## What physical risks should change the family's property advice?

### Takeaway

**Replace “not on a flood list” with a site and policy check, and replace the proposed compulsory exit in 2045 with a condition-based review.** Climate evidence supports precaution and liquidity planning, but cannot price the Wong flat's flood loss or justify a causal 10% climate discount. The warehouse's floor, equipment, access route and insurance matter more than its district name or area.

### Cited Findings

- **Case facts, independently read first:** the home is a self-occupied Tai Kok Tsui flat, estimated at HK$11.5M with HK$1.8M mortgage; the family has basic home insurance. Carmen co-founded a sustainable-packaging distribution business; the case does not specify the warehouse address, floor, lease, insurance, energy use, or a home completion year. Adrian is 54, Carmen 49; intended retirement ages are 65 and 62. [Case PDF, pp.2,4–6](../../Primary%20Info/Award2026Case20260919.pdf). The relative link from this notes folder is for identification; repository path is `Primary Info/Award2026Case20260919.pdf`.
- The official Area 4 task requires retrofit/certification economics, physical-risk effects on insurance/mortgage terms, exit risk and landlord–tenant incentives. It does not prescribe selling at a particular building age. [Final topic, p.3](../../Final/2026%20UFP%20Final%20Round%20Topic.pdf), repository path `Final/2026 UFP Final Round Topic.pdf`; Key Areas repeats the requirement to distinguish measured, estimated and illustrative data.
- **DSD's accessible current framework is Flood-Prone Areas**, replacing the historic flooding-blackspots list. DSD says the historic list targeted large-scale drainage-capacity flooding; recent flooding often arises from other causes, including blocked local drains, and coastal low-lying/windy areas face high tides. Its three groups are blockage-prone locations, areas with drainage works in progress and coastal low-lying/windy residential areas. This is not an exhaustive parcel-level flood probability model. Publication/effective date not displayed. [DSD, Flood-Prone Areas](https://www.dsd.gov.hk/EN/Flood_Prevention/Our_Flooding_Situation/Flood_Prone_Areas/index.html).
- CEDD's Coastal Hazards Study completed in **2021** identified **26** coastal low-lying/windy residential areas; the Shoreline Management Plan study completed at **end-2024**. Their purpose is targeted coastal protection and management. The accessible overview does not certify the safety of every location outside its selected residential areas. Page date not shown in text. [CEDD, Coastal Enhancement and Shoreline Management](https://www.cedd.gov.hk/eng/our-projects/topics-in-focus/index-id-39.html).
- **Observed event, not future probability:** HKO recorded **158.1 mm in one hour**, 23:00–24:00 on **7 September 2023**, at HKO Headquarters, the highest since records began in 1884. Saola also caused low-lying coastal flooding on 1–2 September. Publication date not shown in article text; event dates explicit. [HKO, Typhoons and Rainstorms Affecting Hong Kong in September and October 2023](https://www.hko.gov.hk/en/education/friends_hko/e-newsletter/vol91/rainstorm_tc_2023.html).
- **Observed heat:** HKO's 2025 review records **53 very hot days** (maximum temperature at least 33°C), **54 hot nights** (minimum at least 28°C) and five Black Rainstorm Warnings. These are station/year statistics, not indoor temperatures or a monotonic annual trend. Publication date not visible in fetched text; observations for full-year 2025. [HKO, The Year's Weather 2025](https://www.hko.gov.hk/en/wxinfo/pastwx/2025/ywx2025.htm).
- **Projected heat:** the 1995–2014 baseline is 14 very hot days annually; under SSP2-4.5, HKO projects a 2041–2060 mean of **65**, likely range **42–100**. These are 20-year-average scenario estimates with at least 66% probability for the likely interval; they are not a forecast that 2037 has 65 very hot days. Page undated; CMIP6 data. [HKO, Projection data of very hot days](https://www.hko.gov.hk/en/climate_change/proj_hk_annual_vhot_days_info.htm).
- **Projected sea level:** HKO gives 2100 increases relative to 1995–2014 of **0.37–0.82 m** under SSP2-4.5 and **0.57–1.08 m** under SSP5-8.5; reference location Quarry Bay. These are not 2037 water levels at the Wong building. Rising mean sea level increases storm-surge threat. Undated page, underlying IPCC AR6 sources **2021**. [HKO, Mean sea level projection](https://www.hko.gov.hk/en/climate_change/proj_hk_msl.htm).

### Inferences

- Use **hazard → exposure → vulnerability → financial consequence**. Rain intensity is a hazard; the warehouse stock's height above potential water entry and its drainage/door configuration determine exposure/vulnerability. Financial loss then depends on stock values, interruption days, repair responsibilities, policy cover and cash availability. None can be inferred from “Kwai Chung, 7,500 sq ft”.
- Even an upper-floor unit, if subsequently confirmed, can suffer wind-driven water entry, facade/window damage, unavailable lifts, blocked access or power interruption. It reduces some direct inundation pathways but does not eliminate building or business interruption exposure. Do not carry forward the previous brief's unsupported upper-floor assumption.
- For the home, check water entry at windows and balconies, common-area pumps/electrical rooms/lifts, maintenance history, drainage/backflow and access. For the warehouse, check loading bay and access road levels, roof/wall leaks, goods storage height, packaging stock susceptibility, fire separation and business continuity arrangements. These are due-diligence recommendations, not diagnosed defects.
- Heat affects cooling expenditure, indoor comfort and worker safety/operations. A Grade 1 replacement can reduce energy demand relative to a matched replacement, but cannot be described as a complete heat-health safeguard or proof of climate resilience. Medical insurance does not physically mitigate overheating.
- Do not copy “low–medium likelihood, mitigated: yes” from the old risk register. Without a site survey and policy schedule, record likelihood **unassessed** and mitigation **partly implemented/not verified**.

Missing: exact address, floor, area basis, elevation, plant location, history, drainage, landlord/OC responsibilities, building management, stock values, gross profit and alternate premises. No source permits an address-specific loss probability or expected annual loss.

- Further data to obtain: building records/occupation permit, OC minutes and reserve accounts, repair tenders, previous water damage/claims, insurer surveys, warehouse lease and schedule of condition, 12–24 months operating and utility records. Permission to enter premises or inspect records is a later implementation matter.

## What do insurance and mortgage rules actually require?

### Takeaway

**The family's “basic home insurance” is insufficient information to decide adequacy.** Test building, contents and business interruption separately, including temporary liquidity before claims settle. Building age is a reason to inspect and seek bank quotes; neither HKMA nor HKMC establishes the proposed universal age-50 forced-exit rule.

### Cited Findings

- IA explains that home insurance and building/fire insurance protect different interests. Landlord home cover does not cover tenants' household possessions/liabilities; banks generally require fire insurance for a mortgage. Premiums can reflect floor area, age and property type/floor. The owner's actual policy terms govern; this is no published quote for the Wongs. Page undated. [IA, Understanding Home Insurance](https://www.ia.org.hk/en/home_insurance/index.html).
- **Compulsory OC policy is third-party risks insurance:** effective **1 January 2011**, minimum **HK$10M per event**, in relation to common parts/OC property. This legal requirement does not by itself establish that the OC has first-party cover to rebuild damaged common parts. [Home Affairs Department, Building Management (Third Party Risks Insurance) Regulation](https://www.buildingmgt.gov.hk/en/Policy_and_Legislation/2_4.html).
- IA's **4 December 2023** release reports approximately **HK$1.9bn total gross claims incurred** for Saola and the September 2023 black rainstorm; property damage represented 86%. These are aggregate incurred insurance claims, not total economic loss, final paid claims or a household repair quote. [IA release](https://www.ia.org.hk/en/infocenter/press_releases/20231204.html).
- **Current example, not a recommendation or family coverage:** MSIG's **February 2026** business insurance brochure distinguishes basic BI additional expenditure (up to HK$1M) from **optional loss of gross profit**. Denial of access due to nearby damage and public-utility failure require **more than 48 hours**, with separate limits. Therefore “has BI cover” need not mean “insured for all lost profits/typhoon closure”. [MSIG brochure, pp.5–6](https://www.msig.com.hk/sites/msig_hk/files/H711_Business%20Insurance%20_Brochure_Feb%202026_0.pdf).
- QBE's accessible Hong Kong SME wording, code **UWD.SME.QBIZ.V1-0821**, includes an indemnity period set by the schedule, excluded-property/peril restrictions and specific access/utility provisions. It has an exception where property insurer non-payment is solely due to the material-damage deductible. This illustrates why schedule, exclusions and endorsements must be read together. Code suggests Aug-2021 version; current inception applicability to this client **unverified**. [QBE Hong Kong SME policy wording, pp.24–26](https://www.qbe.com/media/qbe/asia/hongkong/files/qtouch/business-insurance/sme/byo_policy_wording.pdf?rev=5ed1ae2bef654746a2de36f3fd82aefe).
- **MBIS:** private buildings age **30+**, except domestic buildings not exceeding three storeys, **served statutory notices** must appoint a Registered Inspector and perform required repairs under supervision. Age30 is not by itself proof of an outstanding notice. Current page search rendering states update **30 January 2026**; fetched main text leaves date field blank. [Buildings Department, MBIS](https://www.bd.gov.hk/en/safety-inspection/mbis/index.html).
- The **10 September 2025** Government reply after consulting HKMA states HKMA does **not** set a specific mortgage-term limit by property age. Banks commercially consider repayment capacity and condition as well as age; inspection/repair compliance letters can help assessment. The question's “75 minus building age” description is not the regulator's rule. [LCQ17, Mortgage terms for aged buildings](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202509/10/P2025091000373.htm).
- **HKMC RMP:** properties **over 50** considered case by case. The Government explicitly reports that applications involving older-than-50 properties have been approved and inspection reports may be needed (**14 May 2025** reply). [LCQ22, Reverse Mortgage Programme](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202505/14/P2025051300589.htm). The accessible HKMC programme page independently repeats the case-by-case exception; page undated. [HKMC programme eligibility](https://www.hkmc.com.hk/eng/our_business/reverse_mortgage_programme.html).

### Inferences

- For an assumed 1996 home, simple calendar arithmetic makes it 30 in2026, 41 in2037 and 50 in2046 (exact age depends on completion/application dates). The old “completion year +49 =2045 compulsory release” is a **team-created precaution**, not a legal or programme deadline. Recommend reviewing condition, lending options, repairs and alternative housing before commitment, with age50 as a documentary checkpoint rather than automatic sale.
- Keep distinctions clear: **HKMA prudential rules**, **individual bank underwriting policies**, **HKMC Mortgage Insurance Programme rules** and **HKMC Reverse Mortgage Programme** are different. This research establishes no universal conventional-bank age ceiling. Any specific MIP condition needs its own current clause, relevant loan type and exception; do not generalise it to every buyer or date.
- Ask an authorised intermediary to check: named insured/legal interest; permitted occupancy and business description; building/common-parts and tenant fit-out; stock valuation/seasonality; insured perils including flood, storm and water ingress; deterioration/maintenance exclusions; deductibles, time excesses and sublimits; underinsurance/average; BI gross-profit definition, indemnity period and material-damage trigger; access, utility and supplier/customer extensions; temporary premises/increased costs; claims documentation and interim payments. These are a checklist, not claims that every listed clause appears in each policy.
- Landlord building insurance does not establish coverage for the company's stock, fit-out, relocation or earnings. IA's residential landlord/tenant distinction supports checking separate interests, but a commercial lease and policy wording are required to determine the warehouse allocation.
- No reviewed source substantiates a Wong-specific premium increase, flood-cover withdrawal or lender margin increase. Avoid those numerical claims and the implication that insurance will certainly become unavailable. Quote actual renewal terms, reasons, exclusions and competing offers before a claim about climate-driven repricing.

- Obtain company property/BI policy and landlord policy certificate; the family case specifies home/motor cover but not warehouse cover. Insurer illustrations above show design variability, not suitability for a packaging warehouse.
- No validated OC levy or repair quote, insurance premium, claims settlement timing, bank valuation or mortgage covenant has been supplied. No advice to switch insurer, move the mortgage or sell should rely on assumed quotes.

## How should the project quantify and govern consequences?

### Takeaway

Use transparent **stress cash flows and break-even tests** while site-loss evidence is missing. Put the capital needed before reimbursement, permanent uninsured cost and family cash-flow exposure side by side. Treat relative house-price discounts and reverse-mortgage sensitivity as scenario tests; they do not establish a climate hedge or a causal premium.

### Cited Findings

- HKMA staff Research Memorandum **05/2023, 22 December 2023**, analyses 2000–2021 average housing prices across 18 districts using climate/exposure indicators. Its “High” scenario estimates prices **4–21% lower around2050**, depending on assumptions about average property age. The authors explicitly distinguish this from a forecast that actual nominal prices fall by that amount, given other demand/supply forces and mitigation. Historical rainfall impacts were small, and the paper flags future exposure/data limitations after2023. The paper is authors' research, not an HKMA property valuation. [Original paper, pp.1–2,10–14](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/media/eng/publication-and-research/research/research-memorandums/2023/RM05-2023.pdf).
- This does not isolate a green-certification price premium, identify a 2037 Tai Kok Tsui discount, or measure a specific flat's repair cost. Those uses exceed the scope of the study above. The updated2025 banking stress-test PDF could not be fetched successfully in this research pass; figures quoted from it in old team notes are **not independently reverified here**.

### Inferences

**Method recommended.** Model at least (a) physical event/repair plus insurance timing, (b) energy/heat operating cost, (c) relative housing values at exit and (d) combined event with income interruption. State horizon, nominal versus real HK$, and affected legal entity. Keep probability-weighted expected annual loss separate from a stress loss. Without calibrated probabilities, do not multiply an arbitrary scenario by a guessed event probability.

**Illustrative home liquidity test — not family bills, a quote or a forecast.** Assume a one-off HK$100,000 owner levy, HK$20,000 temporary accommodation, and an insurer eventually reimburses HK$70,000 six months later. Immediate funding need =100,000+20,000=**HK$120,000**; eventual net outflow =120,000−70,000=**HK$50,000**, before interest. A ring-fenced reserve must bridge the gross payment, even though the permanent cost is smaller. At an assumed5% annual discount rate, reimbursement PV=70,000/(1.05)^0.5=**HK$68,313**, so event cost PV≈**HK$51,687**. Compare a prevention measure's cost against the reduction in this loss only after obtaining evidence for effectiveness/frequency; no positive expected NPV is asserted.

**Illustrative company interruption test.** Suppose repairs/stock replacement require HK$200,000, temporary operations HK$100,000 and lost cash contribution HK$60,000; assumed later net insurance recovery HK$200,000. Stress liquidity shortfall=**HK$360,000** relative to uninterrupted operations, permanent shortfall=**HK$160,000**. Lost contribution is a cash-flow reduction, not a separate invoice; avoid adding unchanged fixed wages/rent twice. The family's cost is not automatically HK$360,000: at illustrative50% ownership, HK$80,000 is a simple attributable economic share of the net company loss, **not** an automatic personal cash call. Actual capital injection depends on company reserves, debt, guarantees, shareholder agreements and board decisions. Salary/dividend interruption must be modelled separately without double counting company losses/valuation.

**Illustrative break-even condition, no guessed probabilities.** A mitigation costingHK$20,000 and lasting five years at5% discount needs expected annual net benefit of20,000/[sum(t=1..5)(1.05)^−t]=**HK$4,619/year**, after maintenance. That benefit could combine measured operating savings and defensibly estimated avoided loss. A physically essential safety repair should be evaluated for compliance/safety first; it need not pass a discretionary investment hurdle.

**House-price stress design.** Compare retain/repair, downsize and reverse mortgage with the same end date, future repair assumptions, debt, transaction costs, occupation costs, remaining property equity and estate debt. Test both “old home falls, replacement unchanged” and “both prices fall”; their released-cash results differ substantially. Apply the same shock consistently to RMP valuation, remaining home equity and exit proceeds. Lower payout sensitivity at a valuation breakpoint does not remove condition risk, repair liability or falling estate equity; calling RMP a “climate hedge” overstates the evidence. No existing exploratory retirement probabilities are re-exported or endorsed here.

**Strongest counterarguments.** Hong Kong drainage/coastal investment and maintained buildings can reduce exposure; macro2023 research found small historical rainfall-price effects. Moving can incur significant costs and disrupt the family, while an undamaged well-maintained older building may remain suitable. Those points strongly undermine compulsory age-based sale and automatic large climate haircuts. Conversely, a building-level survey, large unfunded levy, unavailable essential cover, bank rejection citing defects, or repeated documented flooding would justify earlier action even where district averages look benign.

**What would change the verdict.** A current site-specific engineering report and credible quantified loss/mitigation effectiveness could support expected-loss/NPV calculations; actual like-for-like insurance quotes could evidence price/availability change; paired repeat-sales or a suitable causal study could support a property-value effect; written bank/HKMC decisions could narrow eligibility. Until then, preserve uncertainty and use scenario thresholds.
