PETE: But will it last? Our written case measured investments alone: {{ladder_1_89}}. The fairer test is what a sensible family would do: sell the business when Carmen retires, keep the flat, and sell it only if they must. Across 10,000 simulated markets, in {{own_sold_pct}} they must. In a typical one, Carmen, at {{story_sale_age}} and on her own, sells the home where they raised Ryan and Chloe. By {{story_out_age}} the money has run out again, and the wealth meant for their children pays the bills: of HK$24.4 million, just {{own_legacy_sold}} is left to them. [Notes: Their own way: the business sold when Carmen retires; the flat kept and swapped for a HK$7M one only when the money runs out; the full budget after a death; the Flexi plan for life; equities 6% a year, inflation 2.5%, as in the written case. "Usually on her own": Adrian dies at 84 on the case's life expectancy, when Carmen is 79; she is alone in {{own_alone_pct}} of the forced sales. Even after the sale, the money lasts to 89 in only {{own_89}}. Why not a reverse mortgage at 83? It would let Carmen stay, but HKMC's fixed payout doesn't keep up with her bills and premiums: in Fahtai's test (HKMC's March 2026 table) the money lasts to 89 less often than selling, and the loan eats into what Ryan and Chloe inherit.]
