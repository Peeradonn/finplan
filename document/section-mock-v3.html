Why these four, and not more investment risk. The parents' investments reach about {{portfolio_2037_today}} in today's money by 2037, short of the {{need_2037}} a 2% real return would need. The obvious alternative, a higher equity allocation, would breach Adrian's low-to-medium risk profile, and even a 7% equity return instead of 6% only lifts the plan to {{eq7_89}}. The four decisions instead use assets the family already owns, and each stays open until its date. Their costs are real but deliberate: releasing the home's value leaves less to Ryan and Chloe, and the annuity cannot be reversed, which is why we annuitise only the MPF. None is load-bearing on its own: if the business cannot be sold, the plan still reaches {{biz_not_sold_89}}, and the drawdown guardrails below and the review triggers in §11 take over.

03Retirement planning · continued

The HK$780K target predates individual cover. From retirement the parents pay their own premiums, which rise with medical inflation (10% in 2027, easing to 6% by 2036) and with age (about 4.6% a year from 65 to 80). For both parents the line reaches HK$375K a year in today's money by 2056, close to half the budget (Figure 8). Buy now, while healthy: cover bought at 54 and 49 is guaranteed renewable to 100, while cover bought at retirement may exclude conditions that have appeared by then (§8). Review the tier at 75: the Standard plan costs about a third as much, and switching at Adrian's 75 lifts the plan from {{ladder_5_89}} to {{switch_89}}, at the price of lower limits and fewer private-hospital choices, a trade-off the family should make with their health in view. Use the public floor: Hospital Authority fees are capped at HK$10,000 a year per patient; critical-illness cover pays for what the cap excludes.

An income floor: essentials paid for life

Using the spending pattern of Hong Kong's top-quartile households (Household Expenditure Survey 2024/25), essentials are about 40% of the HK$780K: HK$312K a year today, about HK$430K by 2039. Converting the parents' MPF into HKMC life annuities at retirement pays HK$268K a year, fixed, for life (Figure 10). Because the payment does not rise with inflation it loses about 45% of its real value over 24 years, which is why the portfolio still matters.

Drawdown: rules agreed in advance

Two to three years of spending sits in cash and short bonds, refilled in good years, so a market fall never forces a sale. Discretionary spending follows the Guyton–Klinger guardrails, triggered by markets rather than by the planned rise in premiums: no inflation increase after a year of negative returns; a 10% cut if the withdrawal rate rises more than 20% above its starting level; a 10% raise if it falls 20% below. What the guardrails cost, and what they buy

The medical-tier review does most of the work: with it, the typical family spends its full HK$780K every year. The guardrails cost the typical family about {{guard_cost}} a year, roughly one overseas trip, taken from discretionary spending; essentials are never cut. What they buy is the worst case: without them, one market path in twenty ends on guaranteed income alone, about {{fixswitch_worst5}} a year by Carmen's 90s; with them, spending never falls below about {{guardswitch_worst5}}. Any shortfall shows up years ahead in the annual review, with pre-agreed next steps: the tier review, then selling the home.
