## 8. RISK MANAGEMENT AND PROTECTION

**The largest gap is income, not death: neither parent could replace their salary if disabled.** We ranked
fifteen ways the plan could fail by severity, likelihood and warning time (a failure-mode analysis, Appendix A2).
The two highest-ranked risks are disability for Carmen and for Adrian, exactly the gap the family itself reported.
Life cover ranks lower because the existing whole-life policies already meet most of the need.

**PROTECTION — GAP, AFFORDABLE TO CLOSE.** Figure 13 sizes each gap by need, not by a multiple of income. Closing
all of them costs **≈HK$135K a year** at the start, 19% of the family's HK$718K surplus, paid only until
retirement. Every figure is a planning estimate from published rate cards; quotes replace them before purchase.

**A) Disability first.** A benefit of 65% of gross pay to each parent's retirement age replaces the income the plan
depends on. Carmen's cover must be underwritten on her salary *before* any salary-to-dividend restructuring (§2),
because disability cover is based on earned income.

**B) Life cover fills a defined gap.** If Adrian dies, Carmen's own income leaves a shortfall of ≈HK$157K a year
until 62; with the mortgage and final costs that is a HK$1.75M gap. If Carmen dies, Adrian's income covers the
household; her gap is the mortgage, the business-loan guarantee and final costs. Renewable term cover is cheap
because it ends when the need does.

**C) Medical cover is bought now, not at retirement.** Group cover ends at 65 and 62. Buying individual VHIS cover
today locks in insurability; VHIS plans guarantee renewal to 100. A deductible matched to the group benefit keeps
the overlap cheap, and premiums are tax-deductible up to HK$8,000 per person (§3 shows why this matters most).

**D) The business survives Carmen.** A two-way buy-sell agreement between the co-founders, funded by key-person
cover owned by the company, lets the family realise the full HK$2.5M stake instead of a forced-sale value.

**E) Ryan starts small.** Term life HK$0.5M and CI HK$0.3M cost ≈HK$900 a year, paid from his own income.

**The cheapest fixes are the ones still undone.** Mirror wills, a guardian for Chloe and enduring powers of
attorney for both parents cost under HK$25,000 once, and remove three of the ten highest-ranked risks (§9).
A joint account holding twelve months' spending (≈HK$1M, moved from idle savings) keeps the household running
while an estate is settled.
