PETE: Good morning, we're Team Axis. I'm Pete. What makes a family's plan sustainable? Our client family asks for it as a share: a quarter of their savings in ESG funds. But a share only says what the funds hold. A plan has to answer three questions. Does it last when climate hits? What does the money actually change? And what can their values afford? Take our client, the Wongs: a home, a packaging business, ten and a half million saved. Nearly half of what a climate shock would cost them lands on the home and the business, where no fund reaches. Every carbon cut we can measure is in the business. And their limit leaves room for about one and a half million of giving. So our answer: a plan is sustainable if it holds its limits when climate hits, claims only what it changes, and pays for values only from the room above its limits. For the Wongs that changes six decisions, with HK$0.48M of the family's money and HK$1.91M of the business's, and cuts about 134 tonnes a year. How do you test a plan for that?
