- Only two ring-fenced pots: the joint emergency fund (≈ HK$1M) and Chloe's education fund.

### Decisions the judges will ask about (write one line each in §5)

| Question | Our answer |
|---|---|
| **Adrian needs only ~5%. Why not 100% Treasuries at 5%?** | (1) After stress inflation of 3.5%, 5% leaves ≈ 1.5% real, below the ≈ 2% the family needs. (2) Bonds bought today mature by 2036–41; the money must last to 2066–72, and reinvestment rates then are unknown (long-run cash assumption 2.5%, stress 0.5%). (3) Medical costs grow 6–10% a year. (4) One issuer. **So: floor with Treasuries, upside with equity.** The ladder funds his first five retirement years at today's ≈ 5%; the 40% equity is for 2046 onward. |
| **Why a ladder of individual Treasuries, when bond ETFs are our default?** | A ladder needs fixed maturity dates, and bond ETFs never mature. This is the one exception, like the education deposits. Buy in 3–4 tranches over 6 months, not all at once. |
| **Why hold HK equity and not only the S&P 500 / Nasdaq?** | The global core is already ≈ 60–65% US, and the Nasdaq is the growth slice. HK equity stays small (5%) because the family is already HK-heavy (home, business, three jobs); it is kept for HKD dividends, no dividend withholding tax, and the lowest fee (0.06%). Swapping it for an S&P 500 UCITS fund is also defensible. |
| **Can a Low–Medium investor hold growth stocks?** | Yes, through a fund and limited to 3–8%. A 30% fall on HK$150K costs ≈ HK$45K. The profile limits how much he can lose, not what he can own. |
| **Why bond ETFs?** | Hundreds to thousands of bonds per fund at 0.07–0.30% a year, rolled automatically. |
| **Why Irish-domiciled UCITS, not US ETFs?** | Non-US holders of US-domiciled funds can face US estate tax above US$60,000. Irish funds also lose less to dividend withholding. |
| **Why rules, not trading on views?** | Pre-set thresholds stop panic selling. Quarterly, rule-based changes also stay clearly on the "investment" side of the IRD's badges-of-trade test, so gains stay untaxed. |

## 2. §6 ESG framework

### The target, in numbers
- Case goal: at least **20–25% of investable assets** in ESG. Base used: the case's HK$10.47M → **HK$2.09–2.62M**.
- Today: ESG equity funds 0.70M + green bonds 0.45M = **HK$1.15M (11%)**.
- With the mixes above (assuming Adrian HK$3.0M, Carmen HK$2.075M; to be updated with the Lead's bucket sizes):

| Source | HK$ |
|---|---|
| Adrian: green bonds 5% + ESG-screened global core 25% + ESG equity 5% | 1.05M |
| Carmen: green bonds 10% + ESG equity 35% | 0.93M |
| Carmen's MPF: 50% in her scheme's ESG constituent fund | 0.31M |
| **Total** | **≈ HK$2.29M ≈ 22%** ✓ |

- Key design choice: making Adrian's global core an **ESG-screened index fund** is what closes the gap. It costs about
  the same as a plain index fund and changes his risk very little.

### Four layers of ESG

| Layer | Method | Where it sits |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Exclusions | Screen out thermal coal, controversial weapons, tobacco | All ESG-screened index funds |
| 2. Best-in-class | Top ESG-rated companies in each sector | ESG index funds (Adrian, Carmen) |
| 3. Thematic | Circular economy, clean tech; mirrors Carmen's sustainable-packaging business | Optional within Carmen's ESG sleeve |
| 4. Impact | Green bonds with checked use of proceeds | Green bond fund (both parents) |

### Fig. D: ESG fund scorecard (to build; idea taken from the champion report)
Gate first: the fund must be **SFC-authorised** and on the SFC's ESG fund list. Then score 3–5 candidates:

| Criterion | Weight | How scored |
|---|---|---|
| MSCI ESG rating | 25% | AAA = 5 … BBB = 1; **below A fails** |
| Sustainalytics ESG risk | 25% | < 10 = 5 … 20 = 1; **20 or above fails** |
| Holdings check (greenwashing test) | 20% | Top-10 holdings, fossil-fuel revenue exposure, HK Taxonomy alignment |
| Total cost (TER + spread) | 20% | Lower is better |
| Size, liquidity, tracking | 10% | AUM, trading volume, tracking difference |

- **Two-rating rule:** a fund enters only if it passes **both** MSCI (≥ A) and Sustainalytics (< 20). A downgrade
  below either triggers replacement within 3 months (rule M10).
- **Why two ratings:** ESG ratings from different agencies correlate only about **0.54**, against about 0.92 for credit
  ratings (Berg, Kölbel & Rigobon, *Review of Finance*, 2022).
- **Fig. E:** a small table of the same 3–5 funds showing where MSCI and Sustainalytics disagree. It shows the rule
  working, not just stated.

### Benefits and limitations (the case asks for both)

| Benefits | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Aligns the portfolio with the values that built the family's wealth | **No return premium assumed**; ESG funds modelled at ordinary asset-class returns |
| Regulatory tailwind: SFC ESG-fund disclosure rules, HKEX climate disclosure | Smaller fund universe in HK; some thematic funds are costly |
| Lower exposure to stranded-asset and reputational risk | Ratings disagree; labels can mislead (greenwashing) |

### Watch-list
- **Government tokenised green bonds:** all issues so far went to institutions only; the HKMA is assessing retail
  suitability (LegCo reply, 4 Feb 2026). Rule M11: assess them for Carmen once retail access opens.

### Rules

| # | If | Then |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Ryan is above his glide path | No new crypto; all new savings to equity; no forced selling |
| D2 | A rally puts him > 10pp above the path | Trim back to the path (locks in gains) |
| D3 | Bitcoin falls ≥ 50% | Do nothing; no "buying the dip" above the path |

| D5 | A platform loses its SFC licence or custody rules change | Move to an HKEX spot ETF within 30 days |

### Custody and succession
- Only SFC-licensed platforms or HKEX-listed spot ETFs. ETF custody is held by regulated custodians.
- Any self-custody needs a documented key-recovery arrangement in Ryan's estate papers: **no key, no inheritance.**
  (Coordinate with the Lead's §9.)
