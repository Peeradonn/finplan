## Slide 7 — Decide: the mandate  *(0:40, ~90 spoken words)*

**Draft title:** "We don't set an ESG target. We set a risk budget — and the share falls out."

**On screen (one idea, shown — not argued):**
- Two columns, two people, two budgets:
  - **Adrian** — prudent, retires 2037 → **tracking-error budget ≤0.5%/yr** → ESG core = **30% of his portfolio** (≈¾ of his equity; he keeps 10% plain IWDA).
  - **Carmen** — values, co-founder → **≤3%/yr** → ESG core = **60% of her portfolio** (all her equity).
- An arrow down to: **Family ESG share = 27.7%** — labelled **"output, not target (case asked 20–25%)."**
- Footer line, small: *Hong Kong does not require advisers to ask about sustainability preferences (HKMA FAQ, 2023:
  "not a mandatory requirement"). We ask — and write the answer into a binding mandate.*
- Tags on screen: budgets **ILLUSTRATIVE**; 27.7% **ESTIMATED**; HKMA FAQ **SOURCED**.

**Spoken (~90 words):**
> "Most teams will tell you they put twenty-five percent in ESG. Twenty-five percent of what — and why that number? We
> don't start from a number. We start from each person's risk budget. Adrian is ten years from retirement; his money can
> drift at most half a percent a year from the market. Carmen can take three. Put the same ESG core inside each budget
> and the share *falls out*: thirty percent for Adrian, sixty for Carmen — three-quarters of his equity, all of hers — twenty-eight percent for the family. We didn't
> aim at twenty-five. Hong Kong doesn't even require us to ask Carmen. We ask, and we make it binding."

**Why this slide (what it must prove):** that suitability, done per person, *produces* the ESG share — the opposite of
typing a target off the case. That is the brief's "mandate" and "how disagreement between decision-makers is resolved"
in one move.

## Slide 8 — Decide: the portfolio  *(0:50, ~115 spoken words → trim toward 105)*

**Draft title:** "Measured, not labelled: 30% less carbon, at the plain fund's fee."

**On screen:**
- A condensed holdings strip (equity core **EDMW**; green bonds Carmen only; Treasuries/aggregate; MPF low-carbon) with
  weights per parent — detail to a backup slide.
- Three numbers, big:
  1. **Fee: 0.20%** — *the same as the plain world fund.* (**SOURCED**, iShares 8 Nov)
  2. **Carbon: 64 vs 91 tCO₂e/US$M sales = −30%, measured** (MSCI data Aug 2026), *and an EU rule forces −7% every
     year.* (**SOURCED**, BlackRock factsheet + Reg 2020/1818)
  3. **Price of values: HK$5.1K/yr in fees (0.05%); up to HK$27K/yr tracking in a bad 5 years — capped and reviewed.**
     (**ESTIMATED/MODEL**)
- One refusal, bottom: *The clean-energy ETF on the SFC's own ESG list — −31% in 2023. A label is not a portfolio.*
  (**SOURCED**)

**Spoken (~115 words, trim to ~105):**
> "Now the portfolio. The ESG fund we recommended last time is on the SFC's list and holds zero dollars — a label you
> can't buy. We replaced it with one you can, at the *same fee as the plain world fund*: twenty basis points. Here's what
> a label won't tell you. Measured carbon intensity: sixty-four, against ninety-one for the plain market — thirty percent
> lower. And it's not a one-off: an EU rule forces that index to cut another seven percent every year or lose its status.
> We measure it; we don't trust the badge. The cost? Five thousand dollars a year in fees — five hundredths of one
> percent. In a bad five years, tracking could cost twenty-seven thousand; we cap it and review it. And we still refuse
> the clean-energy fund on that same list — down thirty-one percent in a single year."

**Why this slide:** the brief's required "portfolio with weights and costs" and "a refusal", answered as **measured and
governed**, not labelled — the topic "Beyond Green Labels" in one slide. The carbon number is now **measured**, which the
brief explicitly rewards over illustrative.

**Honesty guardrails on this slide (say these if asked, don't hide them):**
- The −30% carbon is **footprint (exposure), not impact.** We own lower-carbon firms; we didn't cut anyone's tonnes. The
  tonnes the family *causes* are Carmen's vans (area 2).
- **Why the number can fall for the wrong reason (the measurement trap).** A *lower attributed figure is not proof of a
  real cut.* Portfolio-attributed emissions can drop because valuations, holdings or data coverage changed, even when the
  underlying companies emit exactly the same tonnes — the same way an electricity bill can fall on a tariff change, a
  cooler month or less activity, not efficiency. So we report WACI as **exposure we hold**, dated and caveated; we never
  present a fall in it as tonnes the family abated. *(This is the "Beyond Green Labels" thesis applied to our own number.)*
- Over 19 years the best-in-class screen returned ≈ the market (+0.03/yr); over the **last 5 years** the tighter CTB
  screen lagged ≈1 pt/yr. We show both, cap the gap per person, and review it.

**Governance note this slide hands to the Govern slide (not Win's to present, but his number depends on it):**
Keep **five separate ledgers** and never net one against another: (1) operational inventory (Carmen's vans), (2)
portfolio-attributed emissions (WACI), (3) alignment to a rule (EU CTB −7%/yr), (4) project benefits, (5) charitable
outcomes. Each answers a different question; mixing them double-counts. A tonne "avoided" in a project ledger is not a
tonne removed from the portfolio ledger. This is what lets Win claim the carbon figure *honestly* — it is ledger (2)
only, measured and dated, not a family-wide impact claim.

## C-ideas — innovation boosters (for the 40-mark criterion)

1. **The "two dials → one share" visual (slide 7).** Draw Adrian's 0.5% and Carmen's 3% risk budgets as dials; the ESG %
   drops out of them. No other team derives their ESG weight from a *suitability budget*. This **is** the independent
   thinking.
2. **"We bought a rule, not a badge" (slide 8).** Most teams buy a low-carbon fund. Win buys a *mechanism*: −30% floor,
   −7%/yr, or it loses CTB status — measured yearly on the dashboard. That is Govern made concrete, and it's the literal
   answer to "Beyond Green Labels."
3. **Refuse to claim impact for WACI — out loud.** "A lower footprint is what we *own*, not what we *changed*." Refusing
   the claim every other team will make is a stronger innovation signal than making it.
4. **The "50× the greenium" line.** Market pays ~2bp for a green bond; the green bond *fund* charges ~104bp more than a
   plain one. "We won't pay 50× the greenium for a label" — Carmen keeps green bonds only on proof of impact + no entry
   charge.
5. **US$0 fund as the one-slide proof the framework catches what the label misses** — but only after the phone call.
6. **The measurement-trap line (from the attribution problem).** "A number that falls is not a cut." A WACI drop can come
   from price moves, rebalancing or new data, not real abatement — just as a lower power bill can be tariff, weather or
   activity, not efficiency. We therefore keep five separate ledgers (operational, portfolio, alignment, project,
   charitable) and check for double-counting. Saying this out loud — *refusing* to pass our own measured −30% off as
   impact — is a sharper independent-thinking signal than any green claim a checklist team will make. Portfolio holdings
   do **not** change; only how the number is reported and governed.
