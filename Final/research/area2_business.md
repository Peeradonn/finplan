# Area 2: Financing the transition in Carmen's business, plus the regulatory map (brief area 5)

Research brief for Fahtai. Written 4 Nov 2026 for the Final on 21 Nov 2026.

**Status.** This is research, not slide copy. Every number carries one of three tags:
- **SOURCED**: a public figure.
- **ESTIMATED**: our arithmetic from sourced inputs. The working is shown, and the script is reproducible on request.
- **ILLUSTRATIVE**: our assumption about the Wongs' business. The case gives no operating facts beyond pay, value and the loan.

**Limits on this research.**
- The shared web-search budget ran out partway through. After that, facts were read only from pages already found, so gaps are marked **UNVERIFIED** rather than filled from memory.
- CLP's websites refused every automated fetch (HTTP 403). CLP figures are therefore second-hand or average tariffs.
- I personally re-read these primary sources: the HKMA Grant Scheme guideline, Budget paragraphs 134 and 207, the EV Roadmap's Figure 8, the EMSD FiT page, the LMA SME Practice Note and the DESNZ 2026 workbook.

**Thesis tested:** for the Wongs, the sustainability decisions that move money sit in Carmen's business, not in the 25% ESG portfolio. **Result: confirmed, with a twist.**
- The money in the business is in two places:
  - running costs, mainly the vans, which need no green finance;
  - customer retention, mainly the carbon data that listed customers will ask for.
- The carbon is somewhere else again: about 98% of it is in the packaging she buys and sells.
- The green loan itself is worth almost nothing at this size.

## 1. Bottom line for Fahtai

1. **Scope 3 is about 98% of the footprint, so the vans and lights are the wrong place to look for impact.**
   - Scope 1 (vans) is 28 t and Scope 2 (warehouse power) is 20 t.
   - The total is about 2,760–3,250 t CO2e a year. That is the 48 t of own emissions, plus 1,610–2,100 t for the packaging bought (Category 1), about 1,070 t when it is landfilled in Hong Kong (Category 12), and 27 t of inbound freight.
   - Own emissions are therefore 1.5–1.7% of the total. *ESTIMATED from an ILLUSTRATIVE HK$35M-revenue distributor; DESNZ 2026 and US EPA factors.*

2. **The electric vans pay for themselves without any green label.**
   - The government's own 2025 data show electric van-type light goods vehicles cost **20.6% more before tax**.
   - A diesel van pays 17% First Registration Tax (FRT); the electric one pays none **until 31 March 2028**. That shrinks the gap to about **HK$12,000 a van (3%)**.
   - Fuel falls from about HK$95K to HK$9K a van a year.
   - Net cost is about **−HK$16,000 per tonne**: a saving, not a cost.
   - **The recommendation with a number:** order four electric vans before 31 March 2028.
   - With LED lighting and office air-conditioning, the package is HK$1.91M of capex. It saves **HK$0.37M a year** and cuts **26 t**. That is 54% of Scope 1+2, but under 1% of the total.

3. **The "greenium" is worth roughly nothing at this size.**
   - A 25 bp green discount on a HK$1.5M, 5-year loan saves **HK$12,000 in total**. That equals the cheapest green assessment we found (HKQAA, "as low as HK$12,000").
   - A sustainability-linked ratchet of 5–25 bp on the existing HK$0.9M loan is worth **HK$450–2,250 a year**.
   - The HKMA Grant Scheme needs a loan of **at least HK$100M**, 111 times Carmen's loan.
   - **Advice:** take the cheapest equipment loan, green-labelled only if the label costs nothing.

4. **"Sustainable packaging" is the business's biggest greenwashing risk and its biggest stock risk.**
   - Under Hong Kong law, PLA and PE-lined items count as plastic.
   - Hong Kong has no composting route that accepts packaging.
   - Fibre that goes to landfill makes methane. Cradle to landfill, a tonne of fibre is 2.45 t CO2e and a tonne of plastic 3.18 t. So **a fibre item is lower-carbon only if it weighs less than 1.3 times the plastic item it replaces**. *ESTIMATED; DESNZ factors, which use UK landfill assumptions.*
   - PLA stock worth about **HK$0.77M** (ILLUSTRATIVE, two months' stock) is exposed when Phase 2 of the plastics ban arrives. Its date is still unannounced.

5. **The value lever is customer pull, not the loan.**
   - From financial years starting 1 Jan 2026, HKEX LargeCap issuers must report Scope 3. Their first reports appear in 2027.
   - HKEX tells issuers to engage suppliers and request their data.
   - Each 1% of sales lost to a rival with better data costs Carmen about **HK$209K of stake value** (5.1× EBITDA, her 50%). *ESTIMATED from ILLUSTRATIVE inputs.*
   - A data programme costing about HK$50K a year pays for itself if it keeps **0.6% of sales**.

6. **Say plainly what doesn't apply.**
   - **CBAM:** it does not touch this business. Paper and plastic are outside its scope, and the duty falls on EU importers above 50 t a year.
   - **"Transition finance":** the label doesn't fit. Under the HK Taxonomy Phase 2A, electric vans and LED lighting count as "Green", and "Transition" is listed as not applicable to them.
   - **Rooftop solar on a leased roof: refuse.**
     - The 20-year NPV is −HK$0.12M to −HK$0.86M.
     - The Feed-in Tariff ends in 2033.
     - Under the Feed-in Tariff the power company sells the renewable-energy certificates, so Carmen could claim **0 t** of Scope 2 reduction (our reading of EMSD's FiT terms).

7. **What changes for the family.**
   - **Keep counting HK$3.0M** of the HK$5.0M stake. We would not lift the sale price for green savings, because by 2035 every rival's vans are electric too.
   - The savings add about **HK$155K a year after tax to Carmen's half** if paid out as dividends: about HK$1.7M over 2028–38 (nominal; ESTIMATED from ILLUSTRATIVE inputs).
   - **Don't extend Carmen's personal guarantee** beyond today's HK$0.9M. Secure the van loan on the vans, and write her release into the 2035–39 sale.

## 5. Likely judge questions, with short answers

1. **"Your Scope 3 figures rest on UK and US factors. Why trust them?"** We don't trust the point estimate. We show two methods, mass-based (DESNZ 2026) and spend-based (US EPA v1.3), giving 1,610–2,100 t. Both say the same thing: purchased goods are about 58–65% of the footprint, landfill of what she sells another third, and her own operations under 2%. Step one of the plan replaces both with supplier-specific factors.
2. **"Why not refinance the HK$0.9M as a green loan?"** A green loan finances green assets, and that loan funded general working capital. Relabelling it would be greenwashing. The new van loan can be green, but its label is worth about HK$12K over 5 years, the same as the assessment fee.
3. **"What ratchet would you negotiate on an SLL?"** No Hong Kong bank publishes one, and the LMA principles give no size. Even at 25 bp it is HK$2,250 a year on her loan. We would revisit an SLL if her debt passes about HK$5M, with a Scope 3 KPI such as the share of sales carrying supplier-verified footprints, not an energy KPI.
4. **"Electric vans carry less, and charging in an industrial building is hard."** Both are true; the EV Roadmap itself says battery weight cuts payload. Packaging is bulky and light, so her vans fill by volume before weight (our judgement). Depot charging needs the landlord, which is why the green lease comes first. Even on public chargers at three times the tariff, the saving is about HK$68K a van a year.
5. **"Is 'sustainable packaging' greenwashing?"** It can be. PLA is plastic under Hong Kong law, has no composting route here, and in landfill fibre makes methane. Our test: a fibre item must weigh under 1.3× the plastic item it replaces. And no "compostable" claims without an end-of-life route.
6. **"Does CBAM matter?"** No. Paper and plastic are outside it, and it charges EU importers above 50 t a year. Saying so is part of the advice.
7. **"How does this change the Wongs' plan?"** Three ways. It protects the sale value through customer retention (about HK$209K per 1% of sales). It adds up to about HK$155K a year of after-tax cash to Carmen before the sale, which we don't count. And it caps her personal guarantee. The HK$3.0M we count stays unchanged.
8. **"Where is the Grant Scheme?"** The HKMA guideline effective 24 April 2026 sets a minimum of HK$100M for loans under Track II, so an SME like hers can't use it. The subsidy that does reach her is the FRT waiver on electric vans: 17% of an electric van's taxable value, about **HK$71K a van** at the government-average price, until 31 March 2028.
9. **"Isn't your business made up?"** The structure is illustrative by design, as the brief allows. Every assumption is listed, and the ratios hold at any scale: own emissions under 2%, the van saving, the greenium at about zero.
10. **"Why would a buyer pay more?"** We don't claim they would; the evidence is thin. We claim a buyer and the buyer's bank will ask (HKEX, HKMA GS-1). Eight years of verified data and an electric fleet remove a reason to discount.
11. **"Transition finance is in the brief. Why not use it?"** Under the HK Taxonomy Phase 2A her projects are "Green"; "Transition" is listed as not applicable. Her transition is in her suppliers' factories, and she influences it through purchasing contracts, not a loan.
