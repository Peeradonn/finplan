## The story in one line
The Wongs look secure, but their wealth is in the wrong shape: on their investments alone the money lasts to
Carmen's 89 in only {{ladder_1_89}} of 10,000 simulated markets. Five calls fix it without taking more risk, it survives every test we ran, it
starts this month, and it still leaves a legacy. Then: what this means for planners like us.

## Draft script
Plain spoken English; numbers are the model's placeholders. Each speaker may reword, keeping the numbers and the length.
Reasons the talk leaves out go into that slide's speaker notes (listed after the script).

**1 · Cover** (0:20)
PETE: Hello, we're Team Axis, and this is Built to Last, our plan for the Wong family. I'm Pete, the lead adviser.
FAHTAI: I'm Fahtai: retirement. WIN: I'm Win: investments. LOOKBUA: And I'm Lookbua: protection and risk.

**2 · On paper, secure** (0:30)
PETE: Meet the Wongs. Adrian, 54, wants his capital, his retirement and his health protected. Carmen, 49, runs her own
business and wants their money to reflect her values. Ryan, 24, has just started work and holds crypto. Chloe, 16,
starts university in 2028. On paper, they're secure: HK$24.4 million, 41% of income saved, little debt.

**3 · Yet it runs out** (0:35)
PETE: But will it last? We played out their retirement in 10,000 simulated markets. On their investments alone, the
money lasts to Carmen's 89 in only {{ladder_1_89}} of them. Here are ten typical futures: in {{fut_short_n_word}}, it
runs out when she's {{fut_age_first}} to {{fut_age_last}}. Two reasons. 61% of their wealth is the home and the
business, which pay nothing once they stop work. And medical premiums outside their budget climb to HK$375K a year.

**4 · Our answer** (0:40)
PETE: They asked for a plan that's innovative yet prudent. Our answer is five calls. Insure the income, not the life.
Put the home and the business to work. Fix the cost the budget ignores. Give each person a plan of their own. And make
room for their values and Ryan's crypto, with guardrails. Together they lift the odds from {{ladder_1_89}} to
{{switch_89}}, and to almost every future with spending rules. Lookbua.

**5 · Call 1: insure the income** (0:45)
LOOKBUA: Thanks, Pete. Call one: insure the income, not the life. Neither parent has disability cover: if one couldn't
work, nothing would replace their pay. So disability cover comes first, 65% of pay until they retire; then critical
illness, medical cover of their own, and a term-life top-up. About HK$100K a year, 14% of their surplus. Term, because
the need ends at retirement. And in month one: wills, a guardian for Chloe, and powers of attorney. Fahtai.

**6 · Call 2: put the home and the business to work** (0:55)
FAHTAI: Thanks, Lookbua. Call two: don't take more risk. Doubling their shares only lifts the odds to
{{alone_eq80_89}}, and deepens the worst year to {{alone_eq80_worst}}. Instead, we put what they own to work. The home
pays {{rmp_income}} a year for life through a reverse mortgage, or they downsize: they choose at 65. The business is
sold in stages to her co-founder and managers; we count only HK$3 million of its five. A survivor plans on 70% of the
budget. And the MPF buys annuities: with the home's income, more than their essential bills by 2039. Same investments:
{{ladder_5_89}}.

**7 · Call 3: fix the cost the budget ignores** (0:45)
FAHTAI: Call three: fix the cost the budget ignores, those medical premiums. At Adrian's 75 they review the tier: the
Standard plan costs about a third as much, and the odds rise to {{switch_89}}. From 2039, simple spending rules: no
raise after a bad year, a small trim only if withdrawals run well ahead, and essentials never cut. Remember the ten
futures? Now all ten reach Carmen's 89. Win.

**8 · Call 4: a plan for each person** (0:45)
WIN: Thanks, Fahtai. Call four: one family policy, a plan for each person. Today HK$2.5 million sits in cash, thirty
months of spending, and most of their shares are in funds charging 1.5% a year. We give every dollar a job: a
twelve-month reserve; HK$2.57 million for Chloe's degree, funded today wherever she goes; and a portfolio each, Adrian
40% in shares, Carmen 60%. Ryan saves 30% of his pay in a plan of his own. Index funds alone save HK$45K a year.

**9 · Call 5: values and innovation, with guardrails** (0:40)
WIN: Call five: values and innovation, with guardrails. Carmen asked for 20 to 25% in ESG. We reach 25%, up from 11%,
in SFC-listed funds that must pass two ratings. Crypto isn't yes or no: it's size and form for each person. Adrian
holds none; Carmen up to 2%, through an ETF; Ryan keeps his HK$500K but adds nothing new, so its share falls from 74% of
his wealth to 20% by 35. Lookbua.

**10 · Will it hold?** (0:35)
LOOKBUA: Then we tried to break the plan. The hardest test: Adrian loses his job at 60, with no severance. The four
decisions alone last in {{adrian60_89}} of futures; the full plan, {{rec_adrian60_89}}. Years of low returns, or high
inflation with dearer medical care: {{rec_stressB_89}} or better. And if Carmen lives to 95: {{guardswitch_95}}. Pete.

**11 · What happens when** (0:30)
PETE: It starts this month: wills and a joint account in month one, the cover within three months, a portfolio each
within six, a first family meeting at a year. After that, dates trigger each step: Chloe's offer in 2028, the business
from 2035, the home and the annuities from 2037, the medical review in 2047.

**12 · What passes on** (0:25)
PETE: And the legacy the case is named for. After paying for their whole lives, {{legacy_rmp}} or more still passes to
Ryan and Chloe, in stages at 25, 30 and 35, with a scholarship in sustainable design that joins Carmen's mission and
Chloe's interest.

**13 · Wealth Management Connect** (0:40)
PETE: What does this mean for planners like us? Since 2021, Wealth Management Connect has let residents of Hong Kong,
Macao and nine mainland cities invest across the boundary, now up to three million renminbi each. Southbound, a Hong
Kong bank won't solicit or recommend across the boundary unless the investor asks, so an adviser's value is a clear,
suitable answer when asked. We kept Northbound out of the Wongs' plan: their home, income and business are all in Hong
Kong. If Adrian's work moves across the boundary, it becomes the right tool.

**14 · Who we want to become** (1:00)
PETE: In five years I want to be an investment specialist in a Hong Kong bank's wealth team, matching each Connect
client's portfolio to their risk, with the currency risk made clear. Fitting the Wongs' three risk profiles into one plan
was that job in miniature. FAHTAI: I want to build the risk models that wealth
managers across Asia use for families. This plan taught me that a model's job is to price the trade-offs between
people. Next is the FRM, with Connect funds as my first case. WIN: I want to be an investment strategist for family
clients across Asia-Pacific, fitting each portfolio to its owner on either side of the boundary. LOOKBUA: I plan
further study in systemic risk. The Connect links two markets with different rules, so a shock in one can reach
families in the other. Stress-testing this plan taught me that every risk needs a named defence.
*(Win's line is a draft for Win to confirm; Lookbua's needs his OK.)*

**15 · Close** (0:15)
PETE: Protected first, income for life, and a place for every voice. From {{ladder_1_89}} to {{switch_89}}: built to
last. We are Team Axis. ALL: Thank you.
