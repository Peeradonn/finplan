**The method for any business, in plain words** (say it without the Wongs if asked "how would this work for another client?"):

- **Impact (slide 7).**
  1. *Investigate:* meters, fuel bills, an energy audit; then every source (own fuel: Scope 1; bought power:
     Scope 2; materials, suppliers, disposal: Scope 3). The scopes are a completeness check, not three equal
     workstreams. Go deep where emissions are big, where the client has influence and where rules bite.
  2. *Find alternatives* for the same need, with their trade-offs.
  3. *Measure each* three ways: capital up front; the effect in its own unit (t CO2e, kg of waste, m³ of water,
     never added); and cost per unit cut = net lifetime cost ÷ lifetime reduction. **Minus means it saves money.** A
     saving doesn't prove the cash is there.
- **Funding (slide 8):** (1) Cash flow or supplier terms enough? (2) If not, company cash or borrowing (buffer left,
  cost, repayments)? (3) Then screen the instrument (purpose, term, eligibility, fees). No route: shrink or wait.
- **The label (slide 9):** (1) Compare like for like (same amount, term, security). (2) Net benefit: interest saved
  less the label's costs. (3) Check the obligations. Pay extra only for a stated, agreed benefit.

In PRISM, Impact checks the second test (*claims only what it changes*); Select prices each option and keeps those
that pass all three.

## 4. The detail underneath

**4.1 The business is ours, by design** ("define the client's circumstances and every figure yourself", brief p.2):
a HK$35M-revenue distributor, 14 staff, 4 diesel vans, a leased Kwai Chung warehouse, consistent with Carmen's
HK$720K draw, HK$5.0M stake (50%) and HK$0.9M loan (AB §3a). HK has ≈12 import/export and wholesale SMEs per
manufacturing one (84,395 vs 7,208; TID, June 2025): a distributor is representative. At 100% ownership every HK$ and
tonne doubles; every ratio and verdict stays.

**4.2 Why Scope 3 dominates.** Each tonne of packaging she sells carries 1.2–1.6 t of factory emissions; her vans add
≈0.02 t per tonne delivered (AB §3b). So own operations are 1.5–1.7% of ≈2,760–3,250 t: Scope 3 is **about 98%, on
our assumptions**. The factors are UK (DESNZ) and US (EPA); two methods agree on the shape, not the point estimate;
step one replaces both with supplier data. *Inconsistency:* the brief used the DESNZ **2026** workbook, but backup
29's footer says "2025": say "the UK government's DESNZ factors", no year. **Fibre trap:** factory to HK landfill,
fibre is 2.45 t CO2e a tonne and plastic 3.18 t, so fibre is lower-carbon only if it weighs under **1.3×** the plastic
item (ESTIMATED; UK landfill assumptions).

**4.3 Recycled PET: 108 of the 134 t.**
- **Parity is a window, not a fact:** one dated assessment, hence "quote first". At the 2025 Asian peak (US$485/t
  premium; S&P *Chemical Trends H1 2026*, p.27) it costs ≈HK$248K a year, ≈+HK$2,300 a tonne, ≈2.3 HK cents a cup (DL B4).
- **Resin isn't packaging:** conversion, food-contact compliance and chain-of-custody certification are probably now
  the real green premium, and none is priced yet (SC §4a).
- **If a premium returns, customers pay** it as an itemised line per tonne: a sales decision. **Not the family's
  values budget** (SC §4b).
- **Plastics-ban catch:** recycled PET cups are still plastic, so Phase 2 would ban them for takeaway too. Aim
  recycled content at pre-packaged and retail packaging, fibre at tableware.
- **Slide 12's trigger:** recycled plastic quoted above new → its 108 t are priced again.

**4.4 The vans.**
- **The comparison:** replacing her diesels with *new diesel vans*, stated; owning the project doesn't by itself make
  the cut additional. Tonnes are estimated now, measured from fuel and kWh once running.
- **Diesel HK$31/L** is the government's after-discount average (Aug 2026; LCQ11 Annex I, 7 Oct 2026). Fleets may pay
  less: at HK$25/L the vans still save (−HK$9,700/t; +HK$0.41M to the children, M2).
- **The tax waiver** runs "until end-March 2028" (Budget 2026–27, para 207), worth ≈HK$71K a van: order first.
- **Payload and charging:** the EV Roadmap says batteries cut payload; packaging fills vans by volume first (our
  judgement). Depot chargers need the landlord, hence the green lease. On public chargers at three times the tariff
  the brief's check (pump diesel price) still saves ≈HK$68K a van a year.

**4.5 LED, air-con, lighter board.** LED pays back in ≈3.8 years, air-con ≈5.3 (AB §3c; costs UNVERIFIED). The lighter
board is the cheapest cut on paper (−HK$5,800/t, no capital) but stays out of the 134 t until tested.

**4.6 Cost per tonne.** Net lifetime cost (purchase, install, running, verification, disposal, less savings),
annualised at 6%, ÷ tonnes a year. Negative = saves money. It ranks options; it is not a budget. Yardstick: the EU
carbon price (CBAM certificates €75–82/t in 2026, ≈HK$700).

**4.7 The loan.**
- **Package:** HK$1.91M = HK$1.5M secured loan + HK$0.41M company cash (under a third of a year's retained profit,
  ≈HK$1.43M, ILLUSTRATIVE).
- **Cover:** at HK$31/L the saving no longer covers the payment; the company tops up ≈HK$46K a year to 2032. Debt
  cover **5.7×** before → **≈3×** after (3.3× at the pump price; DL B1). Bank thresholds UNVERIFIED.
- **Collateral:** the vans. Carmen's guarantee stays at the existing HK$0.9M, her release written into the sale (AB
  §3d). Fallback if the bank insists: the HKMC 80% SME guarantee (fee up to 0.45% a year; owners above 50% guarantee
  personally; she holds exactly 50%: check).
- **Co-founder:** the company is 50/50; vans, loan and any dividend need the co-founder.
- **A CPA could catch this:** HK$1.5M at **5.5%** over 5 years is ≈**HK$351K** a year. The slides' ≈HK$349K is the
  payment at the **green rate, 5.25%** (AB §3d), yet slide 9 proposes the plain loan. At 5.5%: savings cover ≈86%,
  top-up ≈HK$48K a year (our arithmetic from AB §3d's payments). No decision changes; say so plainly.

**4.8 Greenium vs green premium.** *Greenium:* the label's finance discount, ≈HK$12K over 5 years. *Green premium:*
the greener thing's extra cost, ≈HK$12K a van today, ≈HK$83K after the waiver. One line: **"The bank's green price
doesn't move this decision. The van's price, the tax waiver and the customer do."**

**4.9 The instruments we screened out**, with Fahtai's 16 Nov wording:
- **Sustainability-linked loan: not proposed, on assumed costs.** A 5–25 bp ratchet on her HK$0.9M loan is
  HK$450–2,250 a year; yearly verification (≈HK$12K, UNVERIFIED: HKQAA's assessment fee as a proxy) pays only above
  ≈HK$4.8M of debt at 25 bp (AB §3d). Exception: HSBC's loan with a free HKGOC certificate, but that KPI covers ≈2% of
  her emissions. **Not a universal SME exclusion.**
- **Supply-chain finance: access unconfirmed.** She buys ≈US$3.4M a year (ILLUSTRATIVE); one US bank's guide anchors
  at ≈US$100M; **no HK bank states a minimum**; we found no HK food, retail or packaging buyer running one. If one
  appears, its ESG part is worth ≈HK$300–1,500 a year; join only if payment terms don't lengthen (SC §3b).
- **HKMA Grant Scheme: ineligible at this size.** Loans must be "at least HK$100 million" (guideline effective 24 Apr
  2026, p.3); it pays review costs, not interest. What reaches her: the e-van tax waiver.
- **Transition finance: no separate instrument.** HK Taxonomy Phase 2A (22 Jan 2026) counts zero-tailpipe vans and LED
  as "Green"; "Transition" doesn't apply. Her transition is her stock, through supplier contracts.

**4.10 Green lease (slide 8's box).** At the 2027 renewal: reinstatement waiver for chargers and LED, shared energy
data, cost recovery capped at measured savings, a renewal option to 2039 (DL B6). It fixes the *split incentive* (she
pays the bills, the landlord owns the walls). Do it, but don't call it green: the world effect is small.

**4.11 Carbon data and her customers.** HKEX App. C2 makes Scope 3 mandatory for LargeCap issuers from financial
years starting 1 Jan 2026 (first reports 2027); her packaging is in their Scope 3, and HKEX tells them to ask
suppliers. Budget ≈HK$54–73K a year from 2027 (DL B3). **−HK$1.55M is a sensitivity:** what the children lose if a
customer worth 10% of sales leaves (M3). **How much the data cuts that risk is not quantified.** It pays for itself if
it keeps 0.6% of sales (≈HK$209K of Carmen's stake per 1%; AB §3e). No study shows an SME sale premium for data.

**4.12 "Sustainable packaging" and PLA.** The EPD's guidance counts PLA as a regulated "biodegradable plastic"
(and PLA linings as regulated linings). Phase 1 (since 22 Apr 2024) banned PLA cutlery, straws, stirrers and plates;
cups and containers only for dine-in; Phase 2 has no date. The EPD's packaging guide (Sep 2025) calls compostables
"still premature"; food-waste collection rejects loads over 20% packaging and utensils. So **"compostable" claims
stop** and tableware stock stays short (≈HK$0.77M of PLA stock exposed, ILLUSTRATIVE; basis to check). The Trade
Descriptions Ordinance (up to HK$500K and 5 years) could catch a false green claim to a business: our inference, no
case found. Cap. 603's text wasn't read; the point rests on EPD guidance and the LegCo Brief.

**4.13 B8, the dividends.** Carmen's half of the van saving paid out from 2033, once the loan is repaid: +HK$1.09M for
the children (`fin_dot_div_n`). It moves money out of a concentrated business, needs the co-founder and rests on
illustrative numbers, so it is **outside the headline** (backups 20, 31 only).

**4.14 Where the business reaches the family.** We keep counting HK$3.0M of her HK$5.0M stake, with no green uplift:
by 2035 rivals' vans are electric too. **+HK$0.51M** = the vans alone vs new diesel (`fin_dot_vans_n`, slides 9, 13);
**+HK$0.54M** = their step in the headline's +HK$1.05M (`fin_fair_vans`, backup 15); **−HK$0.09M** = the data's cost
to the children (slide 13).

**4.15 The rules map (backup 17).** Most rules bind banks and companies; they reach the Wongs through her customers
and her lender. HKEX App. C2: customers ask for her data. HKMA GS-1/GS-2: her lender asks at each credit review (GS-2
issued 22 Sep 2026; banks' transition-plan documentation by 1 Jul 2030). HK Taxonomy: transition finance doesn't
apply to these assets. Cap. 603: her compostable claims stop. HKFRS S1/S2: indirect. EU CBAM: doesn't reach her
(paper and plastic are outside it; the duty falls on EU importers).

## 8. Self-test

<details><summary>1. Why does a distributor's change run through what she buys?</summary>
Scope 3 is about 98% of her footprint on our assumptions. Her vans and warehouse are 48 t of ≈2,760–3,250 t a year.
</details>

<details><summary>2. Which cut needs a loan, and why only that one?</summary>
The vans (≈HK$1.78M): too big for company cash. Recycled PET runs on supplier terms (≈HK$0.27M working capital), LED
and air-con on company cash (≈HK$124K), and the lighter board needs nothing.
</details>

<details><summary>3. What does −HK$12,800 a tonne mean?</summary>
The vans save HK$12,800 for every tonne they cut, annualised at 6% over 8 years at HK$31/L diesel. Negative = a saving.
</details>

<details><summary>4. Greenium vs green premium, with the numbers?</summary>
Greenium ≈HK$12K over 5 years (25 bp, assumed), cancelled by ≈HK$12K of review. Green premium ≈HK$12K a van today,
≈HK$83K after 31 Mar 2028.
</details>

<details><summary>5. Why no transition finance?</summary>
HK Taxonomy Phase 2A counts zero-emission vans and LED as "Green"; "Transition" doesn't apply to them.
</details>

<details><summary>6. What does −HK$1.55M mean, and what may you not say about it?</summary>
What the children lose if a customer worth 10% of sales leaves (a sensitivity). Don't say the data protects it: the
data's benefit isn't quantified.
</details>

<details><summary>7. Why is the lighter board not in the 134 t?</summary>
It hasn't been tested: it counts only once tests show the board still protects the goods.
</details>

<details><summary>8. Why order the vans before 31 Mar 2028?</summary>
The first-registration-tax waiver ends; the e-van's premium over diesel jumps from ≈HK$12K to ≈HK$83K a van.
</details>
