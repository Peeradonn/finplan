## Job in the argument

Slide 7 identifies potentially worthwhile changes, with evidence/feasibility conditions.
Slide 8 asks how much cash each needs, when, and which funding route is suitable.
Slide 9 asks whether the proposed van loan should carry a green label.

Title: **Match the funding to what the change needs.**

Opening question: **How much cash, and when?**
Footer/closing line: **For the vans, we propose borrowing. Should that loan be green?**

The page must not imply that a greener project automatically merits debt, that a
positive accounting profit proves spare cash exists, or that every labelled loan
has a minimum size. Funding approval remains conditional on cash-flow and lender checks.

## Recommended form

A compact decision path across the top, two worked funding routes in the centre,
and one short rejection strip above the closing line. Use a different form from
slide 7's stacked arrows: the relationship here branches by cash need and funding fit.

Top path (approximately one-quarter of the body):

1. **Cash flow / supplier terms?** If they cover the timing and amount without
   straining liquidity, use them. Otherwise proceed to the cash/borrowing comparison.
2. **Company cash or borrowing?** Compare reserve left after funding, total cost,
   and repayment capacity. If neither is viable, resize or defer the change.
3. **Which instrument fits?** Check purpose, tenor, eligibility, security and fees.
   Carry a suitable loan proposal to slide 9 for the green/plain price comparison.

Centre (approximately half the body): two horizontal client routes, arranged by
cash timing rather than a catalogue of financial products.

| Change | What cash is needed? | Proposed route / condition |
|---|---|---|
| Recycled packaging | Supplier price, payment cycle and working-capital gap | Negotiate terms; use company cash only if liquidity holds. Existing early-payment illustration ties up about HK$0.27M; supplier acceptance and final product price are unverified. |
| Electric vans and equipment | Upfront purchases; benefits accrue over time | Assess the existing proposal: HK$1.5M equipment loan + HK$0.41M company cash for the HK$1.91M package, subject to repayment and lender checks. |

Keep the main visual to keywords and capital amounts; put the tests and caveats in
notes. Suggested row labels: **Working-capital cycle** and **Upfront equipment**.
The HK$1.91M package includes vans, chargers, LED and air-con; never call it the
price of the vans alone. The loan is not an approved offer.

Screen-out strip (approximately one-sixth of the body): at most two route/reason pairs,
selected from verified evidence before drawing. Candidate pairs:

- **Paid sustainability-linked structure:** verification/KPI costs exceed the
  assumed benefit for this proposal. This is conditional arithmetic, not a universal
  ban on SME sustainability-linked loans; free-verification exceptions exist.
- **Grant Scheme application:** the proposed loan does not meet the relevant
  published eligibility threshold. Re-check the official guideline before displaying
  its threshold. This is a subsidy screen, not itself a borrowing instrument.

Keep the old size chart, detailed thresholds and five-kind taxonomy in backup if
still useful. Do not carry over the blanket claim that supply-chain finance has a
universal minimum size. A customer's programme is conditional on participation and
terms, not automatically unavailable to this business.

## Metric / decision checks

- Cash need: upfront purchase versus recurring cash conversion gap; amount and date.
- Cash route: remaining operating buffer, not retained profit as a cash proxy.
- Debt route: affordability of payments under a downside cash-flow scenario,
  existing obligations, fees, security and consent.
- Tenor: repayment timing appropriate to the cash-generating asset/activity.
- No arbitrary new reserve or debt-cover threshold. Record the business's agreed
  limit, or mark it as missing and keep the recommendation conditional.

## Spoken draft (about 35-40 seconds)

For each shortlisted change, ask how much cash it needs, and when. Can cash flow
or supplier terms cover it without straining liquidity? If not, compare company
cash with borrowing, then screen instruments for purpose, eligibility and cost.
For Carmen's packaging, confirm prices, payment terms and working capital first.
The equipment needs capital upfront: assess one and a half million of borrowing
alongside company cash, subject to repayment checks. Reject routes that fail their
eligibility or cost test. For the vans, we propose borrowing. Should that loan be green?
